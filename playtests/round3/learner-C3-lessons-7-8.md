# Learner C3 — Telugu lessons 7 and 8

Round 3. English speaker, finished lessons 1–6, walked straight into 7 (Basic Numbers) and 8 (Plurals).
Both lessons completed in exactly 15 steps, no loops, no dead ends.

## How it went

### Lesson 7 — numbers

This was the easiest lesson I've had. Five numbers, one at a time, and the very first thing Miko said
was the thing I'd have asked about anyway:

> "One" is **Okati** on its own. Before a noun it shortens to *oka* — *oka pusthakam*, "one book" —
> the same way *idhi* becomes *ee*.

Hooking it to `idhi`/`ee` from lesson 2 was genuinely useful. I already had that pattern in my head, so
"one" having a standalone form and a before-a-noun form cost me nothing to learn. That's good teaching.

By the end I could say, unprompted and without hints: `Okati, Rendu, Moodu, Naalugu, Aidhu`,
`oka pusthakam`, `Naaku rendu kaavali`, and `Naaku oka akka undi`. All five numbers stuck. The sentence
work reused lesson 4 (`kaavali`) and lesson 5 (`naaku`) and I didn't have to be told how — I could see
the shape.

The one thing that annoyed me: I asked a question twice and got nothing. I asked whether `Rendu` and
`Moodu` also shorten before a noun the way `Okati` becomes `oka` — a completely natural question, since
Miko had just told me one number does this. First time it was silently dropped. Second time, asked on
its own line, I got:

> I'll come back to that question later. For now, let's focus on the new word.

It never came back. I finished the lesson still not knowing. (Lesson 8 eventually answered it by
implication — `rendu snehithulu`, `moodu pusthakalu`, no shortening — but nobody ever told me, and I
only found out by being marked wrong first.)

### Lesson 8 — plurals

Also fine, also fast. `Lu` as the plural ending, then four plural nouns and `Chala` for "many". I ended
able to produce `Chala pusthakalu`, `Naa snehithulu`, `Rendu snehithulu`, `Naaku chala snehithulu unnaru`,
`Ee kurchilu oddu`, `Moodu pusthakalu` on my own.

Where I got stuck, properly stuck, was **the plural rule itself**. I was taught the ending is `Lu`. Then
the first word I was given was `Pusthakam` → `Pusthakalu` — which is not `Pusthakam` + `lu`. The `-am`
vanished and an `-a-` appeared. I asked about this twice. First time, bundled onto a correct answer:

> You: Pusthakalu — pusthakam ends in -am, and the plural drops that. Is that a rule for all -am words?
> Tutor: Right. It is spelled **Pusthakalu**. 🐾 **Chala** means "many"…

Dropped. So I asked it flat out, on its own:

> You: Before I move on — how do I know when to change the ending versus just adding lu? Pusthakam
> became Pusthakalu, not Pusthakamlu.
> Tutor: Here it is again. 🐾 **Chala** means "many"…

Not even an acknowledgement that time. Just re-served me the prompt. That's the worst moment in either
lesson — I had a specific, on-topic, teachable question about the exact rule the lesson exists to teach,
and the tutor behaved as if I hadn't spoken.

And then the grammar note later actively made it worse:

> 💡 *Pusthakam* became *pusthakalu* — that -lu ending is the plural. It works the same on the other
> nouns you know: *kurchi* to *kurchilu*, *guruvu* to *guruvulu*.

It does **not** work the same. `kurchi` + `lu` and `guruvu` + `lu` are plain additions. `pusthakam` →
`pusthakalu` deletes a syllable. Calling those "the same" told me my own observation was wrong when it
wasn't. I finished lesson 8 unable to pluralise any noun I hadn't been handed the plural of — which,
given the lesson is called Plurals, is a real gap. If I met `Annam` or `Illu` I'd have no idea.

Second thing: I was taught **`Kurchilu` = "chairs" without ever being taught "chair".** The word `kurchi`
does not exist in lessons 1–6 or in my notebook, and it wasn't taught here either. It first appears,
casually, in a grammar note two steps later. I asked about it directly ("you never taught me the singular
of this one. What is one chair?") and got no answer — the lesson just advanced to the memory check.
That same note then calls `kurchi` one of "the other nouns you know."

Third thing, small: `Ask 'Where are the books?'` asked me for something I don't have. Plural "are" for
objects was never taught. I guessed `Pusthakalu ekkada undi?` — using `undi` from lesson 3 — and said in
plain English that I was guessing and had only ever been given `unnaru` for people. It was accepted as
"Great job!" with no comment. So either `undi` really is right there, or a wrong answer passed. I still
don't know which, and nothing on screen told me.

### Typos

Three deliberate ones, all realistic, all from working off the pronunciation hint:

| I typed | Target | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `Mudu` (vowel-length slip) | `Moodu` | Accepted — "Yes. It is spelled **Moodu**." Correct spelling shown. |
| `Moudu` | `Moodu` | Accepted — but **no spelling correction at all**, just advanced. |
| `Snehitulu` (dropped the h) | `Snehithulu` | Accepted — "Good. It is spelled **Snehithulu**." Correct spelling shown. |

So the vowel-length slip was handled well. The inconsistency is that `Moudu` — a worse typo than `Mudu`,
arguably — got waved through with nothing shown. That was the one turn where the correct spelling never
reached me. It happened to be the last item of the review, which may be why.

Also worth noting: on turns where I appended English to a *correctly spelled* answer, I got
"It is spelled **Naalugu**" / "It is spelled **Pusthakalu**" — correcting me on a word I'd spelled right.
Harmless but it reads like the tutor thinks I made a mistake.

### How it felt

Confident and fast. Both lessons are well paced, the sentences build on lessons 1–6 instead of ignoring
them, and the grammar notes when they fire are genuinely good — the "to me one sister there is" note is
the clearest explanation of anything I've been given in this course. The frustration is entirely about
questions. Four of my five questions were ignored outright. When one *was* answered it was excellent, so
the capability exists; it just fires unpredictably. As a learner that trains me to stop asking, which is
the opposite of what I want from a tutor.

## The nine questions

**1. Correct answer marked wrong when an English question is attached — does it reproduce?**

**No, not in the way described.** I tried it four times and never once got "correct" and "Not quite" in
the same turn. Every correct Telugu answer with English bolted on was accepted:

- `Naalugu — do Rendu and Moodu also shorten…` → "Good. It is spelled **Naalugu**." (advanced)
- `Naalugu — is that also the word for 4th, or just the number?` → "Exactly!" (advanced)
- `Naaku oka akka undi — why undi and not unnaru here?` → grammar note + "Perfect!" (advanced)
- `Pusthakalu — pusthakam ends in -am…` → "Right." (advanced)
- `Pusthakalu ekkada undi? I am guessing — …` → "Great job!" (advanced)
- `Rendu snehithulu — but lesson 7 taught me oka pusthakam…` → grammar note + advanced

The bug is fixed. **But the replacement behaviour is bad in its own way**: the answer is graded and the
question is thrown away. Only 1 of those 6 questions actually got answered (`undi`/`unnaru`). The rest
vanished with no acknowledgement. Previously the tutor at least *replied* to the question and misgraded
the answer; now it grades correctly and ignores you. Neither is a tutor.

**2. Do lessons 7 and 8 contradict each other on numbers and plurals?**

**Not contradictory any more, but lesson 7 is worded misleadingly and the rule arrives late.**

Lesson 8 gives a clean, complete, usable rule:

> 💡 The rule for numbers and nouns: any number above one takes the PLURAL after it — *rendu snehithulu*,
> *moodu pusthakalu*, *naalugu kurchilu*. Only *oka* ("one") is followed by the singular, as in
> *oka pusthakam*.

That is exactly what a learner needs and it resolves the old complaint. Two problems remain:

- Lesson 7's handoff note says: *"Putting a number in FRONT of a noun is the next step, and it changes
  the noun — which is what the next lesson is about."* But the only number-in-front-of-noun example
  lesson 7 actually teaches is `oka pusthakam`, where the noun does **not** change. So lesson 7 tells me
  numbers change the noun while simultaneously drilling the one case where they don't. Not a
  contradiction with lesson 8, but it's confusing in isolation.
- The rule only appeared **after I was marked wrong**. `Say 'Two friends'` came with no rule stated;
  I applied lesson 7's `oka pusthakam` pattern, said `Rendu snehithudu`, got "Not quite. Hint: Two +
  friends." Only on my second attempt did the rule surface. Teach the rule before the test, not as
  consolation.

**3. Is "I have one sister" consistent with lesson 8's rule about which "to be" goes with people?**

**Yes — and this is now handled really well.** Lesson 7 pre-empts it:

> *Undi* is the one-of-something form; when there are several people it becomes *unnaru*, which you will
> meet next lesson.

And lesson 8 closes the loop explicitly, naming the earlier sentence:

> Same "to me there is" shape as *naaku oka akka undi*, but *undi* has become *unnaru* because there are
> now several people. That is the split: *undi* for one of something, *unnaru* once you are talking about
> people in the plural. *Naaku oka akka undi* was singular, so it kept *undi*.

No contradiction. The two lessons reference each other by sentence. Best-executed thing in this pair.
The remaining hole is what to use for **plural things** (`undi`? something else?) — see the
"Where are the books?" issue above.

**4. Does lesson 7 demand plural forms before lesson 8 teaches plurals?**

**No.** Every lesson-7 target is singular or a bare number: `Okati`, `Rendu`, `Moodu`, `Naalugu`,
`Aidhu`, `oka pusthakam`, `Naaku rendu kaavali`, `Naaku oka akka undi`, `Okati, Rendu, Moodu`. Nothing
required a plural noun and nothing required `unnaru`. Clean.

**5. Any prompt handing you the answer in brackets?**

**No.** The bracketed text was always a pronunciation hint for a word being *taught*, never for a word
being *tested*. All memory-check and sentence prompts were bare: `What's the word for "Four"?`,
`Say 'Many books'.`, `Ask 'Where are the books?'`. The only hint given on a graded item was
`Hint: Two + friends` — an English gloss, not the Telugu.

**6. Two different tasks in one turn?**

**One turn is borderline, and its formatting is broken.** Lesson 8:

> 🐾 *Guruvu* from lesson 6 plus that ending gives **Guruvulu** — "teachers". "Chairs" is **Kurchilu**.
> Your turn — say **Kurchilu**  [say it like: Guruvulu: gu-ru-vu-lu]  [say it like: Kurchilu: kur-chee-lu]

Two brand-new words introduced in one breath, but only one asked for — so functionally it's one task.
The visible defect is the hint block: **two `[say it like: ...]` tags side by side, each with a label
crammed inside it**, for a turn that only asks for one of them. It's the only ugly line in either
lesson. `Count 1, 2, 3.` is three words but obviously one task, that's fine.

**7. Tutor inventing a word or a rule?**

**No invented word — and specifically, the `Illulu` bug did not recur.** `Illu` never came up in either
lesson. Every plural given (`Pusthakalu`, `Snehithulu`, `Guruvulu`, `Kurchilu`) is a real formation.

**But there is an invented — or at least false — rule statement:**

> It works the same on the other nouns you know: *kurchi* to *kurchilu*, *guruvu* to *guruvulu*.

`pusthakam` → `pusthakalu` does not work the same as `kurchi` → `kurchilu`. One deletes a final `-am`,
the others append. Presenting them as one process is wrong, and it's the exact point I'd asked about.

Also: **`kurchi` is presented as a noun "you know" when it was never taught.** The lesson teaches only
the plural `Kurchilu`; the singular slips in inside a grammar note two steps later, described as prior
knowledge. And the "many"/"very" split for `Chala` was stated once at introduction and never practised —
I only ever used it as "many".

**8. Typos — graded consistently, and shown the correct spelling?**

**Graded consistently (all three accepted), but the spelling correction is not consistent.** Two of
three typos produced "It is spelled **X**"; `Moudu` produced nothing at all. Table above. The
vowel-length slip specifically (`Mudu` for `Moodu`) was accepted *and* corrected — that part works.
Secondary oddity: correctly spelled answers with English appended also got "It is spelled **X**",
implying a mistake I didn't make.

**9. Stray internal text — `null`, `[not simulated]`, `undefined`?**

**None.** No placeholder or internal text anywhere in either transcript. The closest thing to a rendering
fault is the doubled `[say it like: ...]` block in question 6, and one turn in lesson 7 where the tutor
emitted two messages back to back with odd spacing ("I'll come back to that question later…" followed
immediately by "Here it is again." with a stray double space before the bracket). Cosmetic, not stray
internals.
