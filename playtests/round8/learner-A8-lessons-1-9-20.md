# Playtest — Learner A8, Telugu, lessons 1 / 9 / 20

Sessions: `r8a1`, `r8a9`, `r8a20`. Complete, 15/15 steps each. No lesson stalled or repeated itself four times.

---

## How it actually went

### Lesson 1

This was genuinely pleasant. Six words, one at a time, each with a pronunciation hint I could work from, and every single production of mine got a verdict. By the end I could say `Namaskaram, nenu bagunnanu, meeru ela unnaru?` off my own bat and I understood *why* — the tip that `Bagunnanu` is `baga` + `unnanu` welded together, with the vowel dropping at the seam, is the single best thing in all three lessons. It made "there is no separate word for 'am'" click instantly instead of feeling like a rule to memorise.

Two things went wrong.

**I asked how to say "and" and never got an answer.** The tutor said *Say 'I am fine, and you?'*. I don't have a word for "and" — it's not in my notebook and nobody had shown me one. I said so:

> You: Wait, how do I say 'and'? You haven't given me that word.
> Tutor: for "I am fine, and you?".
> Tutor: So — Say 'I am fine, and you?'.

That first line is not a sentence. It starts mid-clause. It reads like the tail end of something that got cut off. And the actual question — *what is the word for "and"* — was never answered, not then and not later. I ended up guessing `Bagunnanu, meeru?` and flagging that I was guessing.

**Then that guess got two opposite verdicts in one turn.**

> Tutor: You didn't use the full sentence. The correct way is "Nenu bagunnanu, meeru?". Remember to include 'Nenu' for 'I' in this case.
> System: 💡 Telugu usually drops *nenu* — *bagunnanu* already means "I am fine" on its own. This sentence is the exception...
> Tutor: Spot on!

Marked wrong, corrected, then told "Spot on!" three lines later. I sat there genuinely unsure whether I'd got it or not. Worse, the correction and the tip half-contradict each other: the tutor says *remember to include 'Nenu'*, the tip says *Telugu usually drops nenu*. The tip does explain the exception, so the content is right — but "remember to include Nenu" as a flat instruction is the wrong takeaway from it.

One smaller thing: I asked "why does it have a double n?" about `Unnaru` and got back "It's the polite form for anyone you would call *Meeru*" — which is the exact same sentence it had already given me twice, and has nothing to do with double letters.

### Lesson 9

Smoothest of the three. Six adjectives, no confusion, and combining them with nouns from my notebook (`Pedda illu`, `Manchi snehithudu`, `Vedi neeru`, `Idhi pedda pusthakam`, `Ayana manchi guruvu`) felt like the course paying off. That's the moment where a course earns its keep and this one did.

The problem here was one long tip arriving early and using words I hadn't been given yet. I asked whether `Manchi` goes before or after the noun. The reply:

> System: 💡 Nothing between the two words — *adhi chedu*, and likewise *idhi manchi* for "this is good". Telugu has two different jobs here and keeps them apart: *undi* says a thing EXISTS or is somewhere (*illu akkada undi*), and *bagundi* has it built in for exactly that reason. But pinning a plain description onto a noun needs no verb at all, which is why *adhi chedu* is already a complete sentence.

At the moment I read that, I had not been taught `chedu` (it arrived in the very next line) and had not been taught `bagundi` (two steps later). So the answer to my question leaned on two words I couldn't yet parse. Also it never actually said "before" — I had to infer it from the examples. It's a good explanation delivered at the wrong time.

And the one direct question I asked about `undi` was dropped entirely:

> You: Ayana manchi guruvu — do I need undi on the end of that, or not?
> Tutor: Perfect! Say 'That is bad'.

Graded, praised, question vanished. No acknowledgement it was even asked.

One reply also contradicted itself inside two sentences: *"Bagundi is specifically for things like meals, days, or films, and it's not used directly for people. This course hasn't covered that yet."* It answered me confidently and then told me it hadn't covered it. Pick one.

### Lesson 20

Framed properly as revision — `Eeroju` tagged "from lesson 12", `Baga` and `Santhosham` "from lesson 16", `Gurthundi` explicitly "This one is new". That's exactly right and I liked it.

Except `Sare`. It was introduced as flat-out new — *"To say 'Okay', say **Sare**"* — with no lesson tag, and `Sare` is in my notebook from lesson 4. So I asked about it. And this is the worst thing that happened in the whole playtest:

> You: Hang on, didn't we already do Sare back in lesson 4? Why is it coming up as a new word?
> Tutor: This is a revision lesson, so most of this is yours already.
> Tutor: **Correct.**
> System: 🎓 **Vocabulary done** — 5 words. Quick memory check next.

I produced zero Telugu. I asked a question in plain English. I was told "Correct." and the lesson advanced past the step and closed out the vocabulary phase. If I'd been a real learner who genuinely didn't know `Sare`, I would have been marked as knowing it and moved on. (The deflection is also a non-answer — it didn't address the lesson-4 point at all.)

The rest was fine. `Eeroju bagundi` for "Today was good" was one I'd have got wrong on my own — I said outright that I've never been taught a past-tense "was", and the tutor handled that well: *"This course has not covered it yet. However, you can say 'Eeroju bagundi'..."* then re-asked. That's the right shape.

Two cosmetic oddities. When I bundled a question onto `Gurthundi`, the reply opened `Tutor: Gurthundi. 🐾 It means "it is remembered"...` — echoing my own word back at me with a paw emoji stuck mid-sentence. Later, `Naaku gurthundi. Quick one — is naaku always needed...?` got a turn that was literally just `Tutor: Naaku gurthundi.` before the tip. Both times the actual question ("does it literally mean 'to me it is remembered'", "can I drop naaku") went unanswered.

And there's a leaked line. Answering my `Santhosham` question, it said: *"Santhosham is a noun, meaning 'happiness'. **Your friend says they feel happy today.**"* Nobody mentioned a friend. That sentence belongs to some other prompt.

Also a gloss drift: taught as "it is remembered", quizzed as *"What's the word for 'Remember'?"*, and the closing summary says `Gurthundi — Remember`. Three different glosses for one word.

### What I can actually use unprompted

## The eleven questions

**1. Was a bare question that merely names the answer ever graded correct?**
**No — five for five, it was correctly refused.** All five got their question answered and then the step re-asked verbatim:

- `Is unnaru a verb or an adjective?` → *"Tutor: Here it is again. 🐾 "You are" is **Unnaru**..."*
- `Is bagunnanu something I'd say to a stranger?` → *"Tutor: So — what's the Telugu word for "I'm fine"?"*
- `Is chinna used for people too, or only for objects?` → *"Tutor: Here it is again. 🐾 To say "Small", say **Chinna**"*
- `Can bagundi be used about a person?` → *"Tutor: So — what's the Telugu word for "It is good"?"*
- `Is santhosham a noun or an adjective?` → *"Tutor: Here it is again. 🐾 **Santhosham**, "happiness", from lesson 16..."*

This one works cleanly. **But** see Q2 — a *different* kind of question-only turn does get graded correct, so the protection isn't general.

**2. Was a plain-English "I don't know" ever graded correct?**
**"I don't know" itself, no. A question-only turn, yes — once, and it was bad.**

The honest blank was handled right: *"You: I don't know, sorry"* → *"Tutor: No worries! The word for "How" in Telugu is **Ela**. / Tutor: So — what's the Telugu word for "How"?"* — no credit, re-asked. Same for *"I've never been taught a past tense for 'was'..."* in L20.

The failure: L20, at a step whose target was `Sare`, I wrote *"Hang on, didn't we already do Sare back in lesson 4? Why is it coming up as a new word?"* — an English question, no attempt at an answer — and got **"Tutor: Correct."** followed by **"🎓 Vocabulary done"**. The step was credited and the phase closed on a turn where I produced nothing.

**3. Were my questions answered — especially bundled onto a correct answer?**
Sixteen questions. Roughly half landed. Bundled ones were the weakest.

Answered well (5): `Nenu` gender — *"same for both men and women... doesn't change with gender"*; `chinna` people-or-objects — *"can be used for both"*; the Telugu alphabet, bundled onto a correct answer — *"a syllabic script, and each letter represents a sound"*; "bigger", bundled onto a correct answer — *"This course has not covered how to say "bigger" yet"*; past-tense "was" — *"has not covered it yet. However, you can say 'Eeroju bagundi'"*.

Answered badly or not at all (6, five of them bundled onto correct answers):
- `Unnaru - why does it have a double n?` → *"It's the polite form for anyone you would call *Meeru*."* Wrong question answered, and it's a line I'd already been given twice.
- `Ayana manchi guruvu — do I need undi on the end of that, or not?` → **silently dropped.** Straight to *"Perfect! Say 'That is bad'."*
- `Gurthundi - so does that literally mean 'to me it is remembered'?` → restated the definition, didn't address "literally".
- `Naaku gurthundi. Quick one — is naaku always needed with gurthundi, or can I drop it like nenu?` → *"Tutor: Naaku gurthundi."* then a tip that doesn't answer it.
- `how do I say 'and'?` (asked alone) → the broken fragment, then never.
- `didn't we already do Sare in lesson 4?` (asked alone) → deflected with a generic revision line, and wrongly credited.
- `Manchi - does it go before or after the noun?` → a tip that answers by example only, using two untaught words.

Pattern: **a question bundled onto a correct answer gets you the grade but often loses the question.** Questions asked *alone* fared worst of all.

**4. When a teaching step showed TWO words at once, was I asked to say both? Did the memory check later quiz a word I was never asked to produce?**
Both handled correctly.

L1: *"Your turn — say **Bagunnanu**, then **Ela**"* — both named, both pronunciation hints given on separate lines. L9: *"Your turn — say **Vedi**, then **Bagundi**"* — same.

Memory checks quizzed only words I'd produced: L1 asked "I'm fine", "How", "You are"; L9 asked "Hot", "It is good", "Bad". No gap. L20's five words were all single-word steps.

**5. The answer immediately before a 🎓 or ✨ banner — did it get feedback?**
**Yes, every time — nine of nine banners.** L1: `Bagunnanu, Ela` → *"Correct."*; the double-n turn → *"Great job!"*; `Bagunnanu, meeru?` → *"Spot on!"*; the full greeting → *"Correct! That's the last one."* L9: *"Correct."* / *"Great job!"* / *"Spot on!"* / *"Correct!"* L20: *"Correct."* / *"Great job!"* / *"Spot on!"* / *"Correct! That's the last one."*

Two caveats, though. The pre-🎓 turn in L1 got *"You didn't use the full sentence"* **and** *"Spot on!"* — feedback, but self-cancelling. And the pre-🎓 turn in L20 got *"Correct."* on a turn where I said no Telugu at all.

**6. Was a correct answer ever marked wrong?**
Only in the contradictory L1 turn, and it's arguable. `Bagunnanu, meeru?` drew *"You didn't use the full sentence. The correct way is "Nenu bagunnanu, meeru?"."* — a fair correction against the target, undercut by *"Spot on!"* in the same turn and by the tip's own statement that *"Telugu usually drops *nenu*"*. Otherwise no: every genuinely right answer was marked right, including ones where I attached English text.

**7. "Note the spelling"**
Clean. It fired six times, all six on real slips of mine: `Meru`→`Meeru` (vowel length), `baagunnaanu`→`bagunnanu` (vowel length, twice over), `pustakam`→`pusthakam`, `Eroju`→`Eeroju` (vowel length), `Gurtundi`→`Gurthundi`, `veltunnanu`→`velthunnanu`. It never fired on a word I spelled correctly, and it never mislabelled a grammar change as a spelling error — the L1 `Nenu`-missing case was correctly handled as a sentence correction, not a spelling note.

One nit: it always arrives welded to a positive verdict (*"Yes. Note the spelling..."*, *"Exactly! Note the spelling..."*) — so a vowel slip is simultaneously right and flagged. That's probably deliberate and I didn't mind it. Second nit: in L1 it echoed the whole sentence rather than the wrong word — *"Note the spelling: **Namaskaram, nenu bagunnanu**"* — which makes you hunt for what changed.

**8. Stray internal text?**
Five hits, and one of them is bad.

- **A line starting mid-sentence** (L1): `Tutor: for "I am fine, and you?".` — the entire reply to my "how do I say 'and'" question.
- **Leaked roleplay text** (L20): *"Santhosham is a noun, meaning "happiness". **Your friend says they feel happy today.**"* No friend exists in this session.
- **A turn that is only an echoed word** (L20): `Tutor: Naaku gurthundi.` — just my own answer read back.
- **A paw emoji inside a prose answer** (L20): `Tutor: Gurthundi. 🐾 It means "it is remembered,"...` — 🐾 is the new-word marker everywhere else, so seeing it in an answer to a question is misleading.
- **The same note repeated** (L1): *"It's a polite form used for anyone you would call *Meeru*"* / *"It's the polite form for anyone you would call *Meeru*"* — served as the answer to two different questions and as part of the teaching line, three times total.

Plus a self-contradiction that reads like a bolted-on template (L9): *"Bagundi is specifically for things like meals, days, or films, and it's not used directly for people. This course hasn't covered that yet."*

No `null`, no `undefined`, no `{w}`, no `[not simulated]`, no "Not quite, not quite", no merged pronunciation blocks.

**9. Two different tasks in one turn, or praise landing on a turn I never took?**
No double-tasking — every turn asked for exactly one thing.

Misplaced praise, yes, twice. The big one is L20's *"Tutor: Correct."* on my question-only turn about `Sare`. The other is L1's *"Spot on!"* arriving three lines after *"You didn't use the full sentence"* on the same answer.

**10. Lesson 9's `undi` — coherent? Lesson 20 — framed as revision?**

`undi`: the content is coherent and actually good — *"*undi* says a thing EXISTS or is somewhere (*illu akkada undi*), and *bagundi* has it built in for exactly that reason. But pinning a plain description onto a noun needs no verb at all"* — that's a clear, correct distinction, and it's consistent with the L20 tip (*"*Bagundi* is the whole comment... so it needs no separate word for "was""*) and with L1's `bagunnanu` breakdown. Three tips, one story. Good.

The **delivery** is not coherent. It arrived as the answer to a word-order question it doesn't directly answer, and it used `chedu` (taught one line later) and `bagundi` (taught two steps later) as its worked examples, so at the moment of reading it was partly opaque. And when I asked a plain direct `undi` question — *"do I need undi on the end of that, or not?"* — I got nothing.

Revision framing: **yes, and it's well done** — *"A revision lesson, so most of this is yours already"*, with `Eeroju` "from lesson 12", `Baga` and `Santhosham` "from lesson 16", and `Gurthundi` explicitly *"This one is new"*. Two breaks: `Sare` was presented as brand new (*"To say "Okay", say **Sare**"*) despite being lesson 4 vocabulary with no back-reference, and the closing summary lists all five as flat new words with no revision marking.

**11. Would I keep going?**
Yes — but the `Sare` bug would have to go first, and I'd be annoyed about the questions.

The teaching is genuinely good. Six words a lesson, one at a time, always a pronunciation hint, then compose-a-phrase, then a scene. `bagunnanu` = `baga` + `unnanu`, `bagundi` = `baga` + `undi`, `unnanu`/`unnaru`/`unnavu` as one endings pattern — that's a real mental model, not flashcards, and by lesson 20 I was building `Nenu ippudu paniki velthunnanu` without help. The spelling notes are accurate and never nagged me. Marks were fair on every actual attempt.

What would make me quit is the questions. I asked sixteen and about six went into a void — and the ones I bundled onto a correct answer, which is exactly how a curious person asks, were the ones most likely to be swallowed. Twice I got my own words parroted back instead of an answer. Once I asked for a word the lesson was demanding (`and`), got a broken half-line, guessed, and was marked down for guessing. That specific sequence is the one that would genuinely make me put the app down: it asked me for something it hadn't taught, refused to tell me when I asked, and then penalised me.

And being told **"Correct."** for typing an English question is the kind of thing that makes you stop trusting the score. Once I know a step can be passed without producing anything, the 🎓 banners stop meaning anything.

Fix the question handling and the phantom credit, and this is a course I'd stay on.
