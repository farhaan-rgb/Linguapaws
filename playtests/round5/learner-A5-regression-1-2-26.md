# Playtest — learner A5, Telugu, lessons 1 / 2 / 26

Round 5 regression pass. Three fresh sessions (`r5a1`, `r5a2`, `r5a26`). I came in
knowing zero Telugu; for lessons 2 and 26 I had the `--notes` notebook and nothing
else.

---

## How it actually went

### Lesson 1 — Pronouns & Greetings

This one worked. Six words came at me one at a time, each with a pronunciation hint,
and by the end I could genuinely produce `Namaskaram, nenu bagunnanu. Meeru ela
unnaru?` without being fed the pieces. That's a real thing I can say to a person. The
best moment was the note explaining that `bagunnanu` is `baga` + `unnanu` glued
together — that suddenly made the whole lesson feel like a system instead of six
noises to memorise.

Two things snagged.

The first was an ordering slip. When I asked a question on turn 4, the answer told me
that `unnanu` is "the ending you already met inside *bagunnanu*". I had not met
`bagunnanu`. It was introduced in the very next sentence of the same reply. Small
thing, but as a beginner it made me scroll back thinking I'd missed a screen.

The second was more annoying: I got the exact same explanation note four separate
times across the lesson, and once it printed **twice in a row, verbatim**, in the
same reply:

> System: 💡 Telugu usually drops *nenu* — ... "**I'm** fine, and you?".
> System: 💡 Telugu usually drops *nenu* — ... "**I'm** fine, and you?".

Also, when I asked "is it rude to drop the meeru?", what I got back was two stacked
notes, neither of which was about that. It reads as if any question just triggers
"print the notes attached to the words in your sentence" rather than "answer this".

One thing I want to flag as odd rather than broken: I never actually answered the
review question "What's the word for 'You are'?" correctly. I said `Unnanu` (wrong),
then asked a question instead of retrying — and the lesson replied with the
explanation and then **"Review passed"**. I got moved on from a card I'd failed. Nice
for me, suspicious for the grader.

I said "ok" once during roleplay. It was marked "Not quite" and I got a hint. That's
the right call.

### Lesson 2 — 'What' & This/That

Smoothest of the three. The `idhi` vs `ee` distinction is genuinely well explained —
"standing alone against sitting in front of a noun" is the sentence that made it
click, and it explicitly heads off the wrong guess (that it's about distance) twice.
The word-order note (pointing word first, `emiti` last) meant `Idhi emiti?` and
`Adhi emiti?` came out of me without hesitation later.

The last task — "ask a stranger what their name is using words you know" — was the
single best-handled moment of the whole playtest. I only had `meeru`, `peru`, `emiti`,
so I guessed `Meeru peru emiti?` and said out loud that I had no word for "your". The
reply told me my version is the real casual spoken form, named `mee` as the tidier
possessive, and told me it arrives in lesson 5. That's exactly what I needed and it
didn't punish me for the gap.

One untaught word did leak: while explaining `Ee`, the tutor used *ee illu* = "this
house". `illu` is lesson 3. I could infer it from the gloss so it wasn't harmful, but
it is Telugu I hadn't been shown.

My question about whether `dh` sounds different from plain `d` was not answered — I
got the "That" note again instead. As a beginner reading transliteration, that's an
actual comprehension question, not a nice-to-have.

### Lesson 26 — Feelings

Vocab and review were fine. The frame note is genuinely good — `naaku X-ga undi` as
the reliable default, `nenu X-ga unnanu` as the same meaning with weight on you, plus
the two exceptions (negation drops `-ga`; talking about someone else takes `unnaru`).
That note is why I could then produce `Naaku bhayam ledu` for "I am not scared"
correctly on the first try, for a word the lesson never drilled in that frame.

**And then I got stuck properly, for the only time in the playtest.**

Task: "Say 'I am very tired today'." I have `eeroju` (L12), `chala` = many/very (L8),
`alupu` (this lesson). I tried:

1. `Eeroju nenu chala alupu unnanu` → Not quite. (Fair — I dropped the `-ga`.)
2. `Eeroju nenu baga alupuga unnanu` → Not quite.
3. `Eeroju naaku chala alupuga undi` → Perfect!

Attempt 2 is where I lost faith. The note I'd just been given says, of `nenu` X-ga
`unnanu`, "**both are accepted**". Then the grader rejected exactly that frame, with
no explanation of why, and the same one-line reply three times running. Whatever
"both are accepted" means, it clearly doesn't mean it here, and nothing told me the
task wanted the `naaku ... undi` frame specifically.

Making it worse: the hint was **"Today + I + well + tired"**. `chala` was taught to me
as "many / very"; "well" is `baga`. So the hint pushed me toward `baga`, which is what
attempt 2 was, and attempt 2 was wrong. And the accepted answer uses `naaku`, so the
"+ I +" slot in the hint isn't `nenu` at all. I got there by brute force, not
understanding. It's the one place in three lessons where I passed without knowing why.

Also, when I asked about the hint's wording mid-failure, the reply was the
`Santhosham` vocabulary note — completely unrelated to what I'd asked — followed by
the identical "Not quite" line.
