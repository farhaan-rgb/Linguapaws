# Learner E5 — Telugu lessons 19, 20, 21

I'm 18 lessons in, English-only, working from the notebook the app gives me. Here's how the three lessons went, honestly.

## The short version

Lesson 19 (colours) was the cleanest of the three and I came out actually able to use it. Lesson 21 (postpositions) taught me a real, useful rule and I can apply it — with one gap I'll get to. Lesson 20, the one billed as "review of your day," is the one I'd send back. It's mostly words I already had, it marked a correct answer wrong, and at one point it told me the answer and then rejected the answer it had just told me.

Across all three: **not one of my six questions got an answer.** Not one. That's the thing that wore me down most.

---

## Lesson 19 — Colours & Clothes

Five words: Rangu (colour), Batta (clothes), Telupu (white), Nalupu (black), Erupu (red). Clean teaching loop, good pronunciation hints, and by the end I could genuinely say "Naaku erupu batta kaavali" without looking anything up. That's a win — it reused Naaku and Kaavali from lesson 4 and I felt the old stuff clicking into the new stuff.

**Where it wobbled:**

- I noticed white/black/red all end in **-upu** and asked whether that's a colour ending. The tutor just re-printed the Erupu prompt at me ("Here it is again"). No answer. That's a real pattern I spotted on my own and I still don't know if it's a thing.
- "I like red" — I was never taught how to *build* a "like" sentence. My notebook says `Ishtam = Like` and `Naaku = to me` and that's it. I guessed **Naaku erupu ishtam** and said out loud that I was guessing. It was marked "Exactly!" — so I got full credit for something I assembled from scraps. Nice that it worked, but nobody ever told me that pattern, and I only knew to guess it because I'd been staring at the notebook.
- The last drill asked me to **"Ask 'Which color is that?'"** — and I have never been taught a word for "which." I said so plainly, in English. I got **"Not quite."** Marked wrong for honestly saying I hadn't been taught something. The hint then revealed the intended answer uses *emi* — "what" — not "which" at all. So the English prompt asks for a word that doesn't exist in the lesson, and the answer is a "what" question dressed up as a "which" question. That's just confusing.
- **Zero grammar notes in the entire lesson.** Not one. And this lesson quietly introduced *two* patterns that needed one: adjective-before-noun (Erupu rangu, Telupu batta) and the whole `Naaku X ishtam` construction. Both just... happened, and I had to infer them.

**Idhi vs Ee:** I got "This is white" wrong by writing *Ee telupu* instead of *Idhi telupu* — genuine confusion on my part, my notebook lists both as "this." The hint ("This + white") didn't disambiguate. I worked it out myself, asked the tutor to confirm my theory (Ee needs a noun after it, Idhi stands alone), and got no confirmation — just credit for the answer and on to the next thing. I *think* I'm right. Nobody told me.

## Lesson 20 — "Review & Dialogue: Your Day"

**Does it review, or just add five words?** Mostly the former, and I want to be fair about that: of the five "new" words, **four were already in my notebook** — Eeroju (lesson 12), Baga (16), Santhosham (16), Sare (4). Only **Gurthundi** (remember) was genuinely new. So yes, it really is a review lesson.

But it doesn't *behave* like one. Every one of those four was presented as a brand-new teach: "To say Today, say **Eeroju**." I flagged it in my very first reply — "I already have this one from lesson 12, is this lesson repeating on purpose?" — and got "Correct." and the next word. It felt slightly insulting, like the app hadn't read its own notebook. A review lesson should say *"remember this one?"*, not pretend we've never met. And my notebook now has Eeroju, Baga, Santhosham and Sare listed twice, which makes it messier to study from, not cleaner.

**Then the actual problems.**

*The -ga ending nobody taught me.* Drill: "I am happy today." I wrote **Eeroju nenu santhosham unnanu**. Response: "Correct! Note the spelling: **Eeroju nenu santhoshamga unnanu**." That's not a spelling note — that's a grammar rule. `Santhosham` (happiness, a noun) becomes `santhoshamga` (happily/happy, describing me) and *that is the entire point*, and it was delivered as if I'd fat-fingered a key. My notebook only ever listed "santhoshamga" as an alternate spelling of Santhosham, which is actively misleading — they're different forms doing different jobs.

*Same thing with -ki.* "I am going to work now" — I wrote **pani**, got "Great job! Note the spelling: **paniki**." Again: `pani` → `paniki` (work → to work) is a grammatical ending, not a typo. Both of these got flagged as spelling, both were graded correct anyway, and I only clocked what was happening because I was deliberately testing it. A normal learner would read "note the spelling" and conclude they'd misspelled a word.

*The grammar note that came too late.* Drill: "I remember." I answered **Gurthundi** — literally the word I'd been taught for "remember," ten steps earlier, with no qualifiers. I was marked **"Not quite,"** and *only then* did the note appear explaining that Telugu treats remembering as something that is the case for you, so it needs *naaku*. Good note! Genuinely helpful, cleared it up instantly. Arrived one turn after it would have saved me. And it cites *naaku pani undi* as a phrase I'd recognise — I've never seen that phrase in my life.

*Then the note printed twice in the same turn.* My next reply got the identical 💡 block stacked on itself, two copies back to back. Looked broken.

*The worst bit.* Drill: **"Miko asks about your day. Say 'It was good'."** I wrote **Bagundi** — my notebook literally says `Bagundi = It is good`. Marked **"Not quite,"** with the hint **"Today + well."** But the English prompt contains no "today," and "well" is *Baga*, an adverb, not "good." So I said the hint confused me and tried what the hint told me to try: **Eeroju baga**. And got back:

> Tutor: Eeroju bagundi.
> Tutor: Not quite. Hint: Today + well.

It **stated the answer and rejected it in the same breath.** It gave me `Eeroju bagundi` and then told me the answer was "Today + well" — which is `Eeroju baga`, a different thing. I typed the answer it had just handed me and got "Exactly!" So: the hint was wrong, the drill's English didn't match its expected answer, a literally-correct answer from my own notebook was marked wrong, and the tutor contradicted its own grader. That's four separate failures in one exchange, and it's the point where I stopped trusting the grading.

Also, it replied to me in bare Telugu — "Eeroju bagundi." — with no translation and no framing. As a beginner I had no idea whether that was a correction, an example, or Miko chatting.

**What I got out of it:** Gurthundi, and the `naaku` pattern, which is genuinely valuable. Everything else I already had. As a confidence lap over old material it half-works; as a review it needs to stop pretending the words are new.

## Lesson 21 — Postpositions

This one I liked. Lo (in), Paina (on/above), Kindha (under), Tho (with), Daggara (near). And the core rule landed properly:

> 💡 All five of these follow the noun rather than coming before it — Telugu has postpositions, not prepositions. "In the house" is *inti lo*, never *lo inti*.

That arrived on the **first sentence drill**, which is exactly the right moment — before I could get it wrong, right when I needed it. Best-timed note in the three lessons. And it's a rule I could immediately reuse: I did Pusthakam paina, Naa daggara kurchondi, Ayana tho vellandi and Pedda kurchi kindha in a row with no help, pulling nouns from lessons 2, 5, 8 and 9. **Can I use these on a new noun afterwards? Yes.** I even tried an unprompted one at the end (Neeru pusthakam paina undi) to see if I could build one myself. That's the lesson doing its job.

**But the noun-shape question is the hole.** The brief asks whether the noun has to change first, and here's what happened: I answered "In the house" with **Illu lo** — my notebook's word for house plus the postposition, exactly following the rule. It was marked **"Perfect!"** The note in the same breath said the phrase is ***inti lo***.

So the noun *does* change — `Illu` → `inti` — and nobody told me. Not taught, not corrected, not even flagged as a spelling note (which is what lesson 20 would have done). My answer was accepted, so I had no signal it was wrong; I only picked up "inti" because I happened to read the note carefully and copied it on the next drill. **I still don't know when a noun has to change shape before a postposition, or which nouns do it, or what the changed form is called.** Given lesson 20 was silently doing the same thing with `pani` → `paniki`, this feels like the single biggest gap across all three lessons — endings and stem changes are happening constantly and are never once named as a thing.

**"With me" was a mess.** Drill: "Say 'With me' (using Tho)." I have `Nenu = I` and `Naa = my`. There is no word for "me" in my notebook. I said so, and tried **Nenu tho**. Got neither right nor wrong — just "So — Say 'With me' (using Tho)." again. Guessed **Naa tho** on the next go and it was accepted with no acknowledgement at all. So the drill wanted an oblique pronoun form I was never given, and I found it by trial and error.

**"Tell someone to sit near you"** is ambiguous in English — near *me* (the speaker) or near *you* (whoever I'm addressing)? I guessed *Naa daggara kurchondi* (near me) and it passed, but I was coin-flipping.

**The "grammar notes" that aren't.** Three times, a 💡 note fired that just restated the vocabulary line: "💡 'On' or 'above' is Paina." "💡 'With' is Tho." (twice). Those aren't notes, they're echoes, and they fired specifically on the turns where I'd asked a question — so it *looked* like the app was about to answer me and then didn't. That's worse than silence. And the final note ("The postposition goes AFTER the thing") is the same rule as the first one, restated, on the very last step, after I'd already used it correctly four times.

No, several weren't:
- **"Which"** (L19, final drill) — never taught. Marked "Not quite" for saying so. Turned out to want *emi* = "what".

- **Illu → inti** (L21) — the stem change for "house" before a postposition. Never taught, never corrected, appeared only inside a grammar note while my un-changed form was marked correct.
- **"me"** as distinct from **Nenu** (L21, "With me") — no word for oblique "me" anywhere in my notebook. Found *Naa tho* by guessing.

**7. Typos — graded consistently, correct spelling shown, no false corrections?**

Mostly good, with one consistent hole.

Graded well:
- `Teelupu` (vowel-length slip on Telupu) → "Yes. Note the spelling: **Telupu**." Correct spelling shown. 
- `Baagaa` — exactly what the hint "baa-gaa" tells you to write → "Right. Note the spelling: **Baga**." Correct. *Though: the hint and the spelling disagree, which is why I wrote it that way. `Baga` with hint `baa-gaa` is a trap.*
- `Gurtundi` (dropped h) → "Exactly! Note the spelling: **Gurthundi**." Correct.

**The hole: every typo on the 5th and final vocabulary word got no feedback at all.** `Thelupu` (L19) and `Dagara` (L21) were both swallowed — no "yes," no "note the spelling," nothing. The stage-transition banner ("🎓 Vocabulary done" / "🎓 Review passed") replaced the grading line entirely. Same thing happened to `Naa tho` in L21 and `Santhosham` in L20. So the answer immediately before every stage boundary is silently accepted. Inconsistent, and it means I was never shown that `Dagara` should be `Daggara`.

**No false corrections** — nothing I'd spelled right was "corrected." But `santhosham` → `santhoshamga` and `pani` → `paniki` were both labelled "note the spelling" when they're grammar, which is a *different* kind of wrong: it teaches me to read a real rule as a keyboard slip.

- **A 💡 note printed twice, back to back, in a single response** (L20, the *naaku gurthundi* turn) — identical text stacked.

**9. Two tasks in one turn, or praise on a turn I never took?**

No misplaced praise — every "Correct/Perfect/Exactly" landed on a turn I'd actually taken.

Borderline on two-tasks: **L20's "Final check: Say hello, I am very happy"** is two speech acts (greeting + statement) in one prompt. It's a fair natural sentence and I handled it, but it's the only drill in three lessons asking for two things.

The genuine sequencing problem is the reverse: **grading and a new task collide, and the grading loses.** At every stage boundary the banner ate my grade (see Q7). And in L20's *Eeroju bagundi* turn, a tutor statement and a contradictory grade of that same statement shared one response.

**10. Did any word appear with a different meaning than my notebook gives it?**

Yes, two:

And the actual expected answer used `bagundi`, not `baga`. So the same lesson treats Baga as both "well" and "good" and then rejects it.
- **`Santhosham`.** Notebook: "Happiness" (a noun). Used in L20 as **"happy"** (an adjective) via the unexplained `-ga`, with the two forms listed in my notebook as if they were spelling variants of one word.
- Milder: **`Pani`** is "Work" in my notebook and appeared as `paniki` = "to work" with no note that the ending changes the meaning. Same for **`Illu`** → `inti`.

## How it felt

Lesson 19 felt good — I finished it able to say things I couldn't say an hour before, and the old vocabulary knitting into the new colours was satisfying.

Lesson 21 felt genuinely educational. The postposition rule is the best-taught thing in these three lessons: arrived at the right moment, phrased clearly, and I could immediately use it on nouns from six lessons back. I'd keep that note exactly as it is. I just wish someone had told me the noun changes shape.

Lesson 20 was demoralising. Being marked wrong for `Bagundi` when my own notebook says it means "It is good," then being handed the answer and marked wrong for it in the same breath, then having the hint point at a third thing — that's the moment I stopped believing the grader and started gaming it. And the four recycled words presented as new made me feel like the app wasn't tracking what I knew.

The thing I'd fix first, above every individual bug: **answer my questions.** I asked eight times across three lessons, including things I'd worked out for myself and just wanted confirmed. Zero answers. It never cost me credit, which I appreciate — but when a 💡 icon lights up on the exact turn I asked something and then just repeats the vocabulary word at me, it's hard not to feel talked at rather than taught.

Second fix: **stop calling grammar "spelling."** Three real rules (`-ga`, `-ki`, and the `Illu`→`inti` change) reached me disguised as typo corrections. Those are the most important things in these lessons and they arrived in the smallest font.
