# Learner D5 — Telugu lessons 13, 14, 15

Beginner, English-only, came in off lessons 1–12. Sessions `r5d13`, `r5d14`, `r5d15`,
15 steps each, all three finished. Nobody had been through these before me.

## How it went, plainly

**Lesson 13 (past tense).** I was handed five finished past-tense words in the first
five turns — *chesanu, vellanu, thinnanu, chusanu, cheppanu* — with no explanation of
any kind attached. Not a word about how a past tense is built, what the pieces are, or
what changed from the verbs I already knew. Just five sound-shapes to memorise. The
first grammar note didn't show up until turn 9, well after the vocabulary and the
memory check were over, and even then it wasn't about the past tense — it was about the
*person* ending: "*chesaru* is 'you did', *chesadu* is 'he did'". That note is useful
and I'm glad I got it. But it answers a question I hadn't asked while leaving unanswered
the one I asked twice.

**Lesson 14 (future tense).** Same shape. Four future forms plus *repu*, which I'd
already learned in lesson 12 — so lesson 14 actually taught me four new words in a
five-word slot. The notes here were better and one of them was genuinely the best line
in all three lessons: "The future is carried by the verb itself — *tintanu* — so the
time word only says when, it does not do the tense." That is exactly the right thing to
tell someone. Unfortunately I got that same note four separate times, mostly at moments
where it had nothing to do with what I was doing, and only the fourth firing (when I
actually used *tharuvatha*) was on-topic.

The nastiest thing about lesson 14 is the collision with lesson 11 and nobody warns you
about it. *Velthunnanu* / *velthanu*. *Tintunnanu* / *tintanu*. *Chestunnanu* /
*chestanu*. Three of the four new words are one syllable away from a word I already
have, meaning something different. I made that exact mistake on purpose to see what
would happen (typed *Tintunnanu* for "I will eat") and got a bare "Not quite!" with no
indication that I'd just said the present continuous. That's the single most predictable
error in the whole lesson and it gets the least helpful response.

**Lesson 15 (why / like / no).** Best of the three. The notes were on time, they were
specific, and one of them pre-answered a question I'd just asked in the same breath —
I asked whether *ishtam* was a noun or a verb and the note came back with
*naaku ishtam undi* = "I like it", which is precisely what I needed and which I then
used correctly twice. That felt like being taught. Two problems: *bhayam* ("fear") was
taught and then never appeared again in any sentence, drill, or conversation — dead
weight; and one conversation prompt is simply broken (below).

## What I can actually use unprompted

Nine memorised whole words: four past, four future, plus the why/like/no set. I can slot
a time word in front of them (*ninna adhi chesanu*, *repu intiki velthanu*,
*tharuvatha tintanu*) and I got that right first try every time, so the "time word goes
before the verb, verb carries the tense" idea landed properly.

What I cannot do is make a tense. Which is the point of two of these lessons.

## Where I got stuck

- "Confirm you saw Miko" (L13). I had no idea what was wanted. I don't have an object
  form of "you" — only *meeru* as a subject — so I said so in English and offered
  *chusanu*. Marked wrong. The hint then revealed it wanted a proper name inserted:
  *Nenu Miko chusanu*. "Confirm you saw Miko" doesn't tell you a name is the answer.
- "I went and I ate" (L13). No word for "and" has ever been taught, in any of thirteen
  lessons. I said so. See Q7 below for what happened next.
- "Because I am tired" (L15). Never taught "tired". Never taught "because". Broken
  prompt — see Q2.
- *Tintunnanu* vs *tintanu*. Got it wrong, asked what I'd got wrong, was never told.

## The ten questions

### 1. Could I conjugate a new verb into past or future?

**No. I can only repeat the eight forms I was handed.**

I tried three times to get a rule and was ignored every time — mid-lesson in 13
("is there a rule I can apply to a verb you haven't given me?"), mid-lesson in 14
(a concrete guess: from *ivvandi* = give, would "I will give" be *ivvatanu*?), and once
after 13 finished. The post-lesson attempt got "That's the scenario done — nothing left
to answer here."

What I worked out on my own afterwards, by lining the two sets up:

| past | future | present cont. (L11) |
|---|---|---|
| chesanu | chestanu | chestunnanu |
| vellanu | velthanu | velthunnanu |
| thinnanu | tintanu | tintunnanu |
| chusanu | chustanu | — |

So past→future looks like "put a *t* in before the *-anu*". That much I can see. But I
only have that because both lessons happened to use the same four verbs — nobody pointed
it out, and neither lesson mentions the other. And it's no use for a new verb, because
the *stem* is the part I can't get: *thinnanu* → *tintanu* loses a whole consonant,
*vellanu* → *velthanu* changes *ll* to *lth*. There's no stated stem, no stated rule, no
dictionary form to start from. Given "sit" (*kurchondi*) or "give" (*ivvandi*) — the
only shape of those verbs I own is a polite command — I would be inventing
*ivvatanu* / *kurchotanu* out of thin air and I'd have no reason to think I was right.

I'd also flag: the only non-"I" forms I ever saw were three throwaway examples inside
notes (*chesaru, chesadu, chestaru*). I was never once asked to produce one, so I
couldn't reliably say "he will go" either.

Two lessons titled "Simple Past Tense" and "Simple Future Tense" left me with eight
vocabulary items and no tense.

### 2. Was every word a drill asked for actually taught?

No. Three failures, one of them serious.

- **"I went and I ate" (L13)** needs "and", which no lesson has taught. The accepted
  answer turns out to be *Nenu vellanu, nenu thinnanu* — repeat the pronoun, no
  conjunction. That's a real Telugu strategy and it's fine, but it was never taught
  either, so the task is unanswerable until the tutor leaks the answer.
- **"Confirm you saw Miko" (L13)** requires knowing a bare proper noun goes in the
  object slot. Not taught, and not signalled by the prompt.
- **"Because I am tired" (L15) is a broken prompt.** No word for "tired" exists in the
  course; no word for "because" exists either. I asked for the word for "tired" outright
  and was ignored. Then the hint came back as **"Because + I + going"** — *going*, not
  *tired*. The accepted answer was *Andhuke nenu velthunnanu*, "that's why I am going".
  The English prompt and the graded answer are about different things. Anybody who
  actually tries to translate the sentence they were given cannot pass this step.

### 3. Did a rule I was graded on arrive only after I'd been marked wrong?

Not on any specific grading, no — the rules I was marked against were mostly things I
already had. But the timing is still wrong in the more damaging direction: in both tense
lessons **every grammar note arrived after all five words and the whole memory check
were done.** The past-tense note (turn 9) and the future-tense person note (turn 11)
both landed after I'd already been drilled on the forms. I memorised first and was
explained to afterwards, both times.

And the one moment where a note would have rescued me — right after I got *tintunnanu* /
*tintanu* wrong and asked which piece was the future — the note that fired was the
*tharuvatha* one, for the fourth time, which doesn't address it. The relevant note
("Same *-nu* ending as the past tense... *chestaru* is 'you will do'") came one turn
later, attached to a correct answer.

### 4. Was a correct answer ever marked wrong?

No — and I probed this deliberately.

- Correct answer with an English question bolted on: five attempts across the three
  lessons, **credited every time.** Never once penalised. (Whether the question got
  answered is another matter — Q5.)
- Dropped optional pronoun: *Akkada vellanu* without *nenu* (L13),
  *Ikkada enduku unnaru?* without *meeru* (L15) — both accepted.

The grading is generous in the right direction. The problem is the opposite one, which
is Q10.

to know I'm supposed to use brackets.

The two questions I most needed answered — "is there a rule?" and "which piece is the
future?" — are both in the ignored column. That's not a coincidence of syntax; those are
the questions a tense lesson has to be able to answer.

### 6. Did the tutor reply in Telugu, or use untaught Telugu?

Never replied *in* Telugu — all English, good. It did use Telugu I hadn't been given, in
two forms, both mild:

- **Note words:** *chesaru, chesadu, chestaru* (L13/L14), *naaku ishtam undi/ledu* (L15).
  These are explanatory and clearly labelled as illustration, so they read fine, and the
  *ishtam undi* one was actively helpful.
- **Same-turn spoilers:** twice a note used a word in the same output block as the line
  that first teaches it — *tintanu* in L14 turn 2, *ledu* in L15 turn 3. The note is
  printed above the teach line, so you meet the word before you're taught it, in the same
  breath. Cosmetic, but it reads as sloppy.

### 7. Did the tutor state an answer the grader then rejected, or contradict the lesson?

**Yes, once, and it's the clearest bug in lesson 13.** On "I went and I ate":

```
You: I don't have a word for 'and' ... Guessing: Vellanu thinnanu
Tutor: Nenu vellanu, nenu thinnanu. Now you can try saying: 'I went and I ate.'
Tutor: Not quite. Hint: I + went + I + ate. Final check: 'I went and I ate'.
```

It gave me the answer and marked me wrong for not having it, in the same turn. I typed
its own sentence back verbatim on the next turn and it passed. So the tutor voice and
the grader are not talking to each other.

On the specific thing I was told to watch for — a claim that an ending "changes based on
context" when it actually marks the future — **I did not see that.** The tense notes I
got were correct: "The future is carried by the verb itself... the time word only says
when, it does not do the tense," and "Same *-nu* ending as the past tense, doing the same
job," which correctly identifies *-nu* as the person marker rather than the tense marker.
That's right and it's well put. The closest thing to the old bug is the tutor saying
*chustanu* covers see/look "with a slight nuance in meaning based on context" — but
that's about the verb's meaning, not about an ending, and it isn't wrong.

One genuinely confusing line, in L15: "*Enduku* usually opens the question in speech,
though it can sit at the end too — *adhi enduku* is the everyday form." It tells me the
usual position is the front and then gives the everyday example with it at the back. I
couldn't tell from that which one to use, and I guessed.

### 8. Typos

Four deliberate slips, all handled well:

| typed | target | slip type | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Thinnaanu* | Thinnanu | vowel length | accepted, "Note the spelling: **Thinnanu**" |
| *Thinanu* | Thinnanu | dropped double consonant | accepted, **no spelling note** |
| *Chusthanu* | Chustanu | spurious aspiration | accepted, "Note the spelling: **Chustanu**" |
| *Anduke* | Andhuke | dropped aspiration | accepted, "Note the spelling: **Andhuke**" |

Correct spelling always shown, always the right word, and never once "corrected" a word
I'd spelled right — including all five turns where I spelled it right and attached an
English question. The one inconsistency: *Thinanu* got no spelling note while three
comparable slips did. It was in the recall drill rather than the teach phase, so the
notes may only fire during teaching — but from the learner's side it just looks like the
app sometimes notices and sometimes doesn't.

Worth saying: *Andhuke* is a hint problem more than a typo. The hint reads
`uhn-du-kay` — no aspirated *dh*, and it starts *uh*, not *an*. Working purely from that
hint you would write *Unduke* or *Anduke*. I wrote *Anduke*. Same for *Chustanu*, whose
hint `choos-taa-nu` sits next to *Velthanu*'s `vehl-thaa-nu`, so guessing *th* is the
natural move.

### 9. Stray internal text

No `null`, `undefined`, `{w}`, `[not simulated]`, or truncated lines anywhere. Two real
glitches:

- **The same grammar note printed twice in one turn** (L15, "I don't like it"): the
  *Ledu* note appears as two identical consecutive `System: 💡` lines.
- **Wrong / stale hints.** Three times a `System: 💡` hint fired with content for the
  wrong step. L13: I was on "I saw" (*Chusanu*) and the hint said "'I did' is Chesanu" —
  step 1's content. Same stale hint again later during "I went there", where it's
  irrelevant. L15: I answered *Ledu* correctly and got the hint "'No' is Ledu" — the
  answer to the question I'd just got right.
- **Note repetition generally.** The *tharuvatha* note fired four times in lesson 14,
  identical each time, and only the last was relevant. The *ledu* note three times, the
  *enduku/andhuke* note twice. By the third repeat I'd stopped reading them, which is
  bad, because the notes are the most valuable part of these lessons.

### 10. Two tasks in one turn, or praise on a turn I never took?

**Praise on a turn I never took — twice, and it's a real hole.**

L13, step 4. I asked a question and deliberately did not answer ("Hang on, before I
answer — ..."). Response: `System: 💡 "I did" is Chesanu.` then `Tutor: Good.` and on to
step 5. `--status` confirmed step 5 of 15, 5 words taught. **I never said *chusanu* and
it was marked done.**

L14, drill. I got *tintanu* wrong, then asked "what did I get wrong there?" without
answering. Response: the *tharuvatha* note, then `🎓 Review passed`. **A question passed
the review stage of the future tense.**

So a learner who asks instead of answering can be advanced through the material. It's
inconsistent too — the *same* behaviour got me marked *wrong* twice in the L13
conversation stage (my "is there a rule?" question, and my honest "I've never been taught
how to say 'you' as an object"). Standalone non-answers are sometimes correct, sometimes
wrong, apparently depending on which stage you're in.

**Two tasks in one turn:** once, the L13 "and" turn, where "Now you can try saying: 'I
went and I ate.'" and "Final check: 'I went and I ate'." both land as separate tutor
lines. Not confusing so much as visibly duplicated.

**One duplicated task across turns:** L14 asked "Say 'I will go tomorrow'" twice, as
sentence 1 and sentence 3, with "I will eat now" sandwiched between. Same prompt, same
answer, ten steps into a fifteen-step lesson that only taught four new verbs.

## How it felt

Lesson 15 felt like learning. The notes turned up at the moment I needed them, one of
them answered a question I was in the middle of asking, and I finished able to build
*naaku ishtam ledu* and *adhi enduku kaavali?* myself.

Lessons 13 and 14 felt like flashcards with a tense-shaped label on the box. I finished
both able to say four things in the past and four in the future, and completely unable to
say a fifth. Every time I reached for the rule — three times, in three different ways —
nothing came back. And the words I was given collide with words I already had
(*velthanu* / *velthunnanu*) with no warning at all, so I'm now less confident about
lesson 11 than I was before I started lesson 14.

The thing I'd fix first: tell me the pattern. You already own the perfect table — the
same four verbs appear in past, future, and present continuous across lessons 11, 13, 14.
Put them side by side once, say "the *t* is the future, the *-nu* is the *I*, and the
stem changes are irregular so here they are," and I'd walk out able to do something. As
it stands I did that comparison myself, after the lessons ended, and the app never
mentioned it.
