# Playtest — learner F5 — Telugu lessons 27, 28, 30

I came in off lessons 1–26. English only, no Telugu outside the notebook. Sessions `r5f27`, `r5f28`, `r5f30`. All three ran the full 15 steps and completed. Full transcripts at the bottom.

Short version: lesson 27 fails at the one job its title promises, lesson 28 is the best of the three and genuinely useful, and lesson 30 is not a finale. It's lesson 31 with a smaller word list.

---

## How it actually went

### Lesson 27 — Money

Six words: Dabbulu, Vanda, Veyi, Laksha, Chillar, Rupayalu. The one grammar note — "rupees goes AFTER the number, *vanda rupayalu*" — arrived before I needed it and landed cleanly. That's the good part.

Then it asked me to say "I have money." I have `Naaku = to me / I have` sitting in my notebook from lesson 5, in those exact words. So I wrote **Naaku dabbulu undi**. Marked wrong. The hint came back: "My near + money." The answer it wanted was **Naa daggara dabbulu** — an idiom I have never seen, built by gluing `Naa` (my, L5) to `Daggara` (near, L21) in a possessive sense nobody ever mentioned. So the course taught me one way to say "I have", then failed me for using it and demanded a construction it never taught. That's the single worst moment across all three lessons, because it punishes me for having done the homework.

Once I had the pattern I reused it fine — **Naa daggara chillar ledu** for "I don't have change" worked first try, and that felt good.

Then step 11: **"Ask the price of something in thousands."** I cannot do this. There is no word for "price" and no word for "how much" anywhere in 27 lessons of notes, and lesson 27 doesn't teach one. I said so in plain English — literally "I can't do this one, I've never been taught a word for price or for how much, can you teach me the word for how much?" — and the tutor replied **"Exactly!"** and moved me on. It printed the rupees grammar note as if that were a response, awarded the step, and never answered the question.

So I passed the "ask a price" drill without producing a single Telugu word, and I finished the money lesson unable to ask a price. Same story two steps later: "That costs one lakh." No word for "costs" was ever taught. I wrote **Adhi oka laksha** ("that one lakh"), asked whether there's a verb for "costs", got "Perfect!" and no answer.

When I asked once more after the lesson closed, I got "That's the scenario done — nothing left to answer here."

**Can I ask a price and understand the answer?** Half. If a shopkeeper says "veyi rupayalu" I understand it. I have no way to make him say it.

### Lesson 28 — Weather & travel

This one works. Six words: Enda, Prayanam, Ticketu, Bus, Vana, Paduthundi. The grammar note — "Telugu does not say *it is raining*, it says the rain is falling: *vana paduthundi*" — is the best thing in all three lessons. It arrived before the drill, it explained a real difference from English, and I remembered it.

Where I got stuck: "It's very hot today." I reached for `Vedi = hot` from lesson 9, because the prompt says "hot". **Eeroju chala vedi undi** — wrong. The hint then told me the shape: "Today + very + heat", i.e. **Eeroju chala enda**, with no verb on the end at all. Two things arrived only in that hint: that this sentence uses the noun *enda* rather than the adjective *vedi*, and that Telugu drops the copula here. Neither was stated before I lost the point. A learner working honestly from lesson 9 will fail that step every time.

Also got dinged in the review for answering **Paduthunnanu** instead of **Paduthundi** — I built it by analogy with `chestunnanu` / `velthunnanu` from lesson 11, which is exactly what those lessons trained me to do. I asked, in plain English, why it's `-thundi` here and not `-thunnanu`. Completely ignored; it just re-asked the question. That -undi/-anu distinction (it vs. I) is the most useful grammar fact in the lesson and it never got said out loud.

The nice surprise: "Safe trip." No word for "safe" anywhere. I said so and offered **manchi prayanam** as a flagged guess off `Manchi = good`, and this time the tutor actually replied — "'Manchi prayanam' means 'safe trip' using the word for 'good'" — and gave me the step. Good handling, though it's still a drill whose answer was never taught, and "manchi prayanam" doesn't mean "safe trip", it means "good trip".

**Can I say what the weather is doing?** Yes, and I could do it cold, unprompted: *vana paduthundi*, *eeroju chala enda*. That's a real win. It's a narrow one — no cold, no wind, no clouds, no "it's cold today" — but it's two sentences I'd actually use.

### Lesson 30 — the "Final Capstone"

Five words: Perigaanu, Nundi, Santhoshamga, Telugu, Aakhari. That is fewer new words than either of the other two lessons, and two of them barely count — `Telugu` is `Telugu`, and `Santhoshamga` is already sitting in my notebook from lesson 26.

What it does right is the sentence half. It made me stack things from all over the course, and that felt good: `matladagalanu` (L22), `namaskaram` (L1), `idhi` / `naa` (L2, L5), `unnanu` / `ippudu` (L11), `chala` (L8), `eeroju` (L12), `akkada` (L3), `baga` (L16). "Nenu Telugu santhoshamga matladagalanu" and "Nenu Hyderabad nundi, nenu akkada perigaanu" are the longest things I've built and I built them without hints. That's genuine integration and the other two lessons don't do it.

But as a **finale** it doesn't land, for four reasons.

1. **I never had to understand anything.** Fifteen steps and every single one is "say this English sentence in Telugu." Miko never speaks a word of Telugu at me and never asks me a question I have to comprehend and answer. It's a recital, not a conversation. That's true of all 30 lessons, and the capstone was the last chance to fix it.
2. **It doesn't pull the course together, it pulls the grammar together.** Nothing from money. Nothing from weather or travel, which I'd literally just done. Nothing from family, food, colours, feelings, the "if" clause, the "must" suffix. A capstone that had me haggle a price, ask about the weather, and introduce myself would have been the payoff. Instead it's a monologue about growing up in Hyderabad.
3. **There is no ending.** The last thing that happens is the same `✨ Scenario complete.` stamp and word list that lesson 3 gives you. No "you finished the course," no recap of the 30 lessons, no send-off. I had to ask "That's it? That was the final lesson, right?" to get any acknowledgement at all, and what I got was "You've learned some useful phrases. Keep practicing!" — immediately followed by a second, contradictory line saying there was nothing left to answer.
4. **Its own closing line isn't true yet.** It ends on "Now, I can speak Telugu nicely!" I can't ask what something costs. I can't understand a reply. I can't ask anyone a question except "where is X". Saying that sentence out loud felt hollow, and I noticed it in the moment.

**Verdict: it's five words in a slightly longer coat.** The sentence-building is the best in the three lessons and should be kept. But nothing about it feels like a finish, and after 30 lessons I do not feel I could hold a short conversation — I feel I could deliver a short speech, as long as nobody replies.

## The ten questions

**1. Lesson 30 as a finale.** No. See above. Real integration of grammar across lessons — the best sentence work I've done — but zero comprehension, zero callback to the actual scenario content (money, weather, family, food), fewest new words of the three lessons, and literally no ending beyond the standard completion stamp. It reads as a grammar review that happens to be numbered 30.

**2. Was every word a drill asked for actually taught?** No, six times.
- L27 "I have money" needs **naa daggara** as a possessive — never taught, and it contradicts `Naaku = I have` from L5.
- L27 "Ask the price of something in thousands" — no word for *price* or *how much* exists anywhere in the course.
- L27 "That costs one lakh" — no word for *costs*.
- L28 "Safe trip" — no word for *safe* (the tutor accepted `manchi` = good and called it "safe").
- L28 "It's very hot today" wants `enda` (taught as the noun "Heat/Sun") used predicatively with no verb — that use wasn't taught, and `Vedi = hot` from L9 is the obvious wrong path.
- L30 "Hello, I am Ravi" wants **nenu Ravi**; the verbless "I am [name]" pattern was never taught (L2 gave me `peru` = name, so I said `naa peru Ravi`).

Changed forms specifically: `Paduthundi` vs the `-unnanu` forms from L11 is a changed form of exactly the kind that trips people, and the it/I distinction behind it is never stated.

Two false "spelling" corrections in L30, both on sentences where **every word I typed was spelled correctly**:

Both were marked correct, so no credit lost, but being told to check my spelling when my spelling was perfect is confusing and made me distrust the other spelling notes.

**9. Stray internal text.** No `null`, `undefined`, `{w}`, or `[not simulated]`. Four smaller things:
- **Merged pronunciation hints, twice.** L27: `[say it like: Chillar — chihl-luh-ruh, Rupayalu — roo-paa-yuh-lu]`. L28: `[say it like: Vana — vaa-nuh, Paduthundi — puh-du-thun-dih]`. Two words crammed into one hint bracket while only the second is actually drilled. It reads like two steps collapsed into one, and I nearly answered with the wrong word both times.
- **Broken sentence join.** "So — Say 'I have money'." — capital S mid-sentence after a dash. Happens whenever the grader has nothing to say.
- **Lost feedback.** After `Chilar ivvandi` I got no verdict word at all — the `🎓 Phrases done` banner appeared and the next prompt followed. Same after `Idhi naa aakhari`. Whenever a phase boundary lands, the confirmation for the answer that triggered it vanishes.
- **Doubled, contradictory closing lines.** L30's last exchange returned two Tutor lines back to back: "Yes, that was the final lesson of the course... Keep practicing!" then "That's the scenario done — nothing left to answer here."
- Also: the same 💡 rain note was replayed verbatim twice in L28, the second time in answer to a question it had nothing to do with.

**10. Did the scenarios do their jobs?**
- **L27 (money): no.** I can say money, hundred, thousand, lakh, change, rupees, "hundred rupees", "I have money", "I don't have change", "where is the money". I **cannot ask a price** — there is no "how much" in the course — which is the entire point of a money lesson, and the lesson's own step 11 asks me to do it and then passes me for saying I can't. I can partly understand an answer, since I can parse a number plus *rupayalu*. Add a word for "how much" and this lesson goes from broken to good.
- **L28 (weather): yes.** *Vana paduthundi* and *eeroju chala enda* both came out unprompted and I'd use them tomorrow. Travel side is thin but functional: *bus ticketu kaavali*, *bus ekkada undi?*, *repu prayanam velthunnanu*. Nothing for cold, wind, or cloud, so I can describe two kinds of weather and no others.

---

## What I'd fix, in order

1. Teach a word for "how much" / "price" in lesson 27. Without it the lesson doesn't function.
2. Stop grading plain-English "I don't know this" as a correct answer. It's the one thing a learner should be able to say honestly, and being told "Exactly!" for it destroys trust in every other "Correct" I got.
3. Reconcile `naaku` and `naa daggara` before the drill, not in the hint after I've failed. Same for `enda` vs `vedi` and the no-verb rule.
4. Say the `-thundi` (it) vs `-thunnanu` (I) rule out loud somewhere. It's one sentence and it's the most useful thing lesson 28 could give me.
5. Answer bundled questions, or say "not now" — silence reads as the tutor not listening. Replaying an unrelated canned note is worse than silence.
6. Give lesson 30 an actual ending, some comprehension steps where Miko speaks Telugu and I answer, and at least one callback each to money, weather, and family.
7. Don't call a wording change a spelling note, and don't delete `mariyu` without explaining why.
