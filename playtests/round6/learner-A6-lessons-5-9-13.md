# Playtest — Learner A6, Telugu lessons 5, 9, 13

Sessions: `r6a5`, `r6a9`, `r6a13`. All three finished, 15/15 steps, no loops, no repeated-reply stalls.
I came in with lessons 1–12 in my notebook and no other Telugu.

---

## How it actually went

**Lesson 5 (Possession)** was the smoothest of the three. Six words — `naa`, `naaku`, `mee`,
`ayana`, `ame`, `katha` — and the `naaku ... undi` "have" trick genuinely landed. By the end I could
say `naaku katha undi` without thinking about it, and I understood *why* it works, which is more than
I usually get. The best moment in all three lessons was here: I asked why `undi` sits at the end and
Miko actually explained it, then a note followed that spelled out `naa katha` vs `naaku katha undi`
as "owning versus there-being". That one sentence taught me more than the drills did.

**Lesson 9 (Adjectives)** was easy but felt thinner. `pedda`, `chinna`, `manchi`, `chedu`, `vedi`,
`bagundi`. Adjective-before-noun is dead simple and I can use it unprompted — `pedda illu`,
`manchi snehithudu`, `vedi neeru`. Two things annoyed me. First, `vedi` ("hot") was introduced in a
side-note and then immediately quizzed — I was asked "What's the Telugu word for Hot?" having never
once been asked to say it. Second, one of the prompts handed me the answer: *"Ask for 'Hot water'
(Vedi neeru kaavali)."* That's not a test, that's a read-aloud.

**Lesson 13 (Past tense)** was the most interesting and the most frustrating. It opened with an
honest, genuinely useful warning that I want to praise: the past is stem + `-anu`, **but the stem
changes unpredictably** and *"there is no rule that gets you from one to the other."* Being told
straight that a pattern doesn't generalise is rare and I appreciated it. The frustration was the last
third: the lesson asked me for two things it had never taught me, and only one of them got resolved.

### Where I got stuck

1. **"Confirm you saw Miko."** I know `chusanu` = "I saw". I have never been given any way to say
   "you" as the *object* of a verb. I said so in plain English and asked for help — Miko just
   repeated the prompt verbatim, no help at all. I guessed `Chusanu`, got marked wrong, and *then*
   got the hint "I + name + saw". So the structure I was being graded on only appeared after I'd
   already lost the point. `Miko chusanu` passed.
2. **"'I went and I ate.'"** There is no word for "and" anywhere in my notebook. I said so. Miko
   gave me `mariyu` — good — but the note said *"Mariyu from lesson 10"*, and my lesson-10 notes are
   `bhojanam, billu, ivvandi, dhanyavaadaalu, kurchondi`. **`Mariyu` is not in lesson 10.** The
   course told me I already knew a word it had never taught me.

## The twelve checks

**1. Were your questions answered?**
Mostly no — **3 of 13** got a real answer (Q4, Q8, Q13), plus one half (Q2). Eight were silently
dropped. The pattern is stark: a question bundled onto a *correct* answer is almost always swallowed,
because the grader just moves on. E.g. I typed *"Naa peru. Quick question though — does the word
order ever flip, like peru naa?"* and got back only `Perfect! Say 'Your book'.` Same with the `undi`
contradiction, the plural-adjective question, the `chesaru` question, and the new-verb question. The
three that worked were asked either after a wrong answer or as an outright refusal to answer, which
suggests only the "you didn't produce an answer" branch has a question handler.
Slightly better than the 2/10 and 2/9 earlier testers saw, but not by much.

**2. Was a plain-English "I don't know" ever graded as CORRECT?**
**No — this is fixed.** I tested it twice. L9: *"I honestly don't know how to say that, sorry."* →
`Tutor: Vedi neeru kaavali. 🐾` / `Tutor: So — Ask for 'Hot water' ...` — it supplied the answer and
re-asked, no credit. L13: *"I don't know this one — you've never given me a word for 'and'..."* →
`Nenu vellanu mariyu thinnanu. (I went and I ate.)` then `So — Final check: 'I went and I ate'.` Both
times it re-prompted and made me actually produce it. No "Exactly!", no advance.

**3. Lesson 5's "introduce yourself" — did it demand a name you were never taught?**
**No — I used my own name and it was accepted.** `Introduce yourself — greet Miko and tell her your
name.` → *"Namaskaram! Naa peru Farhaan."* → `Exactly!` Good.
But the follow-up was broken: `Note the spelling: **Namaskaram, naa peru [name]**` — a literal
`[name]` placeholder leaked into the feedback, on an answer that had no spelling error at all.

**4. Is `naaku` actually drilled in lesson 5, or only mentioned in a note?**
**Genuinely drilled.** It's taught as its own vocabulary item with its own "say **Naaku**" prompt, it
gets its own memory-check-adjacent explanation, and crucially there's a production task that can't be
done without it: `Say 'I have a story'.` → `Naaku katha undi` → accepted, followed by the
`naa` vs `naaku` note. That's real practice, not a footnote. This one is in good shape.

**5. Lesson 13 — could you put a NEW verb into the past tense, and were you told honestly whether
that's possible?**
**No, I can't — and yes, it told me straight, up front.** Word two of the lesson:
*"One warning worth having early: the STEM changes unpredictably. Vellu ("go") gives vellanu, but
"eat" gives thinnanu and "see" gives chusanu, and there is no rule that gets you from one to the
other. Learn each verb's stem as its own word; the ENDING is what transfers."*
That's exactly the honest framing this needs, and it's stated before I could form a wrong
expectation. I tested it anyway by asking how to say "I drank water" and guessing `neeru thaaganu`
out loud — the guess was **ignored**, neither confirmed nor corrected, which is a missed teaching
moment but not a lie. Verdict: honest content, deaf follow-up.

**6. Lesson 9 — did anything contradict itself about `undi` / "no word for is"?**
**On the surface, yes — and my direct challenge went unanswered.** Lesson 5 note:
*"Telugu needs no word for "is" here — naa peru Ravi is literally "my name Ravi""*, with `undi`
glossed as "there is". Lesson 9 then introduces *"Bagundi is "it is good" — baga ("well") joined onto
undi ("it is")"* — `undi` relabelled as "it is". I said so explicitly: *"earlier I was told Telugu has
no word for 'is', but you just said undi means 'it is'. Which is it?"* → **ignored**, and the note
that fired was a verbatim repeat of the one that caused the confusion.
Two turns later, unprompted, a note resolved it properly: *"undi says a thing EXISTS or is somewhere
(illu akkada undi) ... But pinning a plain description onto a noun needs no verb at all, which is why
adhi chedu is already a complete sentence."* That's the right explanation. It just arrived late, by
coincidence, and not because I asked. A learner who didn't happen to reach that step would be left
with a flat contradiction.

**7. Did any rule you were graded on arrive only AFTER you'd been marked wrong?**
**Yes, clearly once.** `Confirm you saw Miko.` gave no structure at all. I explained I had no way to
say "you" as an object; it repeated the prompt. I answered `Chusanu` and was marked
`Not quite. Hint: I + name + saw.` — the required structure (use the name in the object slot) was
withheld until after the wrong mark.
A softer case: lesson 5 quizzed `He (respectful) / his` and only on the *next* turn produced
*"Ayana is the respectful form, covering both "he" and "his""*. And in L13 my question about `-anu`
for other subjects was ignored, with `chesaru` / `chesadu` appearing three turns later on schedule.

**8. Was a correct answer ever marked wrong — with an English question attached, or with an optional
pronoun dropped?**
**No. Both cases passed.** Question attached: *"Ayana - wait, if naaku is 'to me', how do I say 'you
have a book'?..."* → graded correct (`Tutor: Ayana.` then advance). Also *"Adhi chusanu. Is 'chesaru'
also the polite way...?"* → `Correct!`. Dropped `nenu`: `Intiki vellanu` for "I went home" → `Perfect!`,
and `Miko chusanu` for "I saw Miko" → `Perfect!`. Grading itself is fair.
The one wrong-mark on a near-miss (`Me` for `Mee`) is defensible in isolation but **inconsistent**
with `Thinnaanu` being forgiven in lesson 13 — the same vowel-length error, opposite outcomes.

**9. "Note the spelling" — did it fire on a correctly spelled word, or label a grammar change as
spelling?**
**Yes, it fired on a fully correct answer once.** *"Namaskaram! Naa peru Farhaan."* → `Exactly! Note
the spelling: **Namaskaram, naa peru [name]**.` Nothing was misspelled; the note is really a template
reminder wearing a spelling label, and it exposed the raw `[name]` slot.
The other three fires were legitimate (`Chedhu`, `Thinnaanu`, `pustakam` — all real slips) and the
"accept but correct" behaviour there is good. No `pani`→`paniki` style grammar-as-spelling case came
up this round, since neither lesson used a case suffix change.

**10. The answer immediately before a `🎓` banner — did it get feedback, or was it swallowed?**
**Swallowed every single time — 10 for 10.** Not one answer before a banner got a verdict:
- L5 `Katha` → straight to `🎓 Vocabulary done`; `Ayana` → `🎓 Review passed`; `Ayana katha` →
  `🎓 Phrases done`; `Ame peru` → `✨ Scenario complete.`
- L9 `Bagundi.…` → `🎓 Vocabulary done`; `Chedu` → `🎓 Review passed`; `Vedi neeru` →
  `🎓 Phrases done`; `Adhi chedu` → `✨ Scenario complete.`
- L13 `Cheppanu` → `🎓 Vocabulary done`; `Thinnanu` → `🎓 Review passed`; `Annam thinnanu` →
  `🎓 Phrases done`; `Nenu vellanu mariyu thinnanu` → `✨ Scenario complete.`
As a learner this reads as "that one didn't count". The last thing I said in each lesson — the
capstone sentence — got no acknowledgement whatsoever.

**11. Stray internal text?**
No `null`, `undefined`, `{w}` or `[not simulated]`. But four other kinds of leakage:
- **Unfilled placeholder:** `Note the spelling: **Namaskaram, naa peru [name]**`.
- **Merged pronunciation blocks / two words in one breath:** L5 `"Her" is **Ame**. "Story" is
  **Katha**. Your turn — say **Katha**` with a doubled hint block (`say it like: Ame = uh-meh` /
  `say it like: Katha = kuh-thuh`). Same shape in L9 with `Vedi` and `Bagundi`. In both cases only
  the second word is drilled and the first is later quizzed cold.
- **Same note repeated verbatim in consecutive turns, three times:** the `Bagundi`/`baga`+`undi` note
  (L9), the `Vellanu`/unpredictable-stem note (L13), and the opening `Chesanu`/stem+`-anu` note which
  re-fired several turns into L13 as if new.
- **A bare echo as its own line:** `Tutor: Ayana.` — the grader repeating my word back with no
  verdict, before the next teaching turn.
Plus a factual leak: *"Mariyu from lesson 10"* when lesson 10 contains no `mariyu`.

**12. Two different tasks in one turn, or praise landing on a turn you never took?**
No praise landed on a turn I didn't take, and no turn asked me to produce two separate things.
Two near-misses worth flagging: the double-word introductions in L5 and L9 (two new words, one
drill) function as one-and-a-half tasks; and in L5 when I deliberately asked a question *instead of*
answering, the reply was `Here it is again.` — the wrong-answer wording, for a turn where I never
attempted an answer. Mislabelled, not mis-graded.

---

## Summary of what I'd fix, in priority order
1. **Answer questions bundled onto correct answers.** 8 of my 13 questions evaporated; that's the
   whole experience of talking to this thing.
2. **Give feedback on the answer before a `🎓`/`✨` banner.** 10/10 swallowed, including every
   lesson's final sentence.
3. **Lesson 13's "confirm you saw Miko" and "I went and I ate" ask for grammar and vocabulary
   never taught** — the object pronoun, and `mariyu` (wrongly credited to lesson 10).
4. **Stop repeating a note verbatim one turn after it was first shown** (3 occurrences).
5. **Make near-miss tolerance consistent** — `Me`/`Mee` wrong but `Thinnaanu`/`Thinnanu` fine.
6. **`Note the spelling` shouldn't fire on correct answers**, and shouldn't leak `[name]`.
7. **Don't quiz a word that was never drilled** (`Vedi`), or a label that was never given
   ("respectful"), and **don't put the answer in the prompt** (`Ask for 'Hot water' (Vedi neeru kaavali)`).
8. **The `undi` "is"/"it is"/"there is" story needs one consistent wording across lessons 5 and 9.**

Good, keep it: the honest "no rule gets you from one stem to another" warning; the `naa` vs `naaku`
owning-vs-there-being note; letting me use my own name; refusing to invent a word for "snow"; and
never grading "I don't know" as correct.
