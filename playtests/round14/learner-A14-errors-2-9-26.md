## 8. Does it feel like it is trying to teach you, or a quiz that moves on? — Blunt

**Mostly teaching, with a quiz's reflexes.** The word-level feedback is genuinely good — better than any tutor I've been through. Being told *"adhi means 'That' — here you want 'This'"* is exactly the sentence a beginner needs: it names what came out of my mouth, not just what should have. Same for "the word for Name is missing" and "all the right words — the order is not." Three turns in I could feel the Idhi/Adhi/Ee triangle taking shape.

But it stops being a teacher the moment the error is inside a word rather than between words. Every ending mistake collapses into one line — *"the ending is the wrong one for who you mean"* — fired whether the person was wrong (`unnanu`), right (`unnaru`), or irrelevant. Twice it told me something false about my own sentence, and once the follow-up hint restated my wrong answer back to me as the target. That is the failure mode of a quiz: it knows the string didn't match, it doesn't know why, so it says something person-shaped and moves on.

And the grader is loose enough that being wrong sometimes isn't wrong. I asked for hot water by saying *"hot water, don't want"* and got "Great job!". I dropped "very" from "I am very tired today" and got "Perfect!". I kept the -ga in a negation the lesson had just told me drops it, and got "Spot on!". A learner who says the opposite of what was asked and is congratulated has learned something false.

Verdict: **a teacher on vocabulary, a quiz on grammar.**

## 1. Did an explanation I HEARD let me fix the same mistake next time?

**Yes, twice, clearly.**

- **L9.** I said `Vedi, bagunnanu` for "hot / it is good". Reply: *"bagunnanu means 'I'm fine' — here you want the word for 'It is good'."* Four turns later the memory check asked "What's the word for 'It is good'?" and I answered `Bagundi` first try, without hesitation. The correction is the only reason I knew.
- **L2.** `Idhi pusthakam` for "This book" → *"idhi means 'This' — here you want 'This (before a noun)'."* I got `Ee pusthakam` immediately after, and the idhi/ee split stuck for the rest of the lesson.

**And once it clearly did not.** In L26 I was told twice that my ending was "wrong for who you mean" when the person was correct. I could not fix it from that, because it wasn't describing my mistake. I only got `unnara` right by guessing the -a from the original card, not from the feedback.

## 2. Was anything I was told about a word FALSE?

**Yes — three things.**

1. **L9, biggest one.** I asked whether `manchi` and `bagunnanu` were related. Answer: *"'Manchi' means 'Good,' while 'Bagunnanu' is more like 'It is good.'"* That is wrong. `Bagunnanu` is "I'm fine" — it says so in my own notebook — and "it is good" is `Bagundi`, which the lesson introduced *two cards later*. When I pushed back ("my notes say bagunnanu = I'm fine, is that wrong?") it refused to engage: *"That word has come up already — take another look at your notes above."* The lesson's own card-level feedback later contradicted the tutor's answer. A learner who trusted the Q&A would carry a wrong gloss into the next lesson.
2. **L26 ×2.** "The ending is the wrong one for who you mean" said about `unnaru` and about `meeru … unnaru`, where the person was right both times. Rows 12 and 13 above.
3. **L2, false claim about my notes.** Asked what `pusthakam` meant, the tutor said *"That word has come up already — take another look at your notes above."* It had not. It had appeared once, seconds earlier, inside a spoken grammar aside, and it was not in my notebook. Same brush-off fired again in L9 for a question that contained no vocabulary at all.

## 3. `skip` — three tries

Tried four times. It works every time, and cleanly: *"No problem — it's **Pusthakam**. That one will come back later."* Gives me the answer, promises a revisit, moves on. Good.

**But: skipping the last card of a section swallows the section-change announcement.**

- L2, skipped the *first* memory-check card → the *"Review passed — now let's build whole sentences"* announcement still fired at the end of the section. Correct.
- L9, skipped a mid-section phrase card → *"Phrases done — now real conversation"* still fired. Correct.
- L26, skipped the **last** memory-check card ("Anger") → **no announcement at all.** It went straight from "No problem — it's Kopam" to "Say 'I am tired'." I had no idea I'd crossed from drilling words into building sentences. In the other two lessons that transition is announced out loud; here it vanished.

## 4. Numbers in the audio

**"6 words" — present, all three lessons.** Spoken as *"Vocabulary done — 6 words. Quick memory check next."* Clear, no dropout.

**"from lesson 10" — never heard once.** No numbered back-reference in any of the three sessions. The closest was L2's *"Say hello (from last lesson)"* — spoken as "from last lesson", vague rather than numbered. If numbered callbacks are meant to be there, they are missing.

## 5. Anything spoken that shouldn't be

Yes, a lot, and it's the roughest edge of the whole experience.

- **`🐾` is read out on nearly every card.** "Hey there! I'm Miko, your friendly guide. **paw prints** 'This' is Idhi." It's in the vocabulary intros, the section intros, the repeat-after-error lines. Constant.
- **Markdown is read out on tutor lines.** `**Idhi**`, `*idhi pusthakam*`, `**Pedda**` — asterisks intact. Note the inconsistency: the `💡`/`🎓`/`✨` panels have a **separate stripped 🔊 Spoken line** where markdown is removed correctly. The plain `🔊 Tutor:` lines have no such cleanup. Two code paths, one of them unfinished.
- **An untaught Telugu sentence spoken at me with no translation.** L2, after a wrong answer: *"Idhi emiti ani cheppandi."* Straight Telugu, `ani` and `cheppandi` both unknown to me, and it arrived **before** the verdict, so my first reaction was that I'd broken something. I had to ask what it meant. (It answered: "please say".)
- **A dangling "This".** L9: *"'Hot' is Vedi. Bagundi is 'it is good' … This is the word for a meal, a day, a film."* Which word? Two were just introduced. By ear, "This" points at nothing.
- **An ellipsis in speech.** L26: *"the frame does not change, only the person in front of it: meeru … unnaru, ayana … unnaru."* A `…` mid-sentence has no spoken form.
- **Run-on grammar walls.** The `Ee` intro is five clauses long and introduces `pusthakam`, `idhi`, `adhi`, `ee` and `aa` in one breath — and it was replayed **verbatim four times** while I got that card wrong. By the fourth I'd stopped listening.
- **Broken English.** "All the right words — the order is not." "you wrote emidi" (in a voice app).

## 6. Verdict → explanation → next instruction — is that the order?

**On error turns: yes, and it's good.** Verdict first, cause second, retry last. Reliable across all three lessons. One exception: the L2 turn where the untranslated Telugu line jumped ahead of the verdict.

**On the final turn of a lesson: it inverts, all three times.**

> 🔊 Spoken: Two words, no verb — ee pusthakam names a thing …
> 🔊 Tutor: **Spot on!**

> 🔊 Spoken: Nothing between the two words — adhi chedu …
> 🔊 Tutor: **Correct! That's the last one.**

> 🔊 Spoken: Unnaru is doing a second job here …
> 🔊 Tutor: **Correct! That's the last one.**

Every lesson ends with a grammar lecture, *then* "Correct!". By ear this is genuinely disorienting: I answer, and instead of hearing whether I got it, I get thirty seconds of explanation. I spent all three of those explanations assuming I'd been marked wrong and only found out at the end that I hadn't. **3 for 3 — it is not a flake, it's the shape of the last turn.** It also happened once mid-lesson (L2, the `Ee pusthakam` turn), so it isn't strictly a lesson-end bug — it fires whenever a card carries a grammar note.

And a question typed while a card is live can be scored as a wrong answer — that happened twice, and it costs you a life on the card for asking.

## 9. What could I say unprompted at the end?

**By ear, confidently:**
- `Namaskaram` — hello.
- `Idhi` / `Adhi` — this / that. Genuinely solid, because I got it wrong in both directions and was told which word I'd said each time.
- `Idhi emiti?` — what is this? The question-word-goes-last note landed.
- `Idhi pusthakam` — this is a book.
- `Pedda illu`, `Vedi neeru`, `Manchi guruvu` — adjective-then-noun, three times over.
- `Adhi chedu` — that is bad. The "no verb needed" note stuck.
- `Naaku bhayam ledu` — I'm not scared. Produced it first try, unprompted.
- `Nenu santhoshamga unnanu` — I'm happy.

**By eye only, shaky:**
- `Ee pusthakam` vs `Idhi pusthakam`. I can recite the rule but I'd hesitate live — it came from a wall of text I heard four times without it clicking.
- `Unnara`. I know it's a question and I know there's an `-a`, but the feedback never once told me that, so I don't trust it.
- `Bagundi` vs `Bagunnanu`. Fixed by the card feedback, then un-fixed by the tutor telling me they were the other way round. I'd guess.
- `Alupuga undi` vs `alupuga unnanu` — two accepted frames, but the error feedback only ever pushed me toward `naaku`, so I'd default there and not know why.

**Would not attempt:** `Kopam` (skipped it in the memory check and it never came back, despite "that one will come back later"), the `-ga`/no-`-ga` negation rule (the grader accepted both, so I learned nothing), `Mee peru emiti` (mentioned as lesson 5, not taught).

---

## Top three fixes

1. **Kill the generic ending message.** *"The ending is the wrong one for who you mean"* was false on 2 of the 3 times it fired. Give it the same shape as the word-level one that works: *"unnaru states — for a question you want unnara."* The word-level template (`X means "…" — here you want "…"`) is right almost every time; the ending path has none of that specificity.
2. **Strip `🐾` and markdown from `🔊 Tutor:` lines.** The `🔊 Spoken:` panels already do this correctly — the same cleanup just isn't applied to the main tutor voice.
3. **Route English questions to the Q&A path, not the grader.** Twice a question was scored as a wrong answer. And the "check your notes" refusal should not fire on a question whose word genuinely isn't in the notes — or, failing that, should not fire at all, since the second ask answers fine anyway.

Two smaller ones: put the verdict **before** the grammar note on end-of-lesson turns, and make the section-change announcement fire even when the last card of a section is skipped.
