## 3. Teaching or a quiz that moves on? (question 8)

**Mostly a quiz — but it teaches at exactly the right moments, and that lifts it.**

The vocabulary and memory-check stages are pure drill: hear word, say word, "Correct." "Right." "Yes."
"Good." Nothing is explained, nothing is connected. If I stopped there I'd call it a flashcard app with
a voice.

The sentence stage is different, and it is genuinely good. Every correct sentence unlocks one explanation
that answers the question I was about to ask. "Two words and no 'I'. *Kaavali* means 'is wanted', so the
thing wanted comes first." "Telugu has postpositions, not prepositions." "*Illu* becomes *inti* before a
postposition — the same reshaping that gave you *intiki* in lesson 3." That last one made a word from
seventeen lessons ago suddenly make sense. That is teaching.

The failure is that the teaching is **one-directional**. It tells me things on its schedule and will not
answer things on mine (six of my eight questions were deflected). And when I get something wrong, the
correction is a single sentence and then straight back to the same prompt — no second angle, no simpler
version. `Naaku badhaga ledu` got me "it should not have the ending sound you used," which by ear is
close to nothing. It never says "*-ga*".

Blunt verdict: **a quiz with unusually good margin notes.** It does not adapt to me, and it does not
converse. But the margin notes are worth the ticket.

had to hit both walls to internalise them. Explanations land when they arrive **after** the pain, not before.

## 8. Anything spoken that shouldn't be (question 4)

- **`…` read aloud.** L26 final explanation: "the frame does not change, only the person in front of it:
  meeru … unnaru, ayana … unnaru." An ellipsis is a silence on the page and a stumble in the ear.
- **Third-person narration inside the voice.** L26: "**Miko says,** 'That's a good question, but remember,
  we haven't covered that yet.'" Miko *is* the voice. It quoted itself.
- **Two verdicts stacked on one turn** (Q5 above): an answer to my question, then a "Not quite" verdict
  on that same question. By ear that is one run-on with two moods.
- **"Scenario"** spoken as the unit name where my notes say "lesson".
- Good news: no markdown, no `🐾`, no emoji leaked into the spoken line — the 🎓/💡/✨/📋 markers stay on
  screen and the spoken version is clean prose. Bold and italics are stripped correctly. No untranslated
  Telugu was left unexplained. No repeated sentences.

`illu lo` and only ever *read* that `inti lo` is what to aim for. It never once came out of the speaker
as something I had to say.

**Couldn't say at all:** anything combining lessons — I can say "I am tired" and "I am in the house" but
never had to build "I am tired at home." Each lesson's five words stay in their own box.
