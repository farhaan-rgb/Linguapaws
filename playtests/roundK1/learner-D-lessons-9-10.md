# Learner D — Kannada, lessons 9 & 10

Adult beginner, native English speaker, no prior Indian-language study. Everything I knew going in
came from my lesson 1–8 notebook. Sessions: `ptD9`, `ptD10`.

---

## Lesson 9 — "Basic Adjectives" (15/15, completed)

### What happened, in order

It opened cold. No hello, no "welcome back", no reference to anything I'd done in eight previous
lessons — straight into:

> 🔊 Tutor: Hey there! I'm Miko, your friendly guide. 🐾 To say "Big", say **Dodda**

Then a very clean five-word drill: Dodda (big), Chikka (small), Olleya (good), Ketta (bad),
Bisi (hot). I fat-fingered one:

> me: `Kettta`
> 🔊 Tutor: Good. Note the spelling: **Ketta**.

That was genuinely nice. It didn't punish me for a typo but it did correct the spelling. Best single
behaviour in either lesson.

Memory check (three questions, backwards through the list) — passed. Then phrases:

> 🔊 Tutor: Say 'a big house'.

I had `mane` from lesson 3 and `dodda` from ten seconds ago, but **nobody had ever told me what order
Kannada puts adjectives in.** I guessed English order and said so out loud:

> me: `Dodda mane (guessing the adjective comes first)`
> 🔊 Tutor: Perfect!

It marked me correct and never acknowledged that I'd flagged a guess. So I learned the word order by
lucky accident, not by being taught it.

`Olleya oota`, `Bisi neeru` — fine. Then conversation:

> 🔊 Tutor: Describe your house as small.

Here's where I got properly stuck. To say "my house is small" I need a word for **is**, and there
isn't one anywhere in lessons 1–8. I wrote what I had, and flagged the gap:

> me: `Nanna mane chikka -- though you never taught me a word for 'is', so I'm guessing`
> 🔊 Tutor: Good question — that one is in your notes already, so have another look there and ask again if it is still unclear.
> 🔊 Tutor: Exactly! Tell Miko the food is good.

**That answer is just false.** It is not in my notes. There is no copula anywhere in lessons 1–8, and
lesson 9 never taught one either. Being told to "have another look" at notes that demonstrably don't
contain the thing is the single most annoying moment of the two lessons — it made me doubt my own
notebook for a minute before I checked and confirmed it isn't there. And then in the same breath it
said "Exactly!" to the sentence I'd just told it I wasn't sure about. So: my real question got a wrong
answer, and my uncertainty got rubber-stamped.

Rest of the lesson: `Oota olleya`, `Idu dodda pustaka`, `Bisi neeru beku`. All accepted first try.

> 📋 **You can now say:** Dodda — Big · Chikka — Small · Olleya — Good · Ketta — Bad · Bisi — Hot

### Lesson 9 gripes

- **Taught in pairs, except when it wasn't.** Big/small, good/bad — then `Bisi` = hot and no cold. It
  set up a pattern in my head and then dropped it. I spent the rest of the lesson waiting for "cold".
- **Zero grammar.** Two different structures went past me — attributive (`dodda mane`) and predicative
  (`oota olleya`) — and the app never once named the difference or explained why "big house" and "the
  food is good" have opposite word orders. I passed both by guessing. A guesser who guessed the other
  way would have been marked wrong with no explanation.
- **No review of anything.** Fifteen steps, and the only lesson-1–8 words that appeared were `mane`,
  `oota`, `neeru`, `pustaka`, `nanna`, `idu` — all as scaffolding for the new adjectives, never as
  things being tested.

## Lesson 10 — "Review & Survival Dialogue" (15/15, completed)

### Does it actually review? No.

Flatly: **lesson 10 is not a review lesson.** It is a sixth-through-tenth new-vocabulary lesson with
the word "Review" in the title. It teaches five brand-new words — Lekka, Kodi, Dhanyavada, Estu,
Kshamisi — and the only "review" is the same three-question memory check on *those five new words*
that lesson 9 also had. Not one thing from lessons 1–8 is tested. No greetings, no numbers, no
plurals, no possessives, no yaaru/yelli/yenu questions, no `beku`/`beda`. Old words appear twice, as
raw material for new sentences (`Pustaka estu?`, `neeru kodi`) — never as the thing under test.

The status line even says it out loud: `lesson: Kannada scenario 10 — Review & Survival Dialogue` /
`words: 5 taught`.

I asked about this in step 1, like anyone would:

> me: `wait, I thought lesson 10 was a review lesson? Is this a new word?`
> 🔊 Tutor: I would rather not answer that with a sentence you have not been taught yet — it will come. Let's stay with what we have covered.
> 🔊 Tutor: Here it is again. 🐾 To say "Bill / account", say **Lekka**

That reply is a non-sequitur. I asked a question *about the lesson*, in English, and got a canned
refusal about not teaching me sentences I haven't reached. It doesn't parse as an answer to anything
I said.

### The rest of it

Vocab drill was smooth. Another typo, handled well again:

> me: `Dhanyavaada`
> 🔊 Tutor: Yes. Note the spelling: **Dhanyavada**.

**Worth saying plainly: "thank you" is taught in the last lesson of the course.** Lesson 1 gives me
hello. I then go through eight more lessons — asking for food, water, books, naming my friends and
my sister — with no way to thank anybody. Sorry/excuse me too. Those are lesson-1 words in every
phrasebook ever printed.

A grammar tip finally showed up, in the wrong place:

> 🔊 Tutor: Ask 'How much?'.
> me: `Estu?`
> 🔊 Tutor: Perfect!
> 💡 That -i ending on *kodi* is the polite command form.

That fired after an answer that has nothing to do with `kodi`, and *before* I'd ever used `kodi` in a
sentence. It's the one piece of real grammar in either lesson and it was bolted onto an unrelated
step. It also doesn't square with lesson 1, where I was given `Iddeera` as the polite form — so the
"-i = polite" rule is presented as if it's the story, when I already have a polite form ending in
something else and nobody has reconciled the two.

**Asked for something never taught:**

> 🔊 Tutor: Ask how much the book costs.

I have no word for "cost", "price", or "rupees". I said so:

> me: `you haven't taught me a word for 'costs' or 'price'`
> 🔊 Tutor: I would rather not answer that with a sentence you have not been taught yet — it will come. Let's stay with what we have covered.
> 🔊 Tutor: So — Ask how much the book costs.

The exact same canned refusal. It told me to stay with what we've covered *while asking me for
something we haven't covered*, and then repeated the demand. I guessed `Pustaka estu?` — noun +
"how much" — and got "Great job!". Correct by luck again.

**Marked wrong when I thought I was right (1):**

> 🔊 Tutor: Thank Miko warmly.
> me: `Dhanyavada`
> 🔊 Tutor: Not quite. Hint: Thanks + name.

"Warmly" does not mean "add the name". Nothing in either lesson taught me that you address someone by
name in Kannada, or where the name goes, or whether it takes an ending. `Dhanyavada Miko` worked, but
the *only* reason I found it is that the hint handed me the answer. If the instruction had said "thank
Miko by name" I'd have got it first try.

**Marked wrong when I thought I was right (2):**

> 🔊 Tutor: Excuse yourself, then ask for water.
> me: `Kshamisi, neeru beku`
> 🔊 Tutor: Not quite. Hint: Sorry + water + give.

This one I'll actually argue about. `Beku` = "want" is from **lesson 4**, and the *final step of
lesson 9*, minutes earlier, was "Ask for hot water" — where `Bisi neeru beku` was marked "Correct!".
So the app taught me `beku` as the way you ask for something, accepted it for that exact task, and
then one lesson later rejected it for the same task in favour of `kodi` — with no explanation
anywhere of the difference between `neeru beku` and `neeru kodi`, or when to use which.
`Kshamisi, neeru kodi` was accepted.

> 📋 **You can now say:** Lekka — Bill / account · Kodi — Give (please) · Dhanyavada — Thank you · Estu — How much · Kshamisi — Sorry / excuse me

### Small inconsistency between the two lessons

In lesson 9 the interstitials were explicitly screen-only:

> 👁  On screen only (NOT spoken): 🎓 **Vocabulary done** — 5 words. Quick memory check next.

In lesson 10 the identical interstitial is both:

> 👁  On screen: 🎓 **Vocabulary done** — 5 words. Quick memory check next.
> 🔊 Spoken:    Vocabulary done — 5 words. Quick memory check next.

And in both lessons the closing summary card is *spoken aloud* with the emoji and the bullet
separators intact — "📋 You can now say: Dodda — Big · Chikka — Small · …". Read out, that's a clipboard
emoji and a string of middle dots. Whatever's deciding spoken-vs-screen is not applying the same rule
across lessons.

## Honest self-assessment

I have now finished what is sold as the complete 10-lesson Kannada course. **No, I could not hold a
real exchange in Kannada.** Not a short one.

What I can genuinely produce unprompted, cold, without a hint:

- `Namaskara` — hello
- `Naanu`, `Neevu`, `Nanna`, `Nimma` — I, you, my, your
- `Chennagiddini` — I am fine
- `Idu` / `Adu` — this / that
- `Yenu` / `Yelli` / `Yaaru` — what / where / who
- `Illi` / `Alli` — here / there
- `Haudu` / `Illa` / `Sari` — yes / no / okay
- `Beku` / `Beda` — want / don't want
- Nouns: `pustaka`, `hesaru`, `mane`, `oota`, `neeru`, `kathey`, `snehita`, `shikshaka`, `akka`, `lekka`
- Numbers 1–5: `ondu, eradu, mooru, naalku, aidu`
- The `-galu` plural
- Adjectives: `dodda, chikka, olleya, ketta, bisi`
- `Kodi`, `Dhanyavada`, `Estu`, `Kshamisi`

And the two or three sentence shapes I can build from those: `Bisi neeru kodi`, `Lekka kodi`,
`Nanna mane chikka`, `Pustaka estu?`, `Kshamisi, neeru kodi`.

What I cannot do, and this is the honest part:

- **I cannot conjugate a single verb.** `Hogu` = "go" is the only verb I was given in eight lessons
  and I have never seen it in a sentence. I cannot say "I am going", "I went", "do you want to go".
- **I have no copula.** I still don't know how to say "is", and when I asked, the app told me to look
  in notes that don't contain it.
- **I cannot answer a question anyone asks me.** I can produce `Yelli?` but if a Kannada speaker says
  anything back to me at conversational speed I have zero comprehension practice — every single step
  in both lessons was me producing, never me understanding.
- **I have no case endings.** "To the house", "with my friend", "of the book" — nothing.
- **I can't count past five**, so `Estu?` is a question I can ask and never understand the answer to.
- I know one greeting and, as of twenty minutes ago, one thank-you.

Realistically what I have is a phrasebook of about 45 words and five memorised sentences. In a
restaurant I could say `Lekka kodi` and `Dhanyavada` and that's a genuinely useful thirty seconds. In
an actual exchange with a person — where they say something back — I'd be lost on their first reply.

The bit that stings is that lesson 10 was my last chance to consolidate, and it spent all fifteen
steps on five new words instead. I finished the course with more vocabulary and no more ability to
use it.
