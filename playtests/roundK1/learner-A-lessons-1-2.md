# Learner A — Kannada, lessons 1 and 2

Adult beginner, native English speaker, zero prior Kannada, zero prior Indian languages.
Sessions: `ptA1` (scenario 1), `ptA2` (scenario 2). Started with an empty notebook.

---

## Lesson 1 — greetings

### How it went, in order

It opened warmly and I liked that:

> 🔊 Tutor: Hey there! I'm Miko, your friendly guide. 🐾 To say "Hello", say **Namaskara**
> say it like: nuh-mus-kaa-ruh

The drill loop for the first six words was genuinely fine. One word at a time, a pronunciation
respelling under each, I repeat it, it says "Correct." / "Right." / "Yes." and moves on. I got
`Namaskara`, `Naanu` (I), `Neevu` (you), `Chennagiddini` (I am fine), and then two at once:

> 🔊 Tutor: “How” is **Hege**. **Iddeera** is “are you”. Your turn — say **Hege**, then **Iddeera**

I mistyped `Chennagidini` (one d) and got a nice, non-punitive correction:

> 🔊 Tutor: Good. Note the spelling: **Chennagiddini**.

That is exactly the right amount of fuss. Good.

The three-question memory check was easy and felt earned. Then sentence building. The first
grammar note landed *after* I got something right, which is a bit late but at least it explained
the thing I had been quietly confused about:

> 💡 *Chennagiddini* is a whole sentence on its own — the -ini ending is the “I”, so there is no
> separate word for “am”, and *naanu* is optional. The matching “you” ending is the -eera you met
> in *iddeera*. That is the whole system: change the ending, change who you mean.

That is a good note. It is the single most useful thing the app told me all session.

### Confusion point 1 — "Are you (polite)"

The memory check asked:

> 🔊 Tutor: Spot on! What's the word for "Are you (polite)"?

But it was taught to me as plain **“are you”**, no politeness marker mentioned:

> **Iddeera** is “are you”

I answered `Iddeera` and it was accepted, so no harm — but I sat there for a second wondering
whether there was a *non*-polite version I had missed. There isn't, as far as I know, because the
app never mentioned one. If politeness matters enough to appear in the quiz prompt and in the
final vocab list ("Iddeera — Are you (polite)"), it should have been said when the word was taught.

### The bad one — I said I didn't know, and got gaslit

> 🔊 Tutor: Correct! Say 'I am fine, and you?'.

Nobody had taught me "and". So I did the honest thing:

> Me: I don't know the word for "and" — you haven't taught me that one. Best I can do is
> Chennagiddini... Neevu?

> 🔊 Tutor: That is the note just above — have another look, and ask again if it is still unclear.
> 🔊 Tutor: Not quite. Hint: I-am-fine + you. Say 'I am fine, and you?'.

**There was no note just above.** I scrolled back through the whole transcript. The two notes I
had at that moment were the `-ini` / `-eera` endings note and nothing else — neither mentions
"and". So the app told me the answer was somewhere I had already been, when it wasn't. That is
worse than just saying "I haven't taught that yet". It made me distrust my own reading of the
screen for a moment.

And then the punchline. I gave the hinted answer:

> Me: Chennagiddini, Neevu?

> 💡 No word for “and” is needed here — Kannada just puts the two halves side by side. *Matte*
> (“and”) exists and is not wrong, but a bare *neevu?* is how this is actually said.

So the app *did* have exactly the explanation I asked for. It was sitting in a note that only
fires after you get the answer right. My honest "I don't know this" was punished with a wrong mark
and a fake pointer, when a one-line "you don't need a word for 'and' here" would have unblocked me
instantly. If the goal is to find out whether the course is teachable: **this specific interaction
is the failure.** The knowledge exists, it is just gated behind already knowing the answer.

### The unfair one — the final check

> 🔊 Tutor: Perfect! Final check: Say hello, I am fine, how are you?

I said what I had been taught, twice over, was correct:

> Me: Namaskara, chennagiddini, hege iddeera?
> 🔊 Tutor: Not quite. Hint: Full intro.

I tried the fused form it had told me about:

> Me: Namaskara, chennagiddini, hegiddeera?
> 🔊 Tutor: Not quite. Hint: Full intro.

Only this passed:

> Me: Namaskara, naanu chennagiddini, neevu hegiddeera?
> 🔊 Tutor: Correct! That's the last one.

This directly contradicts two notes the app itself gave me earlier in the *same lesson*:

- "the -ini ending is the “I”, so there is no separate word for “am”, and ***naanu* is optional**"
- "***Neevu* is optional**: the -eera ending already says “you”"

Then the final graded exercise silently requires both optional words and fails you for leaving
them out. And the hint "Full intro" does not tell you that — "full intro" reads like "say the
whole greeting", which is what I did. I genuinely thought I had a pronunciation/spelling problem
for two attempts. This is the moment I would have closed the app if I weren't being paid to keep
going. Two rounds of "Not quite" with an unchanged, uninformative hint, on an answer the app had
explicitly blessed as correct, is the classic way to make a beginner conclude the language is
arbitrary and they are bad at it.

Also worth noting: the fused/unfused thing. I was told:

> 💡 *Hegiddeera* is the two words you already know ... Kannada fuses them and you will hear it as
> one word, but saying them separately, *hege iddeera*, is equally correct.

"Equally correct" — and then `hege iddeera` was accepted in the roleplay step but I could not tell
whether it was the separated form or the missing pronouns that failed the final check, because
both changed at once. That ambiguity is on the app, not me.

## Lesson 2 — this / that / what

Fresh session, but I still had lesson 1 in my head.

### How it went

Five words, same clean drill loop: `Idu` (this), `Adu` (that — nice touch, "something further
off"), `Yenu` (what), `Pustaka` (book), `Hesaru` (name). Memory check, three for three. No typos
this time.

The sentence building was the best part of either lesson. `Idu yenu?` worked on my first guess
because lesson 1 had already taught me there is no "is", and then:

> 💡 The pointing word comes FIRST, before the thing. The question word *yenu* goes LAST, where
> English puts “what” first.

Clear, short, tells me *why*. `Idu pustaka`, `Adu yenu?` all fell out of that rule. For about six
turns I actually felt like I was learning a language rather than memorising strings.

### Confusion point — "my" appears out of nowhere

> 🔊 Tutor: Great job! Tell Miko 'This is my book' (using Idu pustaka).

"My" has never been taught. Not in lesson 1, not in lesson 2. And look at the prompt: it hands you
the answer in brackets — `(using Idu pustaka)` — which is an admission that the exercise cannot
actually be done. So I asked:

> Me: You've never taught me the word for "my" — I only know Idu and Pustaka. Is it just Idu
> pustaka?

> 💡 “This” is Idu.
> 🔊 Tutor: So — Tell Miko 'This is my book' (using Idu pustaka).

That is not an answer to my question. I asked again, more plainly:

> Me: I know Idu is this. My question was about the word MY. What is the Kannada for my?

> 💡 The pointing word comes FIRST, before the thing. The question word *yenu* goes LAST...

Also not an answer. It fired the word-order note at a question about possessives. It looks like
any question just triggers whatever canned note is nearest, with no attempt to match it to what
was asked.

So I typed the bracketed answer and it passed:

> Me: Idu pustaka
> 🔊 Tutor: Perfect!

Which means **`Idu pustaka` is now officially both "This is a book" and "This is my book."** Those
were two separate accepted exercises in the same lesson, five turns apart, same string. Either the
English prompt is wrong and should say "This is a book", or Kannada really does drop the possessive
here and *that* is the teaching point — in which case say so, because from where I sit it just
looks like the app can't tell the two sentences apart. I still do not know which of those is true.

### The bad one, again — "that one is in your notes already"

> 🔊 Tutor: Ask 'What is your name?'.

"Your" — never taught. I said so:

> Me: no idea, sorry — you haven't taught me the word for "your". I know Neevu means you, and
> hesaru is name, but not "your".

> 🔊 Tutor: Good question — that one is in your notes already, so have another look there and ask
> again if it is still unclear.

It is **not** in my notes. I checked `--status`, which reports `words: 5 taught` — Idu, Adu, Yenu,
Pustaka, Hesaru. No possessive anywhere. This is the same false "check your notes" deflection as
lesson 1, so it isn't a one-off; it's the app's standard reply to "you haven't taught me this."
It is the single most damaging thing in the build, because the two situations it cannot
distinguish are *"the learner is being lazy"* and *"the course has a hole in it"*, and it assumes
the first every time.

I pushed back like a real annoyed person would:

> Me: It is genuinely not in my notes. My notes are: Idu, Adu, Yenu, Pustaka, Hesaru. None of
> those mean "your". Please just tell me the word.

> 🔊 Tutor: So — Ask 'What is your name?'.

Nothing. No note, no hint, no acknowledgement. Just the prompt repeated. Three honest requests for
a word, zero answers.

The only way out was to submit something and be marked wrong:

> Me: Hesaru yenu?
> 🔊 Tutor: Not quite. Hint: Your(Nimma) + name + what.

There it is. **`Nimma`.** The app knew the word the entire time and would only surrender it in
exchange for a failure. I typed `Nimma hesaru yenu?` and finished the lesson.

To be blunt: the fastest strategy in this app is to stop asking questions and start submitting
deliberate rubbish, because wrong answers produce information and honest questions don't. That is
the exact opposite of what you want a beginner to learn.

### Small stuff

- **`Nimma` is not in the final vocab list.** The wrap-up says "You can now say: Idu · Adu · Yenu ·
  Pustaka · Hesaru". The word the app forced me to use to *complete the lesson* isn't there. So my
  notebook is already out of sync with what lesson 3 will presumably assume I know.
- **The two lessons disagree about what gets spoken.** Lesson 1: `👁 On screen only (NOT spoken)`
  for every milestone and note. Lesson 2: the same milestones are `👁 On screen:` **and**
  `🔊 Spoken:`. Same event type, opposite behaviour, and nothing in lesson 2 explains the change.
- **The emoji gets read aloud.** Final line of lesson 2: `🔊 Spoken: 📋 You can now say: ...`.
  The screen version and the spoken version of that same line are byte-identical including the
  clipboard emoji, while every other spoken line has it stripped. Looks like one line missed the
  sanitiser.
- Lesson 1's roleplay prompt "Someone asks 'Neevu hegiddeera?'. Answer 'Naanu chennagiddini'."
  isn't really an exercise — it prints the answer in the prompt. Three of the fifteen steps across
  the two lessons do this.

## Honest self-assessment

### Kannada I could actually produce, unprompted, cold, tomorrow

- **Namaskara** — hello. Solid. Said it four times, it's short, it's stuck.
- **Idu** — this. **Adu** — that. Solid, and the "adu is further away" framing made them stick as a
  pair rather than as two random syllables.
- **Yenu** — what. Solid, and I know it goes at the *end*, which I'd never have guessed.
- **Idu yenu? / Adu yenu?** — what is this / what is that. This I can genuinely use. It's the one
  thing from these two lessons I could walk up to a stranger with.
- **Pustaka** — book. Reasonably solid.
- **Naanu** — I. **Neevu** — you. I can produce these, though I'm now unsure when I'm *supposed*
  to, given the "optional except when it isn't" mess in the final check.

### Kannada I can recognise but not reliably produce

- **Chennagiddini** — I am fine. Five syllables, I typo'd it on first attempt, and if you asked me
  cold tomorrow I'd get maybe "chennagidini" and hope. I know *why* it means what it means, which
  is more than I expected, but the motor memory isn't there off four repetitions.
- **Hege / Iddeera / Hegiddeera** — how / are you / how are you. I can read them. Producing
  `hegiddeera` under pressure is coin-flip, and I still don't know whether the fused or unfused
  form is the one I'm supposed to default to, because the app said "equally correct" and then
  failed me on an answer containing the unfused one.
- **Hesaru** — name. Weak. It got four seconds of my attention in the drill and then only appeared
  again inside a sentence I was fighting with, so it's associated in my head with the frustrating
  bit rather than with "name".
- **Nimma** — your. I only saw it once, in a hint, as `Your(Nimma)` with no space and no
  pronunciation guide. I'd probably recognise it. I would not produce it. And it's not in my
  notebook, so I have no way to revise it.
- **Matte** — and. Mentioned once in a note, immediately undercut by "you don't need it". I will
  not remember this word.

### Did I learn?

Honestly — yes, partly, and less than the fifteen steps of each lesson suggest. The **word order**
teaching in lesson 2 is real learning: pointer first, question word last, no copula. I understood
it, I applied it to a sentence I hadn't seen, and I'd still have it in a week. Same for the `-ini`
/ `-eera` ending idea in lesson 1, which is a genuinely elegant way in.

But of the eleven words, I'd bet money on six. And both lessons ended with me failing an exercise
for a reason the app refused to tell me, then being handed the answer as a punishment. I finished
lesson 2 *less* willing to ask a question than I started it, which for a language app is the worst
possible thing to teach someone. The content is better than the tutor. Somebody has thought hard
about how Kannada works and written good notes about it — and then wired those notes to fire only
at learners who already got it right.
