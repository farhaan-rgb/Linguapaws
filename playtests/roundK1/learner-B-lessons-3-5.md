# Learner B — Kannada lessons 3, 4, 5

Adult beginner, native English speaker, no prior Indian-language study. Coming in with only lesson 1 and 2 in my notebook: Namaskara, Naanu, Neevu, Chennagiddini, Hege, Iddeera, Idu, Adu, Yenu, Pustaka, Hesaru.

Written as I'd tell a friend about it, in order.

---

## Lesson 3 — "The 'Where' & 'Going'"

The first half was honestly lovely. Five words, one at a time, with a pronunciation respelling under each one:

> 🐾 "Where" is **Yelli** — say it like: ehl-lih
> 🐾 To say "Go", say **Hogu** — hoh-gu
> 🐾 To say "Here", say **Illi** — ihl-lih
> 🐾 To say "There", say **Alli** — uhl-lih
> 🐾 To say "Home/House", say **Mane** — muh-neh

I liked that Illi/Alli mirror Idu/Adu — i for near, a for far. Nobody told me that, I spotted it, and it's the single most useful thing I picked up in three lessons. **It should be said out loud.** It's free.

That said, Illi and Alli differ by one vowel and were taught back to back with respellings that look nearly identical (`ihl-lih` / `uhl-lih`). I predicted I'd mix them up and I did, immediately.

### The thing that actually broke my trust (step 7)

Memory check. It asked for "There". I typed the wrong one on purpose-ish — I genuinely wasn't sure:

> **Tutor:** What's the word for "There"?
> **Me:** `Illi`
> **Tutor:** Exactly! Note the spelling: **Alli**.

That is not a spelling note. `Illi` is a different word with the opposite meaning. It told me I was right, and if I hadn't already been suspicious of myself I'd have walked away from that screen believing Illi means "there". This is the worst thing in the whole playtest, because it's a failure that actively teaches you the wrong thing while congratulating you.

I called it out:

> **Me:** `wait, I thought Illi was Here? I just said Illi for There and you said Exactly`
> **Tutor:** I understand the confusion! "Illi" means "Here" and "Alli" means "There".

Which is a fine answer to the question — but it never acknowledged that *it* was the one who got it wrong. "I understand the confusion" reads as "you are confused", when the confusion was manufactured by the app.

For contrast: in lesson 4 I typed `Ila` instead of `Illa` — one missing letter, obviously a typo, obviously the right word — and got a flat **"Not quite!"**. So the fuzzy matching is upside down: it forgives a completely different word and rejects a one-character slip.

### Free-text answers get eaten (step 8)

> **Tutor:** Ask 'Where is the house?'
> **Me:** `guessing here — Mane yelli? you never taught me a word for 'is'`
> **Tutor:** Not quite. Hint: House + where.

My answer *was* house + where. The grader clearly just string-matched the whole line and my English commentary broke it. I retyped bare `Mane yelli` and got **"Perfect!"**. So the lesson I learned is "don't talk to the tutor, just emit tokens", which is exactly the wrong habit for a conversational app. Any time I wanted to say "I'm not sure" or ask a question mid-answer, I had to choose between asking and answering.

Also worth saying: I was never taught that Kannada drops "is". I guessed it from the fact that no copula existed in anything I'd seen. That guess happened to be right, but it was a guess.

### The final check was unfair (steps 14–15)

> **Tutor:** Final Check: Say 'I am going home'.

I have never been shown a word for "going". I have `Hogu`, which the app explicitly told me means **"Go"** (imperative). So I said so:

> **Me:** `you never taught me how to say going, only Hogu which you said was Go`
> **Tutor:** Good question — that one is in your notes already, so have another look there and ask again if it is still unclear.

It isn't in my notes. That's a canned brush-off, and being told to go re-read notes that don't contain the answer is the most patronising moment in three lessons. I pushed back harder:

> **Me:** `it is honestly not in my notes. I have Naanu, Mane, Hogu, Illi, Alli, Yelli. No word for going.`
> **Tutor:** 💡 *Yelli* goes at the end of the sentence rather than the start.

A hint about **Yelli**, on a sentence with no "where" in it. That's a bug — it looks like it fires a canned lesson-level tip whenever it doesn't understand the input. It fired the same Yelli tip earlier too.

Then the grading itself:

> **Me:** `Naanu mane hogu`
> **Tutor:** Not quite. Hint: I + home + go.

The hint is a literal description of what I just typed. I retyped it capitalised, same rejection. I only got through by blindly bolting a suffix onto "mane":

> **Me:** `Naanu manege hogu`
> **Tutor:** Correct! That's the last one.

So the accepted answer contains a dative `-ge` on `mane` that **was never taught, never mentioned, and is not derivable from the hint "I + home + go"**. I passed the final check by brute force, not by knowing anything. If I'd been on my own I'd have quit at that step. And the end-of-lesson summary then cheerfully told me:

> 📋 **You can now say:** Yelli — Where · Hogu — Go · Illi — Here · Alli — There · Mane — Home/House

No mention of `manege`, no mention that I'd just been asked to produce a grammatical form outside that list.

## Lesson 4 — "Desires & Negation"

Much smoother. This was the good lesson.

> Beku = Want · Beda = Don't want · Oota = Food · Neeru = Water · Haudu = Yes · Illa = No/not · Sari = Okay/correct

Two small confusions:

1. **`Neeru` (water) vs `Neevu` (you)** from lesson 1. One letter apart, and nobody flagged it.
2. **`Illa` (no) vs `Illi` (here)** from the lesson I'd just finished. Also one letter apart, also unflagged. By this point my head had `Illi / Alli / Illa` as one undifferentiated soup and I was answering by coin-flip. If the app is going to introduce a new word that collides with last lesson's word, it needs to say "careful, you learned Illi last time — this is Illa".

Pacing wobbled: steps 3 and 5 taught **two** words at once ("Food" + "Water"; "No" + "Okay") while every other step taught one. That felt arbitrary rather than designed.

The negation tip was genuinely good and I used it:

> 💡 *Illa* serves as "no", "not" and "isn't", and it goes at the end.

Sentence building went fine and mostly matched my instincts — `Neeru beku`, `Oota beda`, `Sari, idu beku`, `Haudu, neeru beku` all accepted first try. Being able to reuse `idu` and `pustaka` from lesson 2 felt great; that's the first time the course felt cumulative.

### The one mismatch (step 12)

> **Tutor:** Someone offers you food you don't like. Say **'No, I don't want'**.
> **Me:** `Illa, beda`
> **Tutor:** Not quite. Hint: No + food + don't want.

The prompt says "No, I don't want" — three concepts. The hint says the answer is four concepts including "food", which is not in the English prompt at all. `Illa, oota beda` was accepted. The prompt and the answer key disagree; I answered the prompt and was marked wrong for it.

Also a small nit on `Idu pustaka beku` for "I want this book" — that was accepted, and I'd like to know whether "this book" is really just two nouns stacked, or whether I got lucky again. Nothing told me.

## Lesson 5 — "Possession"

> Nanna = My · Nimma = Your · Avara = His/Her (formal) · Aatana = His · Kathey = Story

### Immediate contradiction in the teaching (steps 3–4)

> **Tutor:** **Avara** is a respectful "his" or "her".
> *(next step)* **Tutor:** To say "His", say **Aatana**.

So two consecutive steps both offer a word for "his" with no contrast drawn between them. Sure enough, two steps later:

> **Tutor:** What's the word for "His"?
> **Me:** `Avara`
> **Tutor:** Not quite!

That is not my mistake. It taught me `Avara` as "his" and then marked `Avara` wrong for "his". The correct answer was `Aatana`, and only *after* that did the prompt start using the disambiguated label:

> **Tutor:** What's the word for "His/Her (formal)"?

The formal/informal distinction only became visible to me by being punished for not knowing it. And the explanatory tip that would have prevented the whole thing arrived **three steps too late**, after the sentence section had started:

> 💡 Kannada makes no gender distinction in the formal form — *avara* covers both "his" and "her".

That tip belongs next to the word, not after the quiz that depends on it. And it still doesn't explain when I'd use `Aatana` over `Avara` — the only thing I know is that one is "respectful" and I have no idea whether that means age, status, strangers, or all three. I finished the lesson genuinely unable to choose between them for a real person.

### The Idu/Adu thing again (step 12)

Same bug as lesson 3, in a nastier form:

> **Tutor:** Identify a book as 'your book'.
> **Me:** `Idu nimma pustaka`
> **Tutor:** Great job! Note the spelling: **Adu nimma pustaka**.

The prompt gave me no this/that cue whatsoever — "identify a book as 'your book'" contains neither. I picked `Idu`. The expected answer was `Adu`, meaning "*That* is your book", and the app framed a this/that meaning change as **"note the spelling"** while telling me I was right. That's the second time it has passed a semantically wrong answer and called the difference spelling.

I challenged it, and got a straight-up self-contradiction:

> **Me:** `hang on, why is it Adu? Idu is 'this' and Adu is 'that' - that's not a spelling thing`
> **Tutor:** "Idu" means "this," and "Adu" means "that." So, in this context, it's important to use "Idu" for "this."

It has now told me, within two messages, that the answer is `Adu` and that I should use `Idu`. It never explains why the exercise wanted "that". I still don't know which was right, and I'd been given a "Great job!" for the version it then argued against.

Everything else in L5 was fine and first-try: `Nanna hesaru`, `Nimma pustaka`, `Aatana kathey`, `Namaskara, nanna hesaru Ravi`, `Idu nanna kathey`, `Avara hesaru`. Building an introduction out of lesson-1 and lesson-5 words felt like the best moment of the three lessons.

## The stuff that made me want to stop

Ranked by how much it hurt:

1. **Wrong answers marked "Exactly!"/"Great job!" with the real answer smuggled in as a "spelling" note.** Twice, both times on this/that and here/there — the exact pairs a beginner cannot yet distinguish. This is worse than a plain rejection because it certifies a wrong belief.
2. **Being asked to produce `manege` when nothing had taught a case suffix**, and being told it was "in my notes already" when it wasn't. I passed by guessing. That's the moment I'd have closed the app.
3. **Prompts and answer keys disagreeing** — "No, I don't want" needing "food"; "identify a book as your book" needing "that"; the hint "I + home + go" rejecting `Naanu mane hogu`. When a hint restates what I just typed and still says "Not quite", there's nothing left to try.
4. **Fuzzy matching backwards** — `Ila` rejected, `Illi`-for-`Alli` accepted.
5. **Talking to the tutor breaks the answer parser.** I can ask a question or I can answer, never both. So the app punishes exactly the honest behaviour ("I'm not sure, but is it X?") it should be rewarding.
6. **Canned deflections.** "That one is in your notes already, have another look" and a Yelli word-order tip on a sentence with no "where" in it. Both fired when the tutor plainly didn't understand me, and both made it feel like nobody was home.
7. **New words that collide with old words go unflagged** — Illa/Illi, Neeru/Neevu, Avara/Aatana.

## What was actually good

- The pronunciation respellings (`hoh-gu`, `muh-neh`) are excellent for someone with zero script.
- Reusing lesson-2 words (`idu`, `pustaka`, `hesaru`) in lessons 4 and 5 is the best thing the course does. Every one of those moments felt like progress.
- The Illa tip and the `-i near / -a far` pattern (which I found myself) are the only two pieces of real grammar I got, and both were worth ten vocab items.
- Miko's tone is warm without being annoying. No complaints there.

## Honest self-assessment

### Kannada I could produce unprompted, cold, right now

Words:
`Namaskara` · `Naanu` · `Neevu` · `Idu` · `Adu` · `Yenu` · `Pustaka` · `Hesaru` · `Yelli` · `Hogu` · `Mane` · `Beku` · `Beda` · `Oota` · `Neeru` · `Haudu` · `Sari` · `Nanna` · `Nimma` · `Kathey`

Sentences I'd actually risk saying to a person:
- `Namaskara` — hello
- `Nanna hesaru Farhaan` — my name is X
- `Neeru beku` / `Oota beda` — I want water / I don't want food
- `Mane yelli?` — where's the house?
- `Nimma pustaka` / `Nanna kathey` — your book / my story
- `Haudu` / `Illa` / `Sari`

That's a real, usable survival set. Twenty-ish words and a handful of frames. Credit where due.

### Recognise but could not produce

- `Illi` / `Alli` — I know these are here/there. Under pressure I would fifty-fifty them, and the app's false "Exactly!" is a live reason for that. I do not trust myself on this pair.
- `Illa` — I know it, but I'd have to stop and think about whether the "no" one is Illa or Illi.
- `Chennagiddini`, `Hege`, `Iddeera` — memorised as noises from lesson 1. I can say "Chennagiddini" but I couldn't build anything from its parts, and nothing since has touched them.
- `Avara` vs `Aatana` — I can tell you both mean "his". I could not choose between them for an actual person, because "respectful" was never defined.

### Things I was asked to say but genuinely cannot

- `manege`. I typed it once, by accident, to escape a loop. I don't know what `-ge` is, when to use it, or whether it works on any other noun. If you asked me "I'm going to the house" tomorrow I'd say `Naanu mane hogu` and be wrong again.
- Anything with a real verb tense. `Hogu` is the only verb I've seen and I was told it means "Go", then quizzed on "I am going". I have no idea how to conjugate anything.
- "Is". I've been building sentences without a copula for three lessons on the assumption that Kannada doesn't use one, and no one has ever confirmed that. It's my biggest open question and the course has never addressed it.

**The one-line version:** I finished with about twenty words and a decent survival phrasebook, but I finished less confident than I should be, because the app twice told me a wrong answer was right and twice asked me for grammar it hadn't taught.
