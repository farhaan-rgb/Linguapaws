# Playtest — Telugu lessons 22 & 26 (learner E2)

Round 2. English speaker, nothing but the notebook from lessons 1–21 (and 1–25 for the second one).
Sessions: `r2e22`, `r2e26`. Both reached 15/15.

---

## Lesson 22 — "The 'Can' & 'Can't'"

### What actually happened

The vocab drip was fine. Six things in five turns: `Galanu` = can, `Lenu` = can't,
`Cheyagalanu` = I can do, `Cheyalenu` = I can't do, `Matladagalanu` = I can speak,
`Sahaayam` = help. Pronunciation hints were readable and I got through it fast.

Then the sentence phase, and this happened:

> Tutor: Perfect! Say 'I can't do'.
> You: Cheyalenu
> Tutor: **Not quite.** Hint: I + cannot do. Say 'I can't do'.

That is the exact word the tutor taught me four turns earlier —

> Tutor: And "I can't do" is **Cheyalenu** — the same verb, the other ending.

— and the same word the memory check had just accepted:

> Tutor: Exactly! What's the word for "I can't do"?
> You: Cheyalenu
> System: 🎓 **Review passed**

So I asked about it, like I would a human teacher. The reply was a mess:

> You: Wait, I'm confused. You just told me 'I can't do' is Cheyalenu, and the memory check accepted Cheyalenu for exactly that. Why is it wrong now?
> Tutor: **Cheyalenu is correct for "I can't do."**  Now, please say "I can do."
> Tutor: So — Say 'I can't do'.

It agreed with me, then told me to say "I can do", then told me to say "I can't do".
Three things in one turn, two of them contradicting each other. I had no idea what it wanted.

What it actually wanted was `Nenu cheyalenu` — the pronoun bolted on. Which is odd, because
the grammar note that fired one turn earlier said the opposite:

> 💡 *-galanu* and *-lenu* are endings, not words: attach them to a verb stem for "I can" and "I can't". **The "I" is already inside them.**

So the tutor tells me the "I" is baked in, and then refuses my answer for not having the "I" in it.
Those cannot both be right.

Same double-instruction glitch again two turns later. I flagged that I didn't know the word for
the Telugu language:

> You: You haven't taught me the word for the Telugu language. I'll guess it's just 'Telugu': Nenu Telugu matladagalanu
> Tutor: Nenu Telugu matladagalanu.  **Now, try to say 'I can't speak Telugu'.**
> Tutor: So — **Say 'I can speak Telugu'.**

Again two different tasks in one breath, and again no answer to my actual question. It never
did tell me whether the language is called "Telugu" in Telugu. I typed the same thing bare on
the next turn and it sailed through, so my guess was apparently right — but I only know that
by accident.

The rest of the conversation phase went well and I enjoyed it. `Nenu sahaayam cheyagalanu`
(I can help), `Nenu eeroju cheyalenu`, `Nenu sahaayam cheyalenu`, `Nenu idhi ippudu cheyalenu`
— all accepted first try, all built by me out of old vocabulary. That felt good.

### Could I say "I can't speak" unprompted?

Yes — `Nenu matladalenu`. I can do it because I was handed `Matladagalanu` and told explicitly
that `matlada-` + `-galanu` is the join, so swapping in `-lenu` is obvious.

But that's the only kind of case I can do, and the lesson went out of its way to tell me not to try:

> Note the stem is *cheya*, not the *cheyu* you might expect — Telugu reshapes the verb before an ending, so **learn these as pairs rather than trying to build them**.

That's honest, and I appreciate honesty, but it also means the lesson explicitly declines to
teach me the thing the lesson is about. Take a verb I already have in my notebook, `Vellu` = go.
I want "I can't go". Is it `vellagalanu`? `vellilenu`? `vellalenu`? The lesson told me the stem
gets reshaped and gave me no rule for how, so I have no way to find out. I asked, at the end:

> You: how would I say 'I can't go'? I only know Vellu / Velthanu for go, and I'm not sure what the stem would be.
> Tutor: **Not quite. null**

A literal `null` in the tutor's reply. That's the last thing I saw in the lesson.

So: I can generalise across the *ending* (-galanu ↔ -lenu on a stem I've been given) and not
at all across the *verb*. Half a rule.

## Lesson 26 — "Feeling Words"

### What actually happened

Nicest vocab section of the two. `Santhosham`, `Badha`, `Kopam`, `Alupu`, `Bhayam`, and
`Unnara`. The `Unnara` explanation was genuinely the best teaching in either lesson:

> *Unnaru* states, *unnara* asks — Telugu adds that -a to make a question rather than reordering the words.

That told me *why*, and I used it correctly later without being prompted (`Meeru kopamga unnara`).
More of that, please.

Then the first sentence drill, and the wheels came off:

> Tutor: Say 'I am tired'.
> You: Nenu alupu unnanu
> Tutor: Not quite. Hint: To me + **tiredly** + it is.

`-ga` — the whole point of the lesson — had not been mentioned once at that stage. All six
vocab words went by, the memory check passed, and no one had told me that `Alupu` becomes
`Alupuga`. I was marked wrong for not knowing an ending nobody had taught me. I said so:

> You: You never taught me a word for 'tiredly' — only Alupu. From the hint I'll guess: Naaku alupuga undi. Is -ga the 'ly' bit?
> Tutor: Naaku alupuga undi.  **Now, try saying 'I am fine'.**
> Tutor: So — **Say 'I am tired'.**

Identical bug to lesson 22 — question ignored, two contradictory instructions, and a stray
"I am fine" task that has nothing to do with this lesson and was never asked again.

When I typed the answer bare, *then* the rule arrived:

> 💡 *Alupu* is the noun "tiredness"; add -ga and it becomes the state you are in. Every feeling word in this lesson does it — *santhoshamga*, *kopamga*, *badhaga* — and it pairs with *undi* for things about you and *unnanu* for you yourself.

Good note. Wrong place. Put it before the drill and I'd have got it first try.

And then it immediately turned out not to be true:

> Tutor: Correct! Say 'I am not sad'.
> You: Nenu badhaga ledu
> Tutor: Not quite. Hint: To me + **sadness** + not.

So the negative wants `Naaku badha ledu` — bare noun, no `-ga`. One turn after being told
"**every** feeling word does it". That's a straight contradiction and it's the single most
confusing moment in either lesson. I asked twice.

First bundled with the answer:

> You: Naaku badha ledu — but you just told me every feeling word takes -ga. Why is it badha here and not badhaga?
> Tutor: So — Say 'I am not sad'.

(My answer was correct and it didn't even notice, because there was a question stapled to it.)

Then on its own turn:

> You: Quick question first: why was it 'badha' with no -ga in the negative, but 'alupuga' in the positive?
> Tutor: Nenu santhoshamga unnanu.  Say that!
> Tutor: Not quite. Hint: I + happy.

Never answered. It just handed me the next answer and simultaneously marked me wrong for
having asked. I still don't know why the negative drops the `-ga`. I've memorised that it does.

Rest of the conversation phase was smooth and satisfying — `Naaku bhayam ledu`,
`Naaku eeroju chala alupuga undi` (I assembled `chala` and `eeroju` from old lessons myself,
which felt great), and `Ayana badhaga unnaru` closed the lesson.

### Could I say "I am angry" unprompted?

Yes: `Nenu kopamga unnanu`. But I want to be precise about *why* — `kopamga` was literally
spelled out for me in the grammar note, so I'm not building it, I'm recalling it. And I'd be
guessing at the frame. There are three frames in this lesson and I was never told how to choose:

- `Naaku alupuga undi` — to-me + feeling-ga + it is
- `Nenu santhoshamga unnanu` — I + feeling-ga + I am
- `Naaku badha ledu` — to-me + bare feeling + not

The note's version of the rule — "*undi* for things about you and *unnanu* for you yourself" —
is a distinction I cannot operate. Is tiredness a thing about me or me myself? I got it right
by copying, not by understanding. Given a new feeling word I'd take a coin-flip on which frame.

So: the `-ga` ending itself, yes, I can attach it. Choosing the sentence it goes in, no.

## How it felt

Both lessons have a good core. The drip-feed pacing is comfortable, the conversation phases
are the best part — I was genuinely pleased with myself assembling `Naaku eeroju chala alupuga
undi` out of four lessons' worth of scraps. Miko is likeable.

But both lessons pull the same trick: mark me wrong for a rule I haven't been given, then
explain the rule after I've already failed. That's demoralising in a way that has nothing to
do with difficulty. And in both lessons, the moment I asked a question, the tutor fell apart —
gave me two conflicting orders, ignored a correct answer, and never once answered anything I
asked. After the third time I stopped asking, which is not what you want from a language app.

## The seven questions

**1. Did the tutor ever state an answer that the grader then marked wrong?**
**Yes. Twice, and one of them is flat-out self-contradiction inside a single turn.**
- L22: tutor taught "'I can't do' is **Cheyalenu**", the memory check accepted `Cheyalenu`,
  and the sentence drill then rejected `Cheyalenu` with "Not quite." When challenged it said
  "Cheyalenu is correct for 'I can't do.'" and re-asked the same question anyway. Only
  `Nenu cheyalenu` passed.
- L22: the grammar note says "The 'I' is already inside them", and the grader then requires
  the separate pronoun `Nenu`.
- L26 near-miss: the tutor typed the answer `Nenu santhoshamga unnanu` and appended
  "Not quite. Hint: I + happy" in the same turn.

**2. Did the tutor reply in Telugu, or use Telugu words I'd never been taught?**
Never replied in Telugu — all instruction and all hints were English. Good. But it **demanded**
two things it never taught:
- `-ga` (L26) was required by the "I am tired" drill before it had been introduced at all.
- The word for the Telugu language (L22 "Say 'I can speak Telugu'") — I guessed "Telugu",
  which seems to have been right, but I was never told.

**3. Did any single turn give me two different things to do?**
**Yes — four times, and it happened every single time I asked a question.** L22: "please say
'I can do'" + "Say 'I can't do'". L22: "try to say 'I can't speak Telugu'" + "Say 'I can speak
Telugu'". L26: "try saying 'I am fine'" + "Say 'I am tired'" (and "I am fine" isn't even part
of this lesson). L26: "Say that!" + "Not quite." This looks like one bug — asking a question
makes the tutor emit its own next-step message *and* the re-prompt.

**4. Was every word a drill asked for actually taught to me?**
No. `-ga` in L26 and the language name in L22 (see Q2). Everything else was either taught in
this lesson or already in my notebook.

**5. Did the tutor get any fact about Telugu wrong, or invent a rule when challenged?**
No wrong facts, and — to its credit — it did **not** invent anything when challenged. It went
the other way: it refused to engage at all. Zero of my four questions got an answer. The
`-ga`-in-positive / no-`-ga`-in-negative contradiction is a real gap in the explanation rather
than a false statement, but "**every** feeling word does it" is overstated to the point of
being misleading, since the very next drill breaks it.

**6. Did the endings generalise?**
- **`-galanu` / `-lenu` (L22): partly.** I can swap the ending on a stem I've been handed
  (`Matladagalanu` → `Matladalenu`). I cannot form the stem for any new verb, and the lesson
  says so out loud — "learn these as pairs rather than trying to build them". So for a verb
  like `Vellu` I'm stuck, and asking got me "Not quite. **null**".
- **`-ga` (L26): the ending yes, the sentence no.** I can attach `-ga`. I cannot reliably choose
  between `Naaku X-ga undi` / `Nenu X-ga unnanu` / `Naaku X ledu`, because the rule offered
  ("*undi* for things about you and *unnanu* for you yourself") isn't usable and the third
  frame was never explained at all.

**7. Were my typos handled sensibly?**
Mixed. L26 was ideal: I typed `Kopum` (straight off the "koh-**pum**" hint) and got
"Yes. It is spelled **Kopam**." Accepted and corrected — exactly right. L22 was too soft:
`Lehnu` for `Lenu` got a bare "Right." and `Sahayam` for `Sahaayam` got silently waved through
with no correction. Note both L22 typos came from the pronunciation hints themselves
("leh-noo", "sah-hah-yam"), so a learner will make them and then never learn the real spelling.

---

## Bug list, shortest version

1. **L22 grader requires `Nenu` where the tutor taught the bare form** — contradicts both the
   taught vocab, the memory check, and its own grammar note. Highest severity.
2. **L26 `-ga` is graded before it is taught.** Move the grammar note ahead of the first
   sentence drill.
3. **Any question triggers a double-instruction turn** with two conflicting tasks, and the
   question is never answered. Also swallows a correct answer if one is bundled with it.
4. **"Every feeling word takes `-ga`" is contradicted by the next drill** (`Naaku badha ledu`).
   Either scope the claim to positives or explain the negative frame.
5. **`"Not quite. null"`** — literal null in the tutor output when a session gets input after
   completion.
6. **L22 asks for "Telugu" (the language)** without teaching it.
7. **L26's "things about you vs you yourself"** rule is not actionable; the three sentence
   frames need naming explicitly.
8. **L22 typo handling is silently permissive**, and the pronunciation hints induce the typos.
