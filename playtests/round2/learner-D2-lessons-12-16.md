# Playtest — Telugu lessons 12 and 16 (learner D2, round 2)

## Short version

Lesson 12 was the smoothest lesson I've had so far. It taught six time words, let me
build sentences out of stuff I already had, and — crucially — it never once asked me for
a past or future verb. That was the thing that ruined it last time, and it's genuinely
fixed.

Lesson 16 was mostly fine and then fell over hard on the very last drill, in exactly the
way the old bug did: **Miko typed the answer out for me, and then the grader told me the
answer Miko had just typed was wrong.** So the worst bug is not gone. It moved.

---

## Lesson 12 — time words

### What I actually learned

Eeroju (today), Repu (tomorrow), Ninna (yesterday), Appudu (then), Ganta (hour),
Tharuvatha (later). All six stuck, and I could use all six unprompted by the end. More
importantly I learned a *rule* I didn't have before: the time word goes at the front and
the verb goes at the end. I got told that twice, once as a tip after I'd already done it
right:

> "The time word leads. Telugu is happy to open with *eeroju* and let the verb close the
> sentence — time first, verb last is the normal shape."

That's a real explanation of how to build something, not just a fact being asserted at
me. Same for this one, which pre-empted a question I was actually about to ask:

> "Telugu is content to use the 'am going' form for a plan, exactly as English says 'I'm
> going tomorrow'. The time word carries the future; the verb does not have to."

That is the single most useful thing either lesson told me. I had been sitting there
thinking "hang on, you want me to say *tomorrow* but the only verb form I've got is *I am
going* — surely that's wrong?" and it answered it before I asked. Good.

### Where I got stuck

Nowhere. I finished in 13 messages with no wrong answers.

### Things asked that I was never taught

None. Every sentence was buildable from my notebook. "Tomorrow one hour" needed *okati*
from lesson 7 and *ganta* from this lesson, and it took `Repu okati ganta` without
complaint — that's the exact string the last tester was marked wrong on.

### Things scored unfairly / oddly

- I mistyped **Ninna** as "Nina" and got "Yes." with no correction at all. I only found
  out later, from the end-of-lesson summary, that it has a double n. Lesson 16 *does*
  correct spelling ("It is spelled **Mellaga**"), so the two lessons behave differently
  and lesson 12's version is the worse one — I'd have walked away spelling it wrong.
- Same again with "Tharuvata" for **Tharuvatha**. Passed silently.

### Confusing / contradictory

- One turn dumped two new words on me at once (Ganta *and* Tharuvatha) and then only
  asked me to say one of them. The pronunciation hints got stacked up weirdly as a
  result: `[say it like: Ganta: gan-tah]  [say it like: Tharuvatha: thuh-ru-vuh-thuh]`.
  Not confusing enough to break anything, but Ganta arrived with no drill and I'd have
  forgotten it if the memory check hadn't happened to ask.
- The conversation section asked me to say "I am going today" **three times** in slightly
  different wrappers ("Tell Miko you are going today", then "Say 'I am going today'").
  Same answer each time. It felt like padding.

### How it felt

Easy and pleasant. Slightly *too* easy — three of the five sentence drills were the same
sentence — but I came out able to say when I was doing things, which is what it promised.

## Lesson 16 — "how" and adverbs

### What I actually learned

Ela (how), Baga (well), Tvaraga (quickly), Mellaga (slowly), Santhosham (happiness). And
two useful patterns, both properly taught rather than asserted:

> "**Santhosham** is 'happiness'. To say you ARE happy, add -ga: *santhoshamga*, and pair
> it with *unnanu* — *nenu santhoshamga unnanu*. Every feeling word takes that -ga."

That's a build rule I could apply. And:

> "*Baga* sits right before the verb, not at the front of the sentence like English puts
> 'well' at the end."

I got `Idhi ela undi?` right first try and the tip afterwards told me *why* it works and
where else to use it ("food, a film, a day"). That one I'll remember.

### Where I got stuck — the last drill

This is the bad one. Asked to say "How is your friend?", I put together
`Mee snehithudu ela unnaru?` from my notes and flagged that I was guessing on *unnaru*.
What came back, in one turn:

> Tutor: Mee snehithudu ela unnaru?  Now, can you say 'I am coming tomorrow'?
> Tutor: Not quite. Hint: Your + friend + how?. Ask 'How is your friend?'.

Three separate problems in four lines:

1. It **printed my answer back at me as if it were the model answer** — same words, same
   order, same question mark — and then said "Not quite." I genuinely could not tell what
   it wanted. I had just been shown the correct sentence and told it was incorrect.
2. It asked me for **"I am coming tomorrow"**. I have never been taught "coming". Not in
   any lesson, not in my notebook. Then it immediately abandoned that and went back to
   the friend question, so I never had to answer it — but if I'd tried, I'd have been
   stuck on a word that doesn't exist for me yet.
3. The accepted answer turned out to be `Mee snehithudu ela?` — **no verb at all.** I only
   got there by typing the hint out literally, word for word. That cleared the drill and
   ended the lesson.

So the grader wanted a three-word verbless fragment, while the tutor itself wrote the
full sentence with *unnaru*. Whichever of those is the intended Telugu, the lesson is
teaching me the other one.

### Things scored unfairly

- `Mee snehithudu ela unnaru?` marked wrong immediately after being shown to me as the
  answer. Nothing else in either lesson was scored unfairly.

### Confusing / contradictory

- **A wrong lesson reference.** After "Go slowly" I was told: *"The -andi ending from
  lesson 3 again — vellandi rather than vellu..."* My lesson 3 notes are
  `Ekkada / Vellu / Ikkada / Akkada / Illu / Undi` — no -andi anywhere. The -andi forms I
  have (*Ivvandi*, *Kurchondi*) are from **lesson 10**. So it pointed me back to a lesson
  that doesn't contain the thing.
- **It waved my question away.** I asked about exactly that, plus whether *Baga* and
  *Bagundi* (lesson 9) are related. The whole answer was:

  > "The polite form adds a suffix to the verb. And yes, 'baga' and 'bagundi' are related
  > as both refer to 'well' but are used in different contexts."

  It didn't name the suffix, didn't address the lesson-3 mix-up at all, and "used in
  different contexts" tells me nothing — *which* contexts? That's the same
  non-explanation shape the last tester complained about. I still don't know when to say
  which.
- It then repeated the same prompt twice back-to-back in one turn ("Now, let's try again.
  Say 'I am doing well'." / "So — Say 'I am doing well'.").
- **Santhosham is labelled two ways.** Taught as "happiness", quizzed as "Happy", and
  listed in the summary as "Santhosham — Happy". I answered the quiz with
  *Santhoshamga* (the "happy" form it had just taught me) and got "Spot on! It is spelled
  **Santhosham**" — so it accepted the adverbial form and then corrected me to the noun.
  Right outcome by accident; the label is just wrong in the data.
- "I am doing well" came up twice, identically.

### How it felt

Good for twelve messages, then genuinely frustrating for the last two. Being shown a
sentence and told it's wrong in the same breath is the most demoralising thing this app
does — I stopped trusting anything Miko typed. And I finished the lesson by copying a
hint rather than by understanding anything, which means the final drill taught me
nothing.

## The seven questions

**1. Did the tutor ever state an answer the grader then marked wrong?**
**Yes — still happening, in lesson 16.** Not in lesson 12. The lesson-16 case is the last
drill: Miko wrote `Mee snehithudu ela unnaru?` and the very next line was "Not quite."
The answer the grader wanted was `Mee snehithudu ela?`. The three specific old cases are
gone — `Repu okati ganta` was **accepted** in lesson 12, and I was never asked for
*chesa*/*chesanu* or *Ela chestanu*/*Ela cheyali* at all. So the specific instances were
fixed; the underlying bug wasn't.

**2. Did lesson 12 demand past or future verb forms I hadn't been taught?**
**No.** Completely clean. Every drill used present continuous from lesson 11, and when
"tomorrow" would have tempted a future form, the lesson explicitly told me the present
form is correct and the time word does the work. This is properly fixed.

**3. Did the tutor get a fact wrong, or invent a rule when challenged?**
**Two problems, one factual and one hand-wave.** Factual: it attributed the -andi polite
ending to "lesson 3", where it doesn't appear (it's lesson 10). Hand-wave: challenged on
*baga* vs *bagundi*, it said they "are used in different contexts" and stopped — which is
the same empty move as the old "changes based on context". It didn't invent a false rule,
it just declined to give one. Separately, whether `Mee snehithudu ela?` with no verb is
correct Telugu at all is a content question worth someone checking; my instinct as a
learner is that the version Miko typed was the better sentence.

**4. Did any single turn give me two different things to do?**
**Yes, once, badly.** "Mee snehithudu ela unnaru? Now, can you say 'I am coming
tomorrow'?" followed by "Ask 'How is your friend?'" — one turn containing a model answer,
a brand-new request, a rejection, and a re-ask of the original. Also a milder one in
lesson 12, where a single turn taught Ganta and Tharuvatha together.

**5. Did the tutor reply in Telugu, or use Telugu I'd never been taught?**
No Telugu-only replies — every Telugu word came with English. One untaught item: it asked
me for "I am coming tomorrow" and I have no word for "coming". It also used *vellandi* and
*santhoshamga*, but both were explained in the same breath, so those were fine.

**6. Did "We'll come back to this" ever come back? Is there a way to skip a drill?**
The phrase never appeared, so nothing was left dangling. But **there is no skip.** When I
was stuck on the friend question the only escape was to type the hint verbatim. I also
tried plain "ok" mid-vocabulary and got "Sure — here it is again" plus the same prompt
repeated — friendly, but it's a re-ask, not a way past. If I hadn't guessed that the hint
was literally the answer, I'd have been stuck on the last step of the lesson.

**7. Did the hints describe the answer that was actually accepted?**
**Mostly yes, and in the one failure the hint was right and the tutor was wrong.** The
hint "Your + friend + how?" describes `Mee snehithudu ela?` exactly — three slots, no
verb — and that is what passed. So the hint was accurate; it was the tutor's own model
answer that contradicted it. Every other hint matched. The pronunciation hints were all
accurate too, though the stacked double hint in lesson 12 read awkwardly.
