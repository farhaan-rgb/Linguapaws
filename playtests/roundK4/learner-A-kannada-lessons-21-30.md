# Kannada lessons 21–30: first play-through (learner A, round K4)

Sessions: `rK5-kanA-L21step` … `rK5-kanA-L30step`. Step mode for 21, 22, 24, 26, 27, 29 and 30; chat for 23, 25 and 28.
Notebook: `--notes --scenario 20`, then `--scenario 21` (that one only added L20: chennagittu, gottu, gottilla, nenapide, matte). I didn't print any notebook past lesson 20.

Numbering: `step-sim --scenario N` puts you on lesson N+1. `lesson-sim --scenario N` puts you on lesson N ("scenario 22 — Can & Can't"). I started chat on the wrong lesson once, then threw that session away. The tool help says both are zero-based, which isn't true for lesson-sim.

## How it went

Honestly, well. Every lesson teaches 5 to 7 words, uses them straight away, and its last sentence pulls in something from an earlier lesson. That last sentence was hard enough that I actually had to think. The course never asked me for a word it hadn't shown me. Twice the step screen corrected my *naanu* to *nanage* ("naanu means "I" — here you want nanage, "To me""). By lesson 26 I had stopped making that mistake.

## Findings, worst first

1. **L28 chat, a question I asked:** "isn't baruttide the same as baruttade?" The tutor answered: "baruttade means "it comes" … You're bundled up in a warm blanket because it's chilly outside." L22 taught me baruttade = "Can (know how to)". When I then typed `male baruttade`, the grader told me "baruttade means "Can (know how to)"". So within two turns the free-text answer and the grader gave me two different meanings, plus a stray sentence that had nothing to do with my question. The spoken tip that came later (baruttide "it is coming", baruttade "it comes", as in Kannada baruttade) does make sense, but only if you already remember L22's "Kannada comes to me".
2. **"Have" is taught two ways and never compared.** In L24 "I have work" is *nanage kelasa ide*. I worked it out from nanage + ide, and it was never taught. In L27 "I have no money" is *nanna hattira duddu illa*. Nothing ever says when you use which.
3. **L23 chat, a question I asked:** "how do I know it's hoga and not hogu?" The answer was "Hoga is for 'to go' when you want to say 'must go,' while Hogu is a different form… Keep practicing". That doesn't tell me anything. I still can't make a "must" form from a verb I haven't been given.
4. **On the step surface, asking a question costs you a try.** In L22, "wait why is it nanage and not naanu?" came back as "Not this one — you have another try". It did explain, but it marked me wrong for asking.
5. **Words used before they were taught, on screen only:** L21 screen 1, the Mele note uses *meju mele*, and meju isn't taught until screen 6. L26 screen 1 uses *aagide*, which comes on screen 2. L21 build-phrase 1 shows *mejina mele*, which is never taught. L30's last tip says "*Hogi baruttene*, from lesson 14, works too", but that phrase isn't in my L14 notebook.
6. **Wrong things the teaching says:** L25 (spoken): "Bandare … Baruttene … Same root, *baru*". But I was taught that bandare comes from *bande*. L27: "Add -a to *ide* … Ideya". Adding -a gives *idea*, not ideya. L29: "Guru literally means 'teacher'", but L6 taught me shikshaka for teacher. L23's ending says "must not touch", and "touch" was never taught.
7. **Generous marking:** `kelge` got "Exactly right" (L21), `kalide` got "Exactly right" for Kalite (L30), and `bus` was accepted for Bassu (L28). I couldn't find a correct answer that was rejected.
8. **Read-aloud.** In L28 chat, the line 'Male is "rain"' is spoken inside English, so an English voice will very likely say "male" the English way. Every "You can now say:" summary reads out slashes and brackets: "Then / in that case", "If (someone) goes", "Town / hometown", "Right? / isn't it?". Some prompts end in doubled punctuation: L27 'I have no change.'.' and L29 '"Really?"?'.
9. **Too big a jump, or too small.** L27 goes straight from five (L7) to ten, a hundred and a thousand, with no six to nine, so I can't say "ninety", and the lesson's own setup needed it ("bill is 90"). L22 spends one of its six new words on "Kannada", and then quizzes me on it from memory. The confusable trio baruttene (L14), baruttade (L22) and baruttide (L28) is only explained in L28.

## By ear

In chat, the grammar tips are now spoken (they come out as 🔊 Spoken). That's real progress. A spoken tip helped me more than once. "Times first, the action last" stuck, and so did "only the last word changes: aagide/aagilla", and I used both correctly afterwards.

In step mode it's the other way round. Every tip, every explanation of why I was wrong, and even every **question prompt** ("How do you say 'Hunger'?", "Say 'I am tired'") is 👁 only. If you just listen, you hear a Kannada word with no question in front of it. On a walk I couldn't do step mode at all. I learned from step mode only because I was reading the screen.

## Step surface, how it felt

Clearing a screen felt like nothing. By lesson 22 I'd stopped reading "Nailed it / Spot on / Right first time". The 💡 tip under the praise was the one line I did read, and it was the useful one. A wrong answer never made me want to stop: one retry, a specific reason, and the right word handed over on the second miss ("Have this one on me"). "Sharp: 14 of 16" at the end was the only number I paid attention to.

## Can I learn Kannada from this?

From these ten lessons, yes, as long as I'm reading. At lesson 20 I could tell someone about my day. Now I can say where things are (on, under, inside, near, with). I can say I can or can't speak Kannada, can or can't make it, and must or mustn't do something. I can join two thoughts with but, or, because and too, and make plans with "if" (hodare, bandare, iddare) and "in that case". I can say I'm hungry, thirsty, tired or sad, ask for change and say I have no money, talk about rain, cold and a bus to my town, use the friendly fillers (alva, hauda, sakkath, guru, paravaagilla, oota aayta?), and say I learned some Kannada and goodbye with *matte sigona*.

**My reading: clean, but not innocent.** The checker only knows words this course teaches. I made two leaks it can't see, because neither word is in the course:
- **L21, screen 4:** I typed `horage` ("outside") on the Hattira screen, expecting "outside" to come next. That's outside knowledge, plain and simple. The course never teaches "outside", and the answer was rejected.
- **L25 chat:** I typed `nimage time iddare, naale baruttira?`. *baruttira* ("will you come?") is outside knowledge, and *time* is English. It was rejected.
- `chai` for Chaha (L24) is English, and it was rejected too.

Everything else I built from parts I'd been taught: *nanage kelasa ide* (nanage + ide), *nanage kooda ishta* (from the tip), *ivattu tumba chali ide* (bisi/chali + ide), *mane hogabeku*. Several of my wrong answers in L25, L28 and L29 happened because I sent my next answer before I'd read the next prompt. That was my fault, not the course's.
