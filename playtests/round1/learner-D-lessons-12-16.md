# Learner D — Telugu, lessons 12 and 16

I'm eleven lessons in. I speak only English. Everything I know is the five-words-a-lesson list in
my notebook. Here's how these two went.

---

## 1. Did I actually learn anything?

**Lesson 12 ("The 'When' (Time)")** — I did learn five words and I'd use them tomorrow:
Eeroju (today), Repu (tomorrow), Ninna (yesterday), Appudu (then), Ganta (hour). Those stuck,
because the drill for them is clean: word, pronunciation hint, type it back, recall check.

**Lesson 16 ("The 'How' (Ela)")** — same deal: Baga (well), Tvaraga (quickly), Mellaga (slowly),
Santhosham (happy). Ela I already had from lesson 1. Those stuck too.

**But this lesson was supposedly about tense, and no, I cannot form a past or future sentence
myself with a new verb.** Not even close. Here is the honest state of my head:

- I know Chestunnanu = "I am doing" (lesson 11).
- Lesson 12 asked me for "I did", "I will do", "I ate". Three different forms.
- What I got told, in order, was: *chesa* (wrong), then *chesanu* (right), then *chestaanu*
  (right for future), then *tintaanu* — which the tutor told me meant "I ATE" — and then the
  real answer turned out to be *thinnanu*.
- Nobody ever told me the rule. Not once. The only thing resembling an explanation was
  **"Just like 'I am doing', you change it to past."** That's not a rule, that's just saying
  "it changes." Change *what* to *what*?

So if you handed me a brand new verb right now and said "make it past tense," I'd have nothing.
I'd stare at it. I tried to work the pattern out myself mid-lesson — I typed
*"Guessing from chestunnanu -> chesanu, would it be 'Ninna nenu tinanu'?"* — and I was told
*"That's a clever attempt!"* and then given a form (*tintaanu*) that was wrong anyway. I ended the
lesson less confident about tense than when I started, because I now believe a false thing
(see §5).

And the lesson's own closing summary agrees with me. After a whole lesson of past and future
drills, the "You can now say" line was:

> **You can now say:** Eeroju — Today · Repu — Tomorrow · Ninna — Yesterday · Appudu — Then · Ganta — Hour

Five nouns. Zero verb forms. So the lesson itself doesn't think it taught me tense either.

Lesson 16 has the same shape: it drills adverbs, then makes me produce command forms and
"how to ___" forms that were never on any list.

**One more thing I noticed:** when I checked my notebook before lesson 16, I could see that
**L13 is "Simple Past Tense" (Chesanu, Vellanu, Thinnanu, Chusanu, Cheppanu)** and
**L14 is "Simple Future Tense" (Chestanu, Velthanu, Tintanu, Chustanu)**. Those are *exactly* the
words lesson 12 demanded from me. So lesson 12 is testing me on lessons 13 and 14. That's just
the wrong order. If lesson 12 had been "here are the time words, use them with the present tense
you already have," it would have been a perfectly good lesson.

## 2. Where I got stuck

### Lesson 12, "Tomorrow one hour" — genuinely stuck, six attempts

This is the worst one. I had all three words. Watch:

> Tutor: Say 'Tomorrow one hour'.
> **Me: Repu Okati Ganta**
> Tutor: Not quite. Hint: Tomorrow + one + hour. Say 'Tomorrow one hour'.

The hint is a literal description of what I just typed. So I said so:

> **Me: That is Tomorrow + one + hour as far as I know. Repu = tomorrow, Okati = one, Ganta = hour. What else could it be?**
> Tutor: You're right about the words! Just remember — when saying it together, the correct form is **Repu okati ganta**.
> **Me: Repu okati ganta**
> Tutor: Not quite. Hint: Tomorrow + one + hour.

It dictated a phrase to me and then marked that exact phrase wrong. I asked again:

> **Me: I typed 'Repu okati ganta' exactly like you said and it's still wrong. I'm stuck - just tell me the answer.**
> Tutor: The correct phrase is **Repu okati ganta**.

Same wrong answer again. Then:

> **Me: I give up on this one**
> Tutor: It's okay to feel stuck sometimes. We can try a different phrase or topic if you'd like. Just let me know! 🐾
> Tutor: So — Say 'Tomorrow one hour'.

That's a very nice sentence that does nothing. "We can try a different phrase or topic if you'd
like" — I said I'd like! And then it asked the same question again. There is no actual skip.

I only got out because I gambled that "one" before a noun might be a shorter word than *Okati*,
and typed **Repu oka ganta** — and that was it. *Oka* has never appeared in eleven lessons of my
notebook. It is not in lesson 7 with the numbers. I got past this step by luck.

Four "Not quite. Hint: Tomorrow + one + hour" replies in a row, near enough. I nearly stopped
the lesson here.

*chesanu*, not *chesa*. The tutor had the word wrong the whole time. Four turns of me being told

### Lesson 16, "How to do?" — same failure mode

### Lesson 16, "Go slowly" — the tutor contradicted its own hint

> Tutor: Say 'Go slowly'.
> **Me: Mellaga vellu**
> Tutor: Not quite. Hint: Slowly + go.
> **Me: Slowly is Mellaga and go is Vellu, so 'Mellaga vellu' IS slowly + go. Why is that wrong?**

*Kurchondi* in lesson 10 made me suspect a polite `-andi` ending. That's me doing the teaching.

### Lesson 16, "I am very happy"

> Tutor: Say 'I am very happy'.
> Hint: I + well + happy.

Three tries, being pushed toward *Nenu baga santhosham*. The real answer:
**Nenu chala santhoshamga unnanu** — three things I've never seen (*chala*, the `-ga` ending,
*unnanu*). The hint "I + well + happy" doesn't describe that sentence at all.

  > Tutor: **Correct!** 'Tvaraga cheyandi' is good for telling someone to do it quickly. [...]

**Wrong, marked right — my typos:**
- Told to say **Ninna**, I typed **"Nina"**. → *"Yes."* Straight through.

I'd rather be corrected. Letting "Nina" and "Twaraga" through means I'll keep spelling them
wrong. Meanwhile the phrase-building steps are so strict that the tutor's own answers fail them.
The tolerance is backwards: loose where it should be tight, tight where it should be loose.

## 5. Confusing or contradictory

**The worst thing in either lesson.** I spotted that *chestaanu* ("I will do") and *tintaanu*
(which I'd just been told meant "I ate") share an ending, and asked:

> **Me: Hang on - you told me chestaanu means 'I WILL do'. Now you're saying tintaanu means 'I ATE'. Those look like the same ending -taanu. Which is it, past or future?**
> Tutor: "Taanu" is a bit tricky! In "chestaanu," it means "I will do," and in "tintaanu," it means "I ate." **The ending changes based on the context.**

That is a made-up answer to a real question. "The ending changes based on context" tells me the
language is arbitrary and unlearnable. And it isn't even true here — my notebook now shows
**L14 Simple Future: Tintanu = I will eat**. So *tintaanu* is FUTURE, the tutor told me it was
past, and when I caught the inconsistency it invented a rule to cover the mistake instead of
saying "you're right, I misspoke." I would have walked out of that lesson believing something
false. That's worse than teaching me nothing.

**Other confusions:**

- **The hints don't describe the answers.** "Hint: I + well + happy" for a sentence that is
  actually *chala santhoshamga unnanu*. "Hint: Tomorrow + one + hour" for something needing *oka*
  not *okati*. "Hint: I + tomorrow + will do" for an answer ordered tomorrow-I-will-do. I stopped
  trusting the hints halfway through lesson 12, which means the scaffolding is worse than useless.

- **The tutor started talking to me in Telugu.** Out of nowhere, in lesson 16:
  > Tutor: Nenu cheptaanu. 'Idhi ela thinali?' anedi. Paws-ome try! 😺 Meeru ee roju em thinnaru? (What did you eat today?)

  and later:
  > Tutor: Nenu mīku vishayam cheptaanu, 'Cheyandi' means "do it." Meeru evarini tvaraga cheyandi cheppali?

  I cannot read any of that. *cheptaanu, anedi, em, thinnaru, mīku, vishayam, evarini, cheppali* —
  none of it is in my notebook. Only some of it gets an English gloss in brackets. And that second
  one is *asking me a question in Telugu* that I have no way to understand, let alone answer.
  I'm 16 lessons into a 5-words-a-lesson course. Also, everything else in the course is plain
  ASCII romanisation and suddenly there's a macron in "mīku" — that's a different spelling system
  appearing without warning.

- **Two spellings for the same word.** I was taught *tintaanu* and later saw *Tintanu* in my
  notes; likewise *chestaanu* vs *Chestanu*, and lesson 16's answer said *ee roju* (two words)
  where lesson 12 taught *Eeroju* (one). Small, but when I'm being marked wrong on exact strings,
  I notice.

- **"Ganta" pronounced "gan-tah" but "Tvaraga" as "tvah-rah-gah"** — fine, but "Twaraga" being
  accepted for "Tvaraga" and "Nina" for "Ninna" made me unsure whether the doubled letters matter
  at all. They presumably do.

- **"We'll come back to this"** appeared twice (after the *chesanu* failure and after
  *santhoshamga unnanu*). We never came back to either. If you say that, come back to it.

## 6. How it felt

The first third of each lesson is genuinely pleasant. Five words, clear pronunciation hint, type
it back, quick recall check — I felt like I was getting somewhere and the pace was right. Miko's
tone is warm without being sickly, and the paw emoji didn't bother me.

Then the phrase-building starts and it falls apart. The problem isn't difficulty — I'd happily be
pushed. The problem is that **I couldn't trust the feedback.** When the tutor dictates an answer,
I type it, and I'm told I'm wrong — three separate times across two lessons — I stop being able
to tell my mistakes from its mistakes. By the end of lesson 12 I was typing semi-random variants
of things I'd been told, hoping to trip the "you've failed enough times, here's the answer"
mechanism. That's not learning, that's picking a lock.

The praise stopped meaning anything early. "Spot on!", "Exactly!", "Perfect!", "Great job!",
"Paws-ome try!" — I got "That's a clever attempt!" for a guess that was wrong, and "Correct!"
immediately followed by "Not quite." on the same input. And the sign-off on lesson 12,
**"That's the scenario done — nicely handled"**, arrived right after I'd failed the final step and
had the answer handed to me. I hadn't handled it nicely. I'd been told the answer. Being
congratulated for that is a small thing but it's the moment I'd have closed the app, because it
told me the tutor isn't actually watching.

The specific thing that would make me quit: I asked the one good question I had all session — the
`-taanu` one — and got a confident, false answer. I'd rather a tutor say "good catch, I got that
wrong" than smooth it over. If I can't rely on it to be right, and it won't tell me when it's
unsure, then I'm better off with a phrasebook.

Would I keep going? I'd finish the vocabulary drills, honestly — those work. I would start
skipping the sentence sections, which are the part that's supposed to matter.

**If one thing gets fixed:** move lesson 12 so it comes after the past and future tense lessons,
or rewrite its drills to only use the present tense I actually have. Right now lesson 12 is an
exam for material I haven't been given, and lesson 16 is the same for commands and infinitives.

**If two things get fixed:** make the tutor's dictated answer and the accepted answer the same
string. Every single stuck point in this session was that one bug.
