# Learner C — Telugu, lessons 7 & 8

Notes from the person doing the learning. I speak English only. Before lesson 7 the
only Telugu I had was the 30 words from lessons 1–6 in my notebook.

---

## 1. Did I actually learn anything?

Yes, but only the flashcard half of it.

**Things I could genuinely use unprompted now:**
- The numbers one to five: *Okati, Rendu, Moodu, Naalugu, Aidhu*. These are solid. The
  drill-and-recall loop at the top of lesson 7 works well.
- The plural words from lesson 8: *Pusthakalu* (books), *Snehithulu* (friends),
  *Kurchilu* (chairs), and the idea that *-lu* is the plural ending. That one grammar
  note — "*pusthakam* became *pusthakalu* — that -lu ending is what pluralises it" —
  was the single clearest thing in either lesson.
- Two full sentences I can say from memory because I typed them successfully:
  *Pusthakalu ekkada?* ("Where are the books?") and *Naa snehithulu* ("my friends").
  Building those out of words I already owned felt great. That's the good version of
  this app.

**Things that didn't stick, and I'd say I actively can't use:**
- *Oka* vs *Okati*. I was taught *Okati* = one. Then "One book" only accepted
  *Oka Pusthakam*. The explanation I got when I asked ("'Oka' is used for a single item
  when describing it, while 'Okati' is more for counting") is probably right but it
  arrived as a correction, not as teaching, and *Oka* never went in my notebook. If you
  asked me tomorrow to say "one book" I'd hesitate.
- Anything to do with "I have". I typed *Naaku oka akka undi* in lesson 7 only because
  the prompt printed it for me in brackets. By the end of lesson 8 I had been given
  *three different* versions of "I have many friends" and I don't know which is real,
  so I'd just not say it.
- *Illu*. I now have less confidence in this word than before lesson 7. See §5.
- *Kurchilu* I can say, but I was never taught the singular *chair*, so I can't say
  "one chair".

Net: about 8 words gained, one grammar rule gained, and one word (*Illu*) actively
damaged.

## 2. Where I got stuck

**Lesson 7, "One book" — stuck 4 turns.**
I typed `Okati pusthakam`. Got:
> Not quite. Hint: One + book. Say 'One book'.

The hint is literally describing what I typed. I tried again with capitals,
`Okati Pusthakam`, same reply verbatim. So I asked in English what I was missing and
got:
> You're correct, but remember to capitalize 'Pusthakam'. It's important for proper
> nouns. Now, let's keep going! What about 'Two books'?
> So — Say 'One book'.

Three separate problems in that one reply. It told me I was correct while the grader
said I wasn't. It told me to capitalise a word I had already capitalised on the
previous turn. And "it's important for proper nouns" is nonsense — *pusthakam* is
"book", it isn't a proper noun. I only escaped because I took a wild swing at
`Oka Pusthakam`.

**Lesson 8, "Tell Miko you have many friends" — stuck 5 turns, and this is the bad one.**
Turn 1, I guessed honestly:
> You: Naaku chala snehithulu undi - guessing, I only have 'undi' from the last lesson
> Tutor: Let's move on.

"Let's move on." That's the whole reply. No right/wrong, no answer, no next prompt. I
had no idea whether I'd passed. So I typed `ok`, and got:
> Naaku chala snehithulu undi. (I have many friends.) Eeroju me snehithulu evaru kalisi
> chudali? (Who are your friends today?)
> Not quite. Hint: My + many + friends. Tell Miko you have many friends.

So the answer *was* the thing I typed one turn earlier and got "let's move on" for. I
typed it back exactly:
> You: Naaku chala snehithulu undi
> Tutor: Correct. Eeroju meeru snehithulu to em chestunnaru? (What are you doing with
> your friends today?)
> Not quite. Hint: My + many + friends. Tell Miko you have many friends.

"Correct." and "Not quite." about the same sentence, in the same message, and I did not
advance. That exact `Not quite. Hint: My + many + friends.` line came back three times
in a row before the lesson finally gave up on me and handed over a *fourth* version of
the answer: `Naaku chala mandi snehithulu unnaru`. I was one turn away from my
stop-the-lesson rule.

Also: the hint said "My + many + friends", which maps to *Naa + chala + snehithulu*.
I tried exactly that (`Naa chala snehithulu`) and it was rejected. The hint and the
answer key disagree.

**Lesson 8, "I don't want these houses" — stuck 3 turns.**
Guessed `Idhi illu oddu` and said I'd never been taught "these". Got:
> Idhi illu oddu is close, but actually it's **Illa oddu**.

So I typed `Illa oddu`. Got:
> Chala bagundi! Ikkada, meeru cheppandi: 'Kurchilu ekkada?' (Where are the chairs?)
> Not quite. Hint: These + houses + don't want. Say 'I don't want these houses'.

Praised in Telugu and marked wrong in the same breath, for typing the exact answer it
had just given me. Then it gave up and said the answer was `Ee illu oddu` — a third
different version. Meanwhile the hint still says three parts and *Illa oddu* is two.

## 3. Asked for things I was never taught

Lesson 7, before lesson 8 existed, demanded plurals as its main exercise. Lesson 8 is
literally called plurals. The order is backwards.

| Prompt | Word I didn't have |
|---|---|
| L7: "Say 'One book'." | ***Oka***. I had *Okati*. Rejected without a word of warning. |
| L7: "Say 'Two friends'." | ***Snehithulu***. I had *Snehithudu*. This is a changed form — I had no way to derive it. Taught to me one lesson later. |
| L7: "Say 'Five houses'." | plural of *Illu* — and the tutor told me it was ***Illulu***, which the grader then rejected. See §5. |
| L7: "Miko asks how many books. Say 'Three books'." | ***Pusthakalu***. Never given. Hint said "Three + books" so I had no fallback. Failed out of it. |
| L8: "Tell Miko you have many friends." | ***mandi***, ***unnaru*** — both appeared only in the give-up answer. |
| L8: "Say 'I don't want these houses'." | "these" — ***Ee*** (and/or ***Illa***). Neither ever taught. |
| L8: "To say 'Chairs', say Kurchilu" | taught the plural without ever giving me the singular *chair*. |

**Prompts that hand me the answer in brackets** — this happened four times and each
time it felt like being told, not taught. I typed them correctly and learned nothing:
- L7: `Say 'I want two' (using Rendu kaavali).`
- L7: `Tell Miko 'I have one sister' (using Naaku oka akka undi).` — this one smuggles in
  *Naaku* and *undi*, two brand-new grammar words, inside a bracket.
- L8: `Say 'Many books' (Chala pusthakalu).` — introduces *Chala*.

If the answer is in the prompt, don't score me on it. Teach *Chala* as a word first.

**Untaught Telugu fired at me mid-conversation.** Four times, the tutor asked me things
in Telugu with no English I could act on:
- `Eeroju me snehithulu evaru kalisi chudali?`
- `Ippudu meeru snehithulu to em chestunnaru?`
- `Ippudu meeru kurchilu gurinchi cheppandi.`
- `Chala bagundi! Ikkada, meeru cheppandi: ...`

*Eeroju, ippudu, kalisi, chudali, chestunnaru, gurinchi, cheppandi, bagundi* — none of
these are mine. It's showing off in a language I'm six lessons into. I ignored all of it.

## 4. Marked wrong when I was right (or right when I was wrong)

- `Okati Pusthakam` for "One book" was marked wrong, then the tutor said "You're
  correct" about it, then still wouldn't let me past. Either it's right or it isn't.
- `Naaku chala snehithulu undi` got three different verdicts across three turns:
  "Let's move on", then "Not quite", then "Correct" + "Not quite" simultaneously. Same
  string every time.
- `Illa oddu` — the tutor's own answer — marked "Not quite".
- `Aidhu Illulu` marked wrong immediately after the tutor told me *Illulu* was the
  plural of *Illu*, and then, after I complained, it doubled down: "Illulu is indeed
  the plural for houses, so it should be Aidhu Illulu." The grader had already accepted
  `Aidhu Illu`. The tutor and the answer key are two different systems that don't talk.

**My typos, which is where the scoring is least consistent:**
- L7 vocab: hint `moo-du`, I typed `Mudu` → rejected. Fair-ish, but strict.
- L7 recall: I typed `Nalugu` for *Naalugu* → "Exactly!". Same class of error as *Mudu*
  (one doubled vowel), opposite verdict.
- L8 vocab: hint `snay-hih-tu-lu`, I typed `Snehitulu` (dropped an h) → accepted.

So a dropped vowel is fatal in the teach step and free in the recall step, and a
dropped consonant is free everywhere. It's not that it's lenient or strict; it's that I
can't predict it, so I never know whether I've actually got the spelling right.

One more: adding a plain-English aside to a correct answer breaks it. `Rendu kaavali
(though you just gave me the answer in the brackets there)` was rejected; bare
`Rendu kaavali` passed one turn later. A human tutor would not care.

## 5. Confusing or contradictory

**The *Illu* mess, in order.** This is the worst thing in these two lessons.
1. My notebook, lesson 3: *Illu = House*.
2. L7 asks "Five houses". I guess `Aidhu Illulu`. Tutor: "That's a good guess! The
   plural of 'Illu' is 'Illulu'."
3. Grader rejects `Aidhu Illulu`, accepts `Aidhu Illu`.
4. I point out the contradiction. Tutor: "Illulu is indeed the plural for houses, so it
   should be Aidhu Illulu."
5. L8 opens with "Plural **Illu** — 'houses'", i.e. *Illu* is now the plural.
6. I ask if it's both. Tutor: "Illu can refer to both 'house' and 'houses' depending on
   context. So, you can use it for either!"
7. L8's grammar note finally says the real thing: "*illu* looks the same singular or
   plural **in this spelling**, though Telugu writes the two differently."

Point 7 is the correct and genuinely useful explanation. Points 2 and 4 are the tutor
inventing a word — *Illulu* — because I guessed it, and then defending the invention.
The tutor agreeing with whatever I guess is worse than the tutor being strict. If it
had said "no, that's not a word" in step 2 I'd have been fine. Instead I spent two
lessons believing something false, and I still half-believe it.

**Other things that didn't land:**
- "It's important for proper nouns" about the word *book*. Just wrong, and it sent me
  chasing capitalisation for two turns.
- The bracketed hints and the answer key contradict each other. "My + many + friends"
  ≠ *Naaku chala mandi snehithulu unnaru*. "These + houses + don't want" (three parts)
  ≠ *Illa oddu* (two).
- Every single tutor reply in the sentence-building phase is **two messages: a chatty
  one that praises me and jumps to the *next* exercise, then a second one that drags me
  back to the current one.** Example: "Now, let's keep going! What about 'Two books'?"
  / "So — Say 'One book'." It reads like two teachers talking over each other and it
  made me genuinely unsure what I was being asked at any moment.
- Lesson 7 had no grammar explanation at all, despite being the lesson that needed one.
  Lesson 8 had exactly one, and it was good.
- Pronunciation hints: *Aidhu* → `eye-du` and *Moodu* → `moo-du` both drop the *h*
  that's in the spelling, and *Snehithulu* → `snay-hih-tu-lu` keeps one *h* and drops
  the other. So I never know if an *h* I hear is in the spelling. That's exactly what
  caused my typos.

## 6. How it felt

The first third of each lesson is genuinely good. Show a word, pronunciation hint, I
type it back, quick recall quiz. Tight, fast, I felt like I was getting somewhere, and
the recall quiz right after is well placed. If the whole app were that I'd use it daily.

Then the sentence phase turns on and it becomes a guessing game. The pattern I hit over
and over: it asks for a sentence, the sentence needs a word I've never seen, I say so
honestly, it praises my honesty, gives me an answer, and then marks that same answer
wrong. I got stuck four separate times across two lessons and escaped exactly one of
them by my own understanding — the other three I either fluked (`Oka Pusthakam`) or was
handed the answer after failing out. Being stuck for five turns on "you have many
friends", watching the same "Not quite. Hint: My + many + friends" come back three
times while the tutor said "Correct" in the line above it, was the point I would have
closed the app in real life.

The praise does not feel earned, and worse, it doesn't feel *informative*. "Perfect!"
"Spot on!" "Great job!" "Chala bagundi!" get spent on typing back a word I was shown
one second earlier, and *also* on answers that are then rejected. So praise stopped
carrying information for me. When it said "That's a good guess!" about *Illulu* I
believed it, and that's precisely how I got misled.

Tone-wise, Miko is fine — friendly, not cloying, the paw emoji is harmless. The problem
isn't the personality, it's that the personality and the grader clearly aren't the same
system, and I can feel the seam.

Would I keep going? For the vocabulary drills, yes. But I'd stop reading the
explanations, because I no longer trust them, and I'd treat every "correct" as
provisional. That's a bad place for a beginner to be at lesson 8. The two things I'd fix
first: **teach plurals (lesson 8) before the lesson that drills plurals (lesson 7)**,
and **make the tutor's stated answer and the accepted answer the same string** — every
one of my four stuck points is that one bug.
