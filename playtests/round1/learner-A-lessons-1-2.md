# Learner A — Telugu, lessons 1 & 2

I'd never seen a word of Telugu before this. Twenty-odd minutes, two lessons, both finished
(15/15 each — 16 turns for lesson 1, 18 for lesson 2). Nothing hard-locked me, which is
genuinely good. But there were a handful of moments where it clearly wanted a word it had
never given me, and one moment where it answered my question in Telugu, which was surreal.

---

## 1. Did I actually learn anything?

Yes, actually. Ten words in and they stuck, mostly because the pattern was so tight.

**I could use these cold, right now, without being prompted:**

- `Namaskaram` — hello
- `Nenu` — I
- `Meeru` — you
- `Bagunnanu` — I'm fine
- `Ela` — how
- `Idhi` — this
- `Adhi` — that
- `Emiti` — what
- `Pusthakam` — book
- `Peru` — name

And I can build these sentences without stopping to think, which felt like a real win:

- `Namaskaram, nenu bagunnanu. Meeru ela unnaru?`
- `Idhi emiti?` / `Adhi emiti?`
- `Idhi pusthakam.`

**What went in one ear and out the other:**

- `unnaru` / `unnanu` / `unnavu` — I *use* `unnaru` correctly now, but only as a noise I
  attach to the end of `Meeru ela ___`. The explanation that `unnanu` = I am, `unnaru` = you
  are AND respectful he/she/they, `unnavu` = close friend, all arrived in one paragraph, and
  I retained about a third of it. If you asked me tomorrow which one goes with a friend I'd
  guess.
- `mariyu` ("and") — I was told it, used it once, and then immediately told it wasn't
  actually needed. So it's gone.
- `mee` (the "proper" possessive) — mentioned once as a thing I'd meet later. Didn't learn it,
  and now I'm slightly worried the thing I *did* learn is wrong (see §5).
- The `Idhi` / `Adhi` split. One vowel apart, and the pronunciation hints are `ih-dhi` and
  `uh-dhi`, which to my English ear are almost the same sound. I got them right in the drills
  because the prompt said "distant object", not because I'd feel the difference in the wild.
- Anything about how the words are actually written. I never once saw Telugu script. I've
  learned ten words in a spelling system that doesn't exist.

---

## 2. Where I got stuck

I never got permanently stuck — no repeated-message loop, no dead end. Both lessons finished.
The two places I stalled:

**Lesson 1, step "Ask 'How are you?'"** — I could not do it with what I'd been given. I had
`Ela` and `Meeru` and `Bagunnanu`, and no second-person "are". I typed:

> `Meeru ela unnanu? -- honestly guessing, you only gave me 'unnanu' for 'I am', I don't know the 'you are' form`

It handled it well:

> Tutor: No problem! "You are" is "meeru unnaru." Now try saying, "I am fine, how are you?"
> Tutor: So — Ask 'How are you?'.

Two things there. It gave me the missing word, which is exactly right. But it also told me to
try *"I am fine, how are you?"* and then in the very next line told me to do the original
*"How are you?"* — two different tasks in one turn, and I had to guess which one counted. I
went with the second one and that was the right call, but that's me pattern-matching on the
"So —" prefix, not understanding.

**Lesson 1, step "Say 'I am fine, and you?'"** — same problem, no word for "and". I typed:

> `Nenu bagunnanu, meeru? (I don't have a word for 'and' yet)`

and got back, in full:

> Tutor: Naku "and" cheppalante "mariyu" ani cheppali. Meeru bagunnaru?

That's the whole reply. I have five words of Telugu and it answered me in a full Telugu
sentence. I could reverse-engineer that `mariyu` was the word I wanted because it was in
quotes, and that's the only reason I got out of it. I said so:

> `Sorry, I can't read that -- you just answered me entirely in Telugu and I only know five words. Can you say it in English?`

and it replied:

> Tutor: I understand! Just say "I am fine, and you?" in Telugu. Let's keep practicing! 🐾

which is friendly but is not an answer to what I asked. It didn't translate anything. If
`mariyu` hadn't been sitting in quotation marks I'd have been finished there.

---

## 3. Asked for things I was never taught

Three clear ones.

1. **Lesson 1 — "Ask 'How are you?'"** Missing: `unnaru`. Taught vocab at that point was
   Namaskaram / Nenu / Meeru / Bagunnanu / Ela, plus `unnanu` mentioned inside a grammar note
   about `bagunnanu`. There is no way to make "how are you" out of those.

2. **Lesson 1 — "Say 'I am fine, and you?'"** Missing: a word for "and". Never given.
   Worse, when it did give me `mariyu`, the grammar note on my *accepted* answer then said
   the natural sentence has no "and" in it at all — so it asked me for a word it didn't want.

3. **Lesson 2 — "Combine 'You' (from last lesson) and 'Name What?'"** This one's borderline.
   The words were all mine (`Meeru`, `Peru`, `Emiti`), but the prompt reads like an internal
   template rather than a sentence — "Combine 'You' and 'Name What?'" is not how a teacher
   talks. And it leans on "from last lesson" when lesson 2 started as a completely clean slate
   that had never re-shown me a single lesson-1 word. If I'd taken a week off between the two
   I'd have had nothing.

Also worth flagging: words got dropped into the chatter that I hadn't met — `anukuntunnanu`,
`evaru`, `unnavu`, `bagunnaru`, `mee`. The first two came with a translation in brackets, which
was kind. The rest didn't.

And the end-of-lesson summary card only lists the five headline words:

> 📋 **You can now say:** Namaskaram — Hello · Nenu — I · Meeru — You · Bagunnanu — I'm fine · Ela — How

`unnaru` isn't on it. It's the word I used in four separate answers. It's arguably the most
useful thing in the lesson and the recap acts like it never happened.

---

## 4. Marked wrong when I was right, or right when I was wrong

**Typos: forgiven, both times, and I think correctly.**

- Lesson 1 I typed `Bagunanu` for **Bagunnanu** — dropped one of the doubled n's, which is
  exactly what happens when you're typing off `baa-gun-naa-nu`. Accepted: "Good."
- Lesson 2 I typed `Emithi` for **Emiti** — added an h, off the `ay-mih-ti` hint. Accepted:
  "Yes."

I'm glad about both. Neither is a knowledge failure and stopping me would have felt petty.
Though: it accepted them *silently*. It never showed me the correct spelling back. So as far
as I knew, `Bagunanu` was right, and I only spelled it properly later because the word was
still on screen from the teaching step.

**A real error, correctly caught.** I deliberately answered "I am fine" as `Nenu bagunnaru`
— wrong person ending, the mistake a beginner genuinely makes right after being told endings
carry the person. It caught it:

> Tutor: Nenu bagunnanu anukuntunnanu. (I think I'm fine.) Miko, meeru ela unnaru?
> Tutor: Not quite. Hint: Direct answer. Someone asks how you are. Answer: 'I am fine'.

Good that it said no. But the correction is odd — Miko answers *as if she were me* ("I think
I'm fine"), and the reply contains the exact right answer `bagunnanu` inside it. So the
"Not quite, try again" that follows is free: the answer is sitting two lines above.

**It gives the answer away.** In lesson 2 I just typed `ok` to see what happened:

> Tutor: Idhi pusthakam. 🐾 I like reading books. Meeru evaru? (Who are you?)
> Tutor: Not quite. Hint: Simple statement. Tell Miko 'This is a book'.

The target answer was `Idhi pusthakam` and that is literally the first thing it said. I copied
it straight off the screen and got "Perfect!". That's not a scoring error exactly, but the drill
stopped being a drill.

**Right when I was wrong, arguably.** `Meeru peru emiti?` was marked correct, and then the note
said:

> In casual spoken Telugu, stringing together *You + Name + What* works. Later you will meet the
> strict possessive *Mee*.

So it's telling me the thing it just praised is the loose version and there's a stricter one it
isn't going to teach me. It then made me produce the same loose form again as the final step of
the lesson. If `Mee peru emiti?` is the real sentence, I've now drilled the wrong one twice.

---

## 5. Confusing or contradictory

**The `mariyu` contradiction.** Sequence, verbatim:

1. Me: `Nenu bagunnanu, meeru? (I don't have a word for 'and' yet)` → rejected.
2. Tutor: `...'and' ... 'mariyu' ...`
3. Me: `Nenu bagunnanu, mariyu meeru?` → accepted, with this note:
   > Telugu usually drops *nenu* — *bagunnanu* already means "I am fine" on its own. This
   > sentence is the exception... so *Nenu bagunnanu, meeru?* is the natural form here.

The "natural form" it printed is the sentence it rejected in step 1. I got marked wrong for
the right answer, told to add a word, and then told the word shouldn't be there. That one
genuinely made me feel stupid rather than taught.

**Pronunciation hints don't match the spelling I have to type.** This is the source of both my
typos, so it's not a nitpick:

- `Namaskaram` → `nuh-mus-kaa-rum`. Three a's in the spelling, and the hint gives me uh, u, aa, u.
- `Pusthakam` → `pus-tuh-kum`. There's a silent-ish `th` in the spelling that the hint spells `t`,
  and the final `-kam` is hinted `-kum`.
- `Emiti` → `ay-mih-ti`. The `E` is hinted `ay`, which is why I reached for `Emithi`.
- `Adhi` → `uh-dhi` vs `Idhi` → `ih-dhi`. Nearly indistinguishable in English respelling for two
  words whose whole job is to be distinguished.

I'm typing, so I'm working from the bold word, but the hint is what I'm *hearing in my head*, and
the two disagree on almost every word. Pick one romanisation.

**The `unnaru` explanation is too much at once.** One paragraph gave me: `unnanu` = I am,
`unnaru` = you are, `unnaru` also = respectful he/she/they, `unnavu` = friend version, and "the
ending carries the person". Four forms, three of which I will never be tested on in this lesson.
I nodded and moved on.

**Miko replying in Telugu.** Covered above, but it's the biggest one. Twice — once for `mariyu`,
once in the `anukuntunnanu` correction — the tutor's conversational voice was Telugu. A beginner
cannot read the tutor's help text. That's not a difficulty setting, it's a wall.

**"from last lesson" across a hard reset.** Lesson 2 opens cold, teaches five new words, then
asks me to combine in `Meeru` and `Namaskaram` from lesson 1 without ever having refreshed them.
Fine for me twenty minutes later; not fine on a Tuesday.

---

## 6. How it felt

Honestly? Better than I expected, and I'd do lesson 3.

The **pace is very good**. Five words, a quick three-question recall, then sentences, then a
little roleplay. It never dragged, I never had to sit through a wall of theory before being
allowed to type something. Fifteen steps is about right — I finished both lessons wanting one
more rather than wanting out.

The **grammar notes are the best thing in here**. "`Bagunnanu` is `baga` + `unnanu`, the vowel
drops where they meet, that's why there's no separate word for 'am'" — that's a real
explanation, it landed immediately, and it made the word memorable instead of arbitrary. Same
with "the pointing word comes first, the question word goes last, where English puts it first."
Those two notes are why I can still build sentences.

The **praise did not feel earned**. Every single correct answer got a different superlative —
Correct, Right, Yes, Good, Spot on, Exactly, Perfect, Great job — cycling in what I'm fairly
sure is a fixed order, because the same sequence showed up in both lessons. So "Perfect!" for
typing back a word that was on screen one line earlier reads as noise, and by the time I did
something that was actually hard (the full three-part greeting) the word "Perfect" had already
been spent five times. I'd rather it said nothing for the easy ones and something specific for
the hard ones.

What made me feel stupid, in order: being answered in Telugu, and the `mariyu` reversal. What
made me feel taught: the `baga + unnanu` breakdown, and the fact that it forgave my two typos
without fuss. What made me distrust it slightly: being told `Meeru peru emiti?` is correct-but-
not-really, and the recap card forgetting `unnaru`.

Net: the teaching engine is good. The tutor's improvised chat is the weak part — it leaks
answers, it drops untaught words, and sometimes it forgets which language I speak.
