# Learner B — Telugu, lessons 3 & 4

I'm an English speaker, no Telugu at all. I came into lesson 3 with ten words in my notebook
from lessons 1–2 (Namaskaram, Nenu, Meeru, Bagunnanu, Ela, Idhi, Adhi, Emiti, Pusthakam, Peru)
and nothing else. Here's how the two lessons went.

---

## 1. Did I actually learn anything?

Yes, genuinely — more from lesson 4 than lesson 3.

**Things I could now say cold, without being prompted:**

- `Ekkada` (where), `Ikkada` (here), `Akkada` (there). These three stuck hard, and I think it's
  because they rhyme and I got to feel the pattern — ihk / uhk / ehk. That was the nicest bit of
  the whole session and nobody had to explain it to me.
- `Illu` (house).
- `Illu ekkada?` — "where is the house?" I could walk out and use this. The one grammar note the
  app gave me is the reason: *"Ekkada went to the end. Every Telugu question word does, where
  English puts it at the front."* That single line did more for me than anything else in two
  lessons. I immediately understood why `Emiti` from lesson 2 works the way it does too.
- The whole of lesson 4: `Neeru kaavali`, `Annam oddu`, `Pusthakam kaavali`, `Adhi oddu`,
  `Sare`. This is real, usable stuff — I could order water and refuse food. The want/don't-want
  pair is a great thing to teach early and I'd trust myself with it.

**What did not stick, at all:**

- **How to say "go".** This is the headline problem. Lesson 3 is literally titled "The 'Where' &
  'Going'" and I finished it without knowing how to tell anyone to go anywhere. My notebook says
  `Vellu = Go`. But every time the lesson actually asked me to use it, `Vellu` was wrong, and it
  wanted a different word I'd never seen — first `vellandi`, then `velthanu`. I now have three
  shapes of "go" in my head, no idea which is which, and my notebook still only lists `Vellu`.
  Net effect: I trust `Vellu` less than I did before the lesson taught it to me.
- `intiki`. Sprung on me in the final reveal (see below). No idea what it is. It looks nothing
  like `Illu`, which is the word I thought I had for house.
- Anything about "is". I asked directly and got told it isn't needed — fine, and useful — but I
  never learned when it *is* needed, so I'm just hoping it never is.

---

## 2. Where I got stuck

Two places, both in lesson 3, both the same underlying thing.

### Stuck point A — "Go there" (3 failed turns)

I typed:
> **Akkada vellu**

Tutor:
> *"Not quite. Hint: There + go. Say 'Go there'."*

That's the bit that broke my brain. The hint says **There + go**. I wrote there, then go. Using
the only two words I'd been given for those meanings. I assumed I'd fat-fingered something, so I
tried again with the capitalisation exactly as taught:

> **Akkada Vellu**
>
> *"Not quite. Hint: There + go. Say 'Go there'."*

Identical message. So I asked in plain English:

> **That's what I put — Akkada vellu is there + go. What am I missing?**
>
> *"I see where the confusion is. The correct phrase should be in a different order. Let's focus on that. Can you try again? 🐾"*

This is straight-up **contradicting its own hint one line earlier**, which had told me the order.
So I flipped it, which I knew was wrong given what lesson 3 had just taught me about word order:

> **Vellu akkada**
>
> *"It's **Akkada vellandi**. We'll come back to this — Ask Miko where the home is."*

So the order was right all along. It was never the order. It was the word — a form of `vellu` I
had never seen. The hint was right, the tutor's diagnosis was wrong, and I burned four turns and
all my confidence finding that out. "We'll come back to this" — it never did.

### Stuck point B — "I go home" (3 turns, and it never accepted anything)

> Tutor: *"Say 'I go home'. Start with 'I' (Nenu)."*

I flagged it honestly:
> **Guessing with what I've got: Nenu illu vellu. I don't know an 'I go' form of vellu though — you only gave me Vellu and Vellandi.**

And got back, in one reply:
> *"Nenu illu vellu. That's how you say it! Now, can you ask someone where their house is?"*
> *"Not quite. Hint: I + home + go. Say 'I go home'. Start with 'I' (Nenu)."*

It told me my answer was correct and marked it wrong **in the same breath**. So I typed the exact
string it had just endorsed:
> **Nenu illu vellu**
>
> *"That's right! Now, can you ask someone where their house is?"*
> *"Not quite. Hint: I + home + go. Say 'I go home'. Start with 'I' (Nenu)."*

"That's right!" and "Not quite." Same turn. Again. And it's also asking me a *new* question
("can you ask someone where their house is?") while simultaneously re-asking the old one, so
there are two live questions on screen and answering either one doesn't count.

I gave up and threw the other "go" at it:
> **Nenu illu vellandi**
>
> *"It's **Nenu intiki velthanu**. That's the scenario done — nicely handled."*

"Nicely handled" is a strange thing to say to someone who just failed three times and got the
answer handed to them.

---

## 3. Asked for things I was never taught

Three, all in lesson 3, all altered forms of a word I *did* have:

1. **`vellandi`** — prompt: *"Say 'Go there'."* Hint: "There + go". I had `Vellu` (glossed
   plainly as "Go"). `vellandi` had never appeared anywhere. Nothing in the lesson had told me
   that "go" changes shape when you're telling someone else to do it — no note, no mention that
   `Vellu` is a different register or person from `vellandi`.
2. **`velthanu`** — prompt: *"Say 'I go home'."* Hint: "I + home + go". Same word again, third
   shape, never shown before the reveal. Note this one is *especially* unfair, because the lesson
   had just spent two turns telling me `Akkada vellandi` was the way to say go — so the obvious
   inference from a beginner's chair is that `vellandi` is "go" and I should reuse it. I did, and
   it was wrong again.
3. **`intiki`** — same prompt. The hint said "home", and my notebook says `Illu = House`. There
   is nothing on screen at any point connecting `Illu` to `intiki`. As a beginner I can't even
   tell those are the same word.

So the two hardest drills in the lesson both required **two** never-taught words at once, and
the hints described them using words I *did* have, which actively pointed me at the wrong answer.

Small related thing: `Nenu ikkada` was accepted for "I am here", and `Nenu illu vellu` was
rejected for "I go home". Both are noun-plus-word-I-was-given with no verb ending. I have no way
to know why one is fine and one isn't.

---

## 4. Marked wrong when I was right (or right when I was wrong)

**Marked wrong when I was right:**

- `Akkada vellu` for "Go there". Correct word order, correct two words, only words available to
  me. Rejected, and rejected with a hint that endorsed exactly what I'd written.
- `Nenu illu vellu` for "I go home". Rejected — while the very same reply said *"That's right!"*
  and *"That's how you say it!"* Whatever the grader is doing, it disagrees with whatever is
  writing the praise line.
- My first stab at "Where is the house?" — I wrote *"You haven't taught me how to say 'is' yet.
  Guessing from what I have: Illu ekkada?"*. The tutor's reply confirmed `Illu ekkada?` is exactly
  right, then re-asked the question anyway (*"So — Ask 'Where is the house?'."*). I had to retype
  the answer it had just told me was correct. Not a big deal, but it means asking a question costs
  you your turn.

**Marked right when I was wrong** — this is the other half of the problem, and honestly it worries
me more:

- Lesson 3, I typed **`Ikada`** where the word is `Ikkada`. Reply: *"Yes."* Straight through.
- Lesson 4, I typed **`Unnam`** where the word is `Annam` (I was working from the hint "un-num",
  which genuinely does read that way). Reply: *"Yes."* Straight through, no correction.
- Lesson 4, final turn, I typed **`Adi oddu`** where "that" is `Adhi`. Not only accepted — it
  ended the lesson on it.

Nobody ever showed me the right spelling again. If I'd been paying less attention I'd have walked
out of there saying "Unnam" and "Adi" forever. And it's a jarring contrast: the app will accept a
misspelled word without comment, but rejects a correctly-spelled correct-order two-word phrase.

Also on the pronunciation hints — `Annam` is written with two a's and hinted "un-num". `Adhi` has
a `dh` I can't hear in "that". Those are the exact two words I misspelled, and I don't think that's
coincidence.

---

## 5. Confusing or contradictory

- **The "wrong order" claim.** *"The correct phrase should be in a different order"* — it wasn't.
  The answer `Akkada vellandi` is the same order I'd written. That's a false explanation, and it
  sent me off to type something I knew was wrong.
- **"That's right!" followed immediately by "Not quite."** Twice, on the same drill, in the same
  reply. When the tutor and the marker disagree in one message there is nothing a learner can do
  with it.
- **Answering-my-own-question replies.** Both times I said something conversational (my honest
  "I don't know that form", and once just `ok`), the tutor typed the correct answer *for* me, then
  asked me a brand-new unrelated question, then re-asked the original. Three things in one reply.
  The `ok` case in lesson 4: *"Annam oddu. What do you want now?"* / *"Not quite. Hint: Food + don't
  want..."*. It handed me the answer as if I'd got it, and then failed me.
- **`Vellu` means three things.** By the end I had `Vellu`, `vellandi`, `velthanu` all glossed as
  "go" and no explanation of the difference. My notebook records only `Vellu`, which is the one
  form the lesson never accepted. That's an actively misleading set of notes.
- **`Illu` vs `intiki`.** Same problem, no bridge.
- **"We'll come back to this."** It didn't. Once a word was revealed to me it was never drilled,
  so I never got the repetition that would have made it stick. `vellandi` I only used once,
  immediately after being told it.
- **Lesson 4 just... stops.** My last answer got no response at all — no "correct", no wrap-up,
  no goodbye from Miko, straight to the summary banner. After 14 turns of feedback the silence on
  the final one felt like the app crashed.
- Minor: lesson 3's summary card says *"You can now say: ... Vellu — Go"*. I demonstrably cannot.

---

## 6. How it felt

**Lesson 4 was lovely.** Five words, sensible pairs (want/don't want, food/water), then
straight into little scenes — "Miko offers you water", "point far away". I got eight in a row
right, it reached back and reused `Idhi`, `Adhi` and `Pusthakam` from lesson 2 which felt
genuinely good, and I finished it feeling like I'd gained something I'd actually use in a
restaurant. If every lesson were lesson 4 I'd keep going happily.

**Lesson 3 was demoralising**, and it's a shame because the first two thirds were the best
teaching in the whole session. The ekkada/ikkada/akkada trio is a great piece of design. Then the
sentence-building half stopped being a lesson and became a guessing game where the hints were
pointing the wrong way and the tutor was arguing with itself about whether I was right.

The praise doesn't feel earned, in both directions. "Nicely handled" after three failures and a
giveaway is hollow — I know I didn't handle it. And "Yes" to my typos is worse, because it means
"Yes" carries no information; I stopped believing the positive feedback by the end. The one time
the app *did* teach me something in a way I felt in my chest was the `Ekkada`-goes-to-the-end
note, which is one sentence and cost nothing. More of those, please.

Miko's tone is fine — warm, not cloying, the paw print is cute and doesn't get in the way. No
complaints there. The pace is right, 15 steps is about my limit.

Would I keep going? Yes, but with a specific worry rather than general enthusiasm: I don't trust
that the drills will only ask me for words I've been given, and I don't trust that "correct"
means correct. That makes me hesitant rather than eager. Fix the verb-forms thing and make the
marker agree with the praise, and I'd be all in.

**If you fix one thing:** never ask me for a form of a word you haven't shown me. And if a hint
says "There + go", let "there go" be right.
