### 2. Lesson 13 and the past tense — does it tell you how the past is formed, and honestly whether you could do it to a NEW verb?

**Yes, and yes.** Best thing in the three lessons. Step 1, unprompted:

> "Look at the shape: a stem, then **-anu** for the past, then nothing else — the -nu on the end is the "I", the same -nu you met in *chestunnanu*. So the past is stem + -anu"

Step 2, still unprompted, the honest half:

> "One warning worth having early: the STEM changes unpredictably. *Vellu* ("go") gives *vellanu*, but "eat" gives *thinnanu* and "see" gives *chusanu*, and there is no rule that gets you from one to the other. Learn each verb's stem as its own word; the ENDING is what transfers."

**A verb it never conjugated.** I tried twice.
- `Vellu` → I guessed `Vellanu` in step 1 and said I was guessing. It confirmed it and immediately warned me not to generalise. Ideal.
- `Kurchondi` ("sit please") → *"how would I say 'I sat'? … is the past stem something I can work out?"* This got **no answer at all** — just the opening `Chesanu` note replayed. So the lesson tells you the honest rule but won't apply it to a verb you bring it. Which is a shame, because the honest answer ("no, and here's the stem") was two sentences away.

It also volunteered `chesaru` = "you did" and `chesadu` = "he did" mid-lesson, which was a nice bonus.

### 5. Lesson 9's `undi` — is it coherent now?

**Yes, the content is coherent. Getting it out of the tutor took three tries.**

The teaching line: *"**Bagundi** is "it is good" — *baga* ("well") joined onto *undi* ("it is"), the same joining you met in *bagunnanu*."*

I asked directly, twice, bundled onto correct answers, and got nothing both times. Third time I asked it **on its own**, and got the answer that resolves it completely:

> "Nothing between the two words — *adhi chedu*, and likewise *idhi manchi* for "this is good". Telugu has two different jobs here and keeps them apart: *undi* says a thing EXISTS or is somewhere (*illu akkada undi*), and *bagundi* has it built in for exactly that reason. But pinning a plain description onto a noun needs no verb at all, which is why *adhi chedu* is already a complete sentence."

That is coherent and it's a good explanation. The old contradiction ("undi is 'it is'" then "there's no word for is") is gone — it now distinguishes *existence/location* (`undi`) from *plain description* (no verb). Lesson 20 reinforced it consistently: *"Bagundi is the whole comment — baga joined to undi — so it needs no separate word for 'was'."*

One nitpick: the answer handed me `adhi chedu` in full immediately before asking me to produce `adhi chedu`. Free answer.
